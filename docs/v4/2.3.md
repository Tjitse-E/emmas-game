# Moon Meadow World 2.3.0: proposal and build design

Made on 28 October 2026 from Emma's feedback on 2.2.0 and Tjitse's notes. Four code readers, three designers (learning first, Emma first, build first) and two critics (learning, feasibility) produced it. Tjitse approved the direction (section 11). Part 2, after section 12, is the build design (layout, contracts, lanes, tests), as in [2.2.md](2.2.md).

## 0. Calls Claude made (check these first)

- **Colours are free from the first play.** Only items (shapes) are earned. Green hair with a green gown is her own example, and holding it back would refuse it. Tjitse's limit applies to dressing time and to new items.
- **Dressing time counts as free-play time.** The 60 s at the Dressing Corner comes out of free play's 120 s of unchecked play, so a play has no more unchecked time than 2.2.
- **Patterns come before doubles.** The bead tree teaches patterns. Doubles ("the same on each earring") carry the unclear-goal risk that postponed the twin egg in 2.2. They wait for a mockup Emma tries first.
- **The fence stays where it is in 2.3.** New places fill the empty parts of today's meadow. The meadow grows with riding in 2.4 (decision 2).
- **No new gown shapes.** The four gown cells (party dress, ball gown, ball gown with gloves, tutu) take any colour, and the wings and the back bow become extras she can wear with any gown. 2.2's fairy dress was the party dress with wings, so it is now that pair (Part 2 settles this, section 15).

## 1. Summary

- **One palette for everything.** Colour and shape are split. Hair, gown, crown, shoes, hair things and jewellery each take any of about 12 colours plus rainbow. Green eyes, a green gown and green hair work on her first 2.3 play.
- **More to wear.** Hair styles go from 4 to 12. New rows for the crown (5 shapes), shoes (4 shapes, with toe tips under the ball gowns), earrings and a necklace.
- **The Dressing Corner.** Lumi's gazebo trunk becomes a place where she changes during the game. Her two babies dress up first (two reading moments). Then a music box plays for 60 s while she dresses. It ends kindly and her look is kept.
- **A collection she earns.** Every side quest gives a gift: she picks one of two new things, and it goes into the trunk. The inside of the trunk lid shows every set, and an empty place shows the picture of the quest that fills it.
- **New learning.** Reading: colour words in short phrases with "the" ("the green cape"), where she must read both words. Maths: patterns on bead strings at a new bead tree.
- **More sound.** About 16 ElevenLabs sound effects and a day and a night background sound, plus three synthesised effects the game has but never plays.

## 3. The Dressing Corner

**Where.** At the south step of Lumi's gazebo (-14, 2.4). The lilac trunk is already in the gazebo ("a little dressing corner for later", `places.ts:89`). It moves to the step, next to a new standing mirror (about 300 triangles). Her followers and the herd near the gazebo step aside, as at the picnic.

**When.** A trunk bubble appears in free play once she has finished her first quest of the afternoon, because her gift waits in the trunk. She taps the bubble or the gazebo, or walks near the step. One visit per play.

**The challenge.** Lumi: "Your babies want to dress up first!" Each baby gets a phrase wish, such as "the green cape" printed in its bubble, with a basket of 4 or 5 tinted pictures (section 6). A right pick: the baby wears it on its head. A wrong pick: the 2.2 light contrast (the wrong picture shows its own words, and the letters that differ glow). Two reads, about one minute. They are checked moments and feed the reading level.

**The time limit.** The trunk opens and the gifts pop out. A music box on the panel plays a tune made in code, and six moon beads around it fade, one every 10 s. There is no clock and no number. With 15 s left, Elizabeth says "Nearly ready!" Done ends the visit sooner.

**The kind ending.** When the tune ends, the game waits until her finger is up (read from `window`, at most 2 s). Then Princess Emma curtsies, the chime plays and Elizabeth says "Lovely!" Her look is saved as it is. Nothing is taken off and nothing says no.

**The start mirror.** The customiser after Play stays, with everything she owns and the same music box, but 45 s and no challenge (decision 1). On a first ever play the crown row is not there yet. It appears after Lumi gives the crown.

## 4. The wardrobe

**Palette.** Twelve colours (eleven and rainbow), green among them, and for the hair six natural shades as well (Part 2, section 15 has the ids and hex values). The crown keeps white gems as its outline, and a test checks every hair colour against every crown colour.

**Hair styles (12).** Today's 4, plus 6 that are only data (low pigtails, side ponytail, curly pigtails, long straight tails, short bunches, a high ponytail with curls) and 2 with small code changes (one plait, a low bun). Loose waves and two buns stay out: they clip through the wings and cost frame time. Styles must differ in outline, because ponytail variants look alike from the follow camera.

**Crowns, shoes, jewellery.** Crowns are cached shapes with a colour (Lumi's crown, a tiara, a star, a heart and a flower crown). Shoes: slippers, bow shoes, boots and star shoes, with toe tips under the ball gowns so they show with every gown. Earrings at the ears and a necklace of beads. Hair things keep today's six and get four more (shells, moons, daisies, cherries).

**The panel.** Six fixed tabs in the icon column: hair, eyes, gown, crown, shoes, sparkles. The earrings and the necklace sit on the eyes tab, which frames her face (Part 2, section 16). A tab acts on pointerdown and changes what the rails hold. Each tab has at most five rails of six cells, so nothing pages and nothing moves under a finger. Only items she owns show. When she picks a colour, Elizabeth says its name ("Green."), so the colour words are known before she reads them.

**Budget.** Her busiest look today is 7,846 triangles against a test cap of 8,000. The cap goes to 10,000. New parts add no draw calls. The portrait is measured again at the gazebo step.

## 5. Earning and the collection

Each quest gives one gift per play. She picks one of two items from that quest's set. The item shows on her for one twirl, then flies into the trunk with the `fanfare` sound. When a set is full, its quest puts a sticker on the trunk lid.

| Set | Earned at | Learning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 crowns | Royal picnic (2.2) | Two near-word reads |
| 3 shoes | Hay rack (2.2) | Adding across ten |
| 4 earrings, 2 necklaces | Bead tree (new) | Patterns |
| 4 hair styles, 4 hair things | Dressing Corner gate | Two phrase reads |

Ribbons stay Lumi's goodnight gift. There are 21 items in all, about 12 to 15 plays of collecting.

Each afternoon offers the picnic and one maths quest (the rack and the bead tree in turn, and the one she missed last time comes first). The trunk bubble follows her first finished quest. The three-minute cutoff for new quests is widened to fit this.

## 6. New learning

**Colour phrases (reading, GOAL "short phrases", v3 step R27).**

- The unit is a phrase: "the", a colour and a thing. Only colours that a tint can show are used. Green, gold, blue and brown are targets. Red and pink are decoys only, because no other colour shares their first letter. Black, grey and white wait for coloured art.
- A basket is a two by two grid plus one: two colours with the same first letter times two things with the same first letter. For "the blue bow": blue bow, brown bow, blue bead, brown bead, and from level 2 one more near item. A child who reads only first letters picks right 1 time in 4.
- Things come from v3's greyscale pictures that have an ok clip: bag, bead and bow, sock and shirt (Part 2, section 18). Cape and coat look alike, so they never share a basket.
- She reads past the first letter in both words: gr against go, bl against br, sock against shirt, and bow against bead.
- Elizabeth reads the phrase after she acts. "The" is printed in every phrase. It never decides an answer, so it only gives her practice in seeing a tricky word.
- Emma checks every tinted picture first, as with the 2.2 words. Gold may look yellow to her.

**Patterns (maths, GOAL "patterns", M08).**

- At the bead tree a baby's necklace has three gaps. The beads differ in shape and colour (heart, star, moon, round), so colour alone never fills a gap.
- Strings go from ABB and ABC to AABB, with gaps in the middle at the higher level. Each pattern is chosen so that copying the last bead, or swapping between two beads, is wrong at least once.
- Each gap gives its answer at once. Odds per gap are 1 in 3 or 4, and the hint ladder works as with the eggs.
- Patterns may be easy for her, because they come before the across-ten sums she does now. The log shows it within a few plays, and then doubles move up.

**What Tjitse can see.** Each play sends one `kind=play` line with the checked time, the dressing time and the first-try rate of the gate reads. After three plays this shows whether dressing is pushing out learning.

## 8. Sound

**Free, in 2.3.0 for sure.** The game already has synthesised `hatch`, `munch` and `fanfare`, but never plays them. They go on the egg, the food and every new item. The music box is made in code.

**ElevenLabs sound effects.**

- About 16 effects of about 1.5 s: egg crack, a foal's happy nicker (two), stable door, trunk lid, gift paper, dress swish, bead clink, crown twinkle, hay rustle, a big splash (two), slide whoosh, bedtime bell, an owl at night.
- Two background sounds of 15 s, mono: a day meadow (birds and a breeze) and a night (crickets).
- No footsteps or hooves: with up to 20 unicorns they would sound all the time.
- Tick, pop and chime stay synthesised. They cost nothing and start without delay.
- First a probe: one 2 s effect, to measure what it really takes from the account. The API has no seed, so every approved file is kept. Tjitse listens to every sound, because speech-to-text cannot check effects.
- The sounds play from v4's own audio module, not from `shared/audio.js`, so v3 does not change.

**Rules for learning.** The background sound stops during checked moments, so it never covers letter sounds or her thinking time. No effect plays over Elizabeth. A miss never sounds more fun than a right answer.

**Credits.** Part 2, section 21 has the exact list: about 2,000 credits for 16 effects and 2 background sounds in one take, about 1,250 for 68 new speech lines, and about 1,600 kept back for retakes, inside the 5,000 of decision 3.

## 11. Decisions (Tjitse, 28 October 2026: "Lets do it")

1. **Dressing limits:** as proposed. A 45 s start mirror without a challenge, and one Corner visit per play, after her first quest, behind two phrase reads, for 60 s.
2. **A bigger world:** as proposed. Fill today's meadow now; the fence moves in 2.4 with riding and the trails.
3. **Audio credits:** 5,000 credits for 2.3.0, for speech and sound effects together. `tools/audio/budget.json` has the cap at 7,277 (2,276.9 booked before 2.3.0, plus 5,000).
4. **Protecting her collection:** not answered. Claude's call: accept the risk in 2.3.0, write the new save key next to the 2.2 key, and ask the browser to keep the storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`) where it exists.

Tjitse's further asks, the same evening:

- **Three sums per egg.** One sum per egg is not enough. Each egg asks three, at mixed levels: some easier, some harder. At her school everything stays within 10, so at least one sum per egg is within 10 (section 12).
- **Address the critics.** The three points the critics found (white, grey and black cannot be tinted; the quest cutoff counts quest time; a new channel in `shared/audio.js` would change v3) are handled as sections 6, 5 and 8 say.
- **A solid mix of play and learning.** The game must stay interesting enough that she keeps her attention, with many words and much maths in different forms.

## 12. Three sums per egg (Claude's calls)

- An egg hatches after three sums. Three warm hearts on the nest rim light up one per right sum, and the egg glows and wobbles more after each. The hatch comes after the third.
- Per egg: one easy sum within 10 (as at school), one middle sum that ends at or just past 10, and one hard sum across ten at her maths band. The easy one comes first; the other two come in either order.
- Warm egg (adding, the missing part): easy needs 6 to 10 with 3 to 7 warm (add 3 to 6); middle needs 10 to 12; hard keeps the 2.2 bands (11 to 15). Snow egg (taking away): easy starts at 7 to 10 and takes away 3 to 5; middle starts at 11 or 12 and ends under 10; hard keeps the 2.2 bands.
- The floor stays: no sum adds or takes away 1 or 2. Stones stay 3 to 9 with the 2.2 wrong-stone kinds.
- Every sum is its own checked moment with its own hint ladder and its own result line. The band moves per sum, as in 2.2.
- About 20 to 25 s per sum, so the two eggs take about 2 minutes longer than in 2.2.

## 13. Calls Claude made in Part 2 (check these first)

- **Earrings and the necklace sit on the eyes tab.** The sparkles tab cannot hold ten hair things, jewels and two colour runs in five rails. The eyes tab frames her face, where the earrings and the necklace show.

- **The quest cutoff counts free time, not wall time.** No picnic or maths quest starts after 90 s of free time. The trunk bubble stays until free play ends.
- **The first 2.3 play offers the bead tree**, then the maths quests take turns, and the one she did not finish comes again.
- **The egg's hearts are on the 2D panel** (three hearts above the cups), and the 3D egg glows warmer per heart. The panel stays on screen between the three sums.
- **The snow egg's easy sum needs at least 4 cups**, so the goal clip exists for every sum (num-egg-4 and num-egg-5 are new).
- **The music box tune, the colour words and the phrase clips are new; the Corner's opening line is v3's** `lumi-dressing-open` "Come and try my Dressing Corner!", said when the trunk bubble first shows.
- **The 2.2 panel and actor code keep running on a legacy catalogue** (`src/wardrobe-legacy.ts`) until each lane moves its own files over. The game plays as 2.2 after step 0.
- **The mist is not an obstacle.** It lies behind the stables' back line, where nobody walks, so no route changes.

- **Checked time** is about 9.5 minutes: 6 egg sums of 22 s, 2 namings of 60 s, 5 word reads and 2 phrase reads of 30 s, 3 or 1 maths quest moments, 3 doors of 15 s. That is half the play.
- **Free time** is the start mirror (at most 45 s) and free play (120 s, at most 150 s, with up to 60 s of dressing inside it). The rest is walking, story and slide play.
- **Forms.** Reading: CVC to CCVC and CVCC words, phrases of two read words with "the", and spelling a name. Maths: the missing part (adding), taking away, numerals on doors, and patterns. Every reading moment and every maths moment is a reason to go on: a baby's wish, an egg that hatches, a gift for her trunk, the Dressing Corner.

**Where the mix was weak, and the fix (all within sections 0 to 12):**

1. **Phrases come only at the Corner.** A play where she finishes no quest has no phrase. Fix (Claude's call): the colour words she hears in the start mirror are printed in the wardrobe's word pill when they are one syllable of at most five letters (pink, red, gold, green, blue, white, brown). She sees and hears the printed colour words on every play, before she must read them.
2. **Seven adding sums of one shape.** With three sums per egg, the hay rack is the seventh missing-part sum. Fix: the bead tree takes every other play (section 17.4), so patterns, a new form, come every second play, and first.
3. **Three sums in a row may lose her.** Fix (Claude's call): the panel stays on screen between sums; sums 2 and 3 skip the intro; a heart lights with the `heart-glow` effect and "A happy heart!"; the egg glows warmer and wobbles more. The easy sum comes first and is short.
4. **More reading data.** The two gate reads feed the reading level, so it moves on 7 reads a play instead of 5.

## 20. Sound (sound lane)

**The v4 channel (`src/audio/sound.ts`).** It runs on `EmmaAudio.context`, the shared player's context, which `audio.unlock()` unlocks in the Play tap. Its own gain nodes go to the destination, so `shared/audio.js` and v3 do not change.

- Two buses. Effects: gain 0.8, 0.3 while speech plays (60 ms down, back 400 ms after speech ends). Ambience: 0.22 by day and 0.26 by night, 0.08 under speech, 0 during checked moments (400 ms out, back 1.5 s after the moment).
- `fx(id, { duck, fallback })`: skipped when speech is playing, unless `duck` (only for Lumi's or a foal's story line, never Elizabeth's). An effect still sounding when speech starts ducks with the bus. A missing file plays the synthesised fallback.
- `ambience('day' | 'night' | null)`: one looping buffer source with loopStart and loopEnd from the manifest (no MP3 gap), crossfaded over 2 s. `audio.stop()` never stops it. Step 0 wires day at Play, night at dusk, and the right one on a jump.
- `checked(on)` nests; step 0's `checked()` in game.ts calls it.
- `preloadFx()` decodes the 16 effects and the day loop after the speech preloads (step 0 wires it in `begin`); the night loop loads at dusk. Mono, so all of it is about 7 MB decoded.
- `audio.muted` from the shared player is respected.

**The music box tune (made in code).** An original eight-bar melody in C major, 3/4 at 92 beats a minute, played with a bell voice (a triangle and a sine an octave up, 1.2 s decay). It loops the eight bars to fill `ms`; the last four bars slow to 70% by scheduling (not playbackRate, so the pitch stays) and the last note lands on the tonic at `ms`. It plays on the effects bus, so it ducks under speech.

**The generator (`tools/audio/gen-sfx.py` and `sfx.sh`, sound lane).** Run outside the sandbox: `sh games/moon-meadow-world/tools/audio/sfx.sh [--probe] [--only a,b] [--retake id] [--dry-run]`.

- It imports `load_key`, `http`, `credits_left` and `read_ledger` from `scripts/generate-audio.py` with importlib, so the speech script does not change and the key is read only inside that process.
- For each effect or loop without `public/audio/sfx/<id>.mp3` (or with `--retake`): planned credits = seconds × `sfxPerSecond` (budget.json, 40). It refuses when the ledger plus the plan passes `cap`, or when the account would fall under `floor`.

- `--probe` makes only `bedtime-bell` (2 s), reads the account counter before and 60 s after, and writes `sfxProbe` and, when it differs, `sfxPerSecond` into budget.json. It runs first, once the sound lane is merged.
- `--dry-run` prints the plan and the credits and calls nothing. The lane tests it without the network.

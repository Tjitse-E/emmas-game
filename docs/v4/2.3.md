# Moon Meadow World 2.3.0: proposal and build design

Made on 28 October 2026 from Emma's feedback on 2.2.0 and Tjitse's notes. Four code readers, three designers (learning first, Emma first, build first) and two critics (learning, feasibility) produced it. Tjitse approved the direction (section 11). Part 2, after section 12, is the build design (layout, contracts, lanes, tests), as in [2.2.md](2.2.md).

## 0. Calls Claude made (check these first)

- **Colours are free from the first play.** Only items (shapes) are earned. Green hair with a green gown is her own example, and holding it back would refuse it. Tjitse's limit applies to dressing time and to new items.
- **Dressing time counts as free-play time.** The 60 s at the Dressing Corner comes out of free play's 120 s of unchecked play, so a play has no more unchecked time than 2.2.
- **Patterns come before doubles.** The bead tree teaches patterns. Doubles ("the same on each earring") carry the unclear-goal risk that postponed the twin egg in 2.2. They wait for a mockup Emma tries first.
- **The fence stays where it is in 2.3.** New places fill the empty parts of today's meadow. The meadow grows with riding in 2.4 (decision 2).
- **No new gown shapes.** The four gown cells (party dress, ball gown, ball gown with gloves, tutu) take any colour, and the wings and the back bow become extras she can wear with any gown. 2.2's fairy dress was the party dress with wings, so it is now that pair (Part 2 settles this, section 15).

## 1. Summary

- **One palette for everything.** Colour and shape are split. Hair, gown, crown, shoes, hair things and jewellery each take any of about 12 colours plus rainbow. Green eyes, a green gown and green hair work on her first 2.3 play.
- **More to wear.** Hair styles go from 4 to 12. New rows for the crown (5 shapes), shoes (4 shapes, with toe tips under the ball gowns), earrings and a necklace.
- **The Dressing Corner.** Lumi's gazebo trunk becomes a place where she changes during the game. Her two babies dress up first (two reading moments). Then a music box plays for 60 s while she dresses. It ends kindly and her look is kept.
- **A collection she earns.** Every side quest gives a gift: she picks one of two new things, and it goes into the trunk. The inside of the trunk lid shows every set, and an empty place shows the picture of the quest that fills it.
- **New learning.** Reading: colour words in short phrases with "the" ("the green sock"), where she must read both words. Maths: patterns on bead strings at a new bead tree.
- **More sound.** About 16 ElevenLabs sound effects and a day background sound, plus three synthesised effects the game has but never plays. The night sound waits for 2.3.1.

## 3. The Dressing Corner

**Where.** At the south step of Lumi's gazebo (-14, 2.4). The lilac trunk is already in the gazebo ("a little dressing corner for later", `places.ts:89`). It moves to the step, next to a new standing mirror (about 300 triangles). Her followers and the herd near the gazebo step aside, as at the picnic.

**When.** A trunk bubble appears in free play once she has finished her first quest of the afternoon, because her gift waits in the trunk. She taps the bubble or the gazebo, or walks near the step. One visit per play.

**The challenge.** Lumi: "Your babies want to dress up first!" Each baby gets a phrase wish, such as "the green sock" printed in its bubble, with a basket of 4 or 5 tinted pictures (section 6). A right pick: the baby wears it on its head. A wrong pick: the 2.2 light contrast (the wrong picture shows its own words, and the letters that differ glow). Two reads, about one minute. They are checked moments and move their own phrase level, so the new form never makes her word baskets easier (section 18).

**The time limit.** The trunk opens and the gifts pop out. A music box on the panel plays a tune made in code, and six moon beads around it fade, one every 10 s. There is no clock and no number. With 15 s left, Elizabeth says "Nearly ready!" Done ends the visit sooner.

**The kind ending.** When the tune ends, the game waits until her finger is up (read from `window`, at most 2 s). Then Princess Emma curtsies, the chime plays and Elizabeth says "Lovely!" Her look is saved as it is. Nothing is taken off and nothing says no.

**The start mirror.** The customiser after Play stays, with everything she owns and the same music box, but 45 s and no challenge (decision 1). On a first ever play the crown row is not there yet. It appears after Lumi gives the crown.

## 4. The wardrobe

**Palette.** Twelve colours (eleven and rainbow), green among them, and for the hair six natural shades as well (Part 2, section 15 has the ids and hex values). The crown keeps white gems as its outline, and a test checks every hair colour against every crown colour.

**Hair styles (12).** Today's 4, plus 6 that are only data (low pigtails, side ponytail, curly pigtails, long straight tails, short bunches, a high ponytail with curls) and 2 with small code changes (one plait, a low bun). Loose waves and two buns stay out: they clip through the wings and cost frame time. Styles must differ in outline, because ponytail variants look alike from the follow camera.

**Crowns, shoes, jewellery.** Crowns are cached shapes with a colour (Lumi's crown, a tiara, a star, a heart and a flower crown). Shoes: slippers, bow shoes, boots and star shoes, with toe tips under the ball gowns so they show with every gown. Earrings at the ears and a necklace of beads. Hair things keep today's six and get four more (shells, moons, daisies, cherries).

**The panel.** Six fixed tabs in the icon column: hair, eyes, gown, crown, shoes, sparkles. The earrings and the necklace sit on the eyes tab, which frames her face (Part 2, section 16). A tab acts on pointerdown and changes what the rails hold. Each tab has at most five rails of six cells, so nothing pages and nothing moves under a finger. Only items she owns show. When she picks a colour, Elizabeth says its name ("Green."), so the colour words are known before she reads them.

**Budget.** Her busiest look today is 7,846 triangles against a test cap of 8,000. The cap goes to 10,000. New parts add no draw calls. The portrait is measured again at the gazebo step.

## 5. Earning and the collection

Each quest gives one gift per play. She picks one of two items from that quest's set. She puts it on and keeps it, and a sparkle trail takes it to her trunk with the `fanfare` sound. When a set is full, its quest puts a sticker on the trunk lid and from then on pays from the set with the most items left.

| Set | Earned at | Learning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 crowns | Royal picnic (2.2) | Two near-word reads |
| 3 shoes | Hay rack (2.2) | Adding within 10 (section 19.1) |
| 4 earrings, 2 necklaces | Bead tree (new) | Patterns |
| 4 hair styles, 4 hair things | Dressing Corner gate | Two phrase reads |

Ribbons stay Lumi's goodnight gift. There are 21 items in all, about 12 to 15 plays of collecting.

Each afternoon offers the picnic and one maths quest (the rack and the bead tree in turn, and the one she missed last time comes first). The trunk bubble follows her first finished quest. The cutoff for new quests counts free time without dressing (section 17.3), so dressing never costs her the maths quest.

## 6. New learning

**Colour phrases (reading, GOAL "short phrases", v3 step R27).**

- The unit is a phrase: "the", a colour and a thing. Only colours that a tint can show are used. Green, gold, blue and brown are targets. Red and pink are decoys only, because no other colour shares their first letter. Black, grey and white wait for coloured art.
- A basket is a two by two grid plus one: two colours with the same first letter times two things with the same first letter. For "the blue bow": blue bow, brown bow, blue bead, brown bead, and from level 2 one more near item. A child who reads only first letters picks right 1 time in 4.
- Things come from v3's greyscale pictures that have an ok clip: bag, bead and bow, sock and shirt (Part 2, section 18). Cape and coat look alike, so they never share a basket.
- She reads past the first letter in both words: gr against go, bl against br, sock against shirt, and bow against bead.
- Elizabeth reads the phrase after she acts. "The" is printed in every phrase. It never decides an answer, so it only gives her practice in seeing a tricky word.
- Every tinted picture with an ok check plays from her first play; Emma names them on her iPad's picture check, and a cross turns one off (section 18). Gold may look yellow to her.

**Patterns (maths, GOAL "patterns", M08).**

- At the bead tree a baby's necklace has three gaps. The beads differ in shape and colour (heart, star, moon, round), so colour alone never fills a gap.
- Strings go from ABB and ABC to AABB, with gaps in the middle at the higher level. Each pattern is chosen so that copying the last bead, or swapping between two beads, is wrong at least once.
- Each gap gives its answer at once. Odds per gap are 1 in 3 or 4, and the hint ladder works as with the eggs.
- Patterns may be easy for her, because they come before the across-ten sums she does now. The log shows it within a few plays, and then doubles move up.

**What Tjitse can see.** Each play sends one `kind=play` line with the checked time, the dressing time and the first-try rate of the gate reads. After three plays this shows whether dressing is pushing out learning.

## 8. Sound

**Free, in 2.3.0 for sure.** The game already has synthesised `hatch`, `munch` and `fanfare`, but never plays them. They go on the egg, the food and every new item. The music box is made in code.

**ElevenLabs sound effects.**

- About 16 effects of about 1.5 s: egg crack, a foal's happy nicker (two), stable door, trunk lid, gift paper, dress swish, bead clink, crown twinkle, hay rustle, a big splash (two), slide whoosh, bedtime bell, an owl at night.
- One background sound of 15 s, mono: a day meadow (birds and a breeze). The night one (crickets) waits for 2.3.1: it would cost 600 credits for about 4 minutes of play, most of them checked moments where it is silent.
- No footsteps or hooves: with up to 20 unicorns they would sound all the time.
- Tick, pop and chime stay synthesised. They cost nothing and start without delay.
- First a probe: one 2 s effect, to measure what it really takes from the account. The API has no seed, so every approved file is kept. Tjitse listens to every sound, because speech-to-text cannot check effects.
- The sounds play from v4's own audio module, not from `shared/audio.js`, so v3 does not change.

**Rules for learning.** The background sound stops during checked moments, so it never covers letter sounds or her thinking time. No effect plays over or under Elizabeth, letter sounds included: her line waits for an effect (at most 1 s), and a pick in the wardrobe that says a word plays no effect. A miss never sounds more fun than a right answer.

**Credits.** Part 2, section 21 has the exact list: about 1,400 credits for 16 effects and the day sound in one take, about 1,500 for 72 new speech lines with their retakes, and about 2,100 kept back, inside the 5,000 of decision 3.

## 11. Decisions (Tjitse, 28 October 2026: "Lets do it")

1. **Dressing limits:** as proposed. A 45 s start mirror without a challenge, and one Corner visit per play, after her first quest, behind two phrase reads, for 60 s.
2. **A bigger world:** as proposed. Fill today's meadow now; the fence moves in 2.4 with riding and the trails.
3. **Audio credits:** 5,000 credits for 2.3.0, for speech and sound effects together. `tools/audio/budget.json` has the cap at 7,277 (2,276.9 booked before 2.3.0, plus 5,000).
4. **Protecting her collection:** not answered. Claude's call: accept the risk in 2.3.0, write the new save key next to the 2.2 key, and ask the browser to keep the storage (`navigator.storage.persist()`) where it exists.

Tjitse's further asks, the same evening:

- **Three sums per egg.** One sum per egg is not enough. Each egg asks three, at mixed levels: some easier, some harder. His words: "On her school everything is until the number 10". 2.3.0 read this as one sum per egg within 10 and the other two past 10. That was wrong: every sum stays within 10, the hay rack's too (section 12, corrected on 29 October 2026).
- **Address the critics.** The three points the critics found (white, grey and black cannot be tinted; the quest cutoff counts quest time; a new channel in `shared/audio.js` would change v3) are handled as sections 6, 5 and 8 say.
- **A solid mix of play and learning.** The game must stay interesting enough that she keeps her attention, with many words and much maths in different forms.

## 12. Three sums per egg (Claude's calls)

- An egg hatches after three sums. Three warm hearts on the nest rim light up one per right sum, and the egg glows and wobbles more after each (the snow egg glows frostier). The hatch comes after the third.
- **Every sum stays within 10, as at her school** (corrected on 29 October 2026; 2.3.0 asked a middle sum of 11 or 12 and a hard sum of 13 to 16, and the hay rack 11 to 15). The need, the warm cups, the answer and every stone are 10 or less. GOAL.md asks for adding and taking away within 10 and for number bonds to 10, so the hard sum is a bond to 10. The old line that bonds to 10 are too easy for her is overridden by Tjitse's ask and GOAL.md.
- Per egg: an easy sum (total 7 or 8), a middle sum (total 9) and a hard sum, a bond to 10 at her maths band (band B shows 5 stones). The easy one comes first; the other two come in either order.
- Warm egg (adding, the missing part): easy needs 7 or 8 with 3 to 5 warm; middle needs 9 with 3 to 6 warm; hard needs 10 with 3 to 7 warm. Snow egg (taking away): easy starts at 7 or 8; middle starts at 9; hard takes away from 10. No total is under 7 (spec 4.4). Section 19.1 has the exact table.
- The floor stays: no sum adds or takes away 1 or 2 (the answer and the other part are 3 or more). A wrong stone may now be a 2 (section 19.1), never the answer. The easy and middle sums show 4 stones; the hard sum shows 4 or 5 by her band.
- Every sum is its own checked moment with its own hint ladder and its own result line. Only the hard sum (and the hay rack) moves the band, because the easy sum is nearly always a first try.
- About 20 to 25 s per sum, so the two eggs take about 2 minutes longer than in 2.2.

## 13. Calls Claude made in Part 2 (check these first)

- **Earrings and the necklace sit on the eyes tab.** The sparkles tab cannot hold ten hair things, jewels and two colour runs in five rails. The eyes tab frames her face, where the earrings and the necklace show.
- **The gown cells are party, ball, gala and tutu.** "Gala" is 2.2's lilac ball gown with white gloves. 2.2's fairy dress is the party dress with the wings extra.
- **Phrase things are bag, bead, bow, sock and shirt.** Cape and coat look alike (2.2's group cape, cloak, coat), so the proposal's "cape against coat" pair is out.
- **Dressing ends kindly but never makes free play run long.** The Corner's music box gets the free time that is left, at least 30 s and at most 60 s. Free play never ends while the wardrobe is open, so a play has at most 150 s of free time.
- **The quest cutoff counts free time, not wall time.** No picnic or maths quest starts after 90 s of free time, not counting dressing (17.3). The trunk bubble stays until free play ends.
- **The first 2.3 play offers the bead tree**, then the maths quests take turns, and the one she did not finish comes again.
- **The egg's hearts are on the 2D panel** (three hearts above the cups), and the 3D egg glows more per heart (warmer on the warm egg, frostier on the snow egg). The panel stays on screen between the three sums.
- **The snow egg's easy sum needs at least 4 cups**, so the goal clip exists for every sum (num-egg-4 and num-egg-5 are new). Since the sums stay within 10 (19.1) it needs at least 3, with num-egg-3.
- **The music box tune, the colour words and the phrase clips are new; the Corner's opening line is v3's** `lumi-dressing-open` "Come and try my Dressing Corner!", said when the trunk bubble first shows.
- **The 2.2 panel and actor code keep running on a legacy catalogue** (`src/wardrobe-legacy.ts`) until each lane moves its own files over. The game plays as 2.2 after step 0.
- **The mist is not an obstacle.** It lies behind the stables' back line, where nobody walks, so no route changes.

After the two reviews (section 24):

- **A gift stays on.** She keeps wearing the item she picked and it is saved. One item does not undo the dressing limit, and taking it off would be a refusal.
- **A full set keeps paying.** Its quest then pays from the set with the most items left (`giftSetFor`), so the picnic, the only reading quest, still pays after four plays.
- **Phrases play from her first play** (decision 1, since section 25's follow-up). The live game uses every ok pair of `tools/phrase-check.json`, Claude's checks too, minus each pair crossed on her iPad's check page (`check.html`, 18). A cross turns a pair off at once. With fewer than 6 strong ones the gate plays two 2.2 dress-up word wishes instead, and the build warns instead of stopping. `?phrases=emma` keeps the rule of the second pass, only the pairs Emma has checked; `?phrases=all` takes every ok pair whatever the iPad holds (flows and dev).
- **Phrases have their own level** (`save.phrases`, starting at her word level), as patterns do.
- **Level 1 phrases** pair the colour with a target colour of another first letter, and the thing with its partner. Every item can be a target, so nothing is ruled out by its tier. Gold and brown never share a basket (Claude's call after the final review, 29 October 2026): gold renders ochre on her iPad, phrases play before Emma has named the pictures, and a cross cannot take that pairing out without turning every phrase off. So level 1 pairs gold with blue and brown with green.
- **New egg ranges and one band rule.** Easy and middle sums never reach a bond to 10 and show 4 stones; hard sums start at 13. Only the hard sum and the hay rack move the band. Replaced on 29 October 2026: every sum stays within 10 and the hard sum is the bond to 10 (19.1). The band rule stays.
- **Free time.** The quest cutoff counts free time without dressing. The Corner's free time is the music box's time only.
- **Nothing sounds under Elizabeth.** Her line waits for an effect (`fx()` resolves, at most 1 s); a wardrobe pick that says a word plays no effect; the music box goes to 0.1 under speech.
- **The night loop waits for 2.3.1.**
- **The bead tree shows its first gap** with the ghost on her first visit ever (`save.seen.beads`).
- **The bead trays: both of 19.2's fixes** (Claude's call). Level 1 has a tray of 4 with both cross beads, and ABC is rare at levels 2 and 3, so colour alone or shape alone fills a whole string at first try in 12.5% of level 1 strings and about 14.5% at levels 2 and 3.
- **A tap on the trunk after her visit** opens the treasure board for 3.5 s (`layer.board`). Quest bubbles carry a small swatch of a gift she can still earn (`badge`).
- **Quests return framed.** Each quest resolves with the frame on and a release function; quests.ts runs the gift picker in that frame, then releases.

**Where the mix was weak, and the fix (all within sections 0 to 12):**

1. **Phrases come only at the Corner.** A play where she finishes no quest has no phrase. Fix (Claude's call): the colour words she hears in the start mirror are printed in the wardrobe's word pill when they are one syllable of at most five letters (pink, red, gold, green, blue, white, brown). She sees and hears the printed colour words on every play, before she must read them.
2. **Seven adding sums of one shape.** With three sums per egg, the hay rack is the seventh missing-part sum. Fix: the bead tree takes every other play (section 17.4), so patterns, a new form, come every second play, and first.
3. **Three sums in a row may lose her.** Fix (Claude's call): the panel stays on screen between sums; sums 2 and 3 skip the intro; a heart lights with the `heart-glow` effect and then "A happy heart!"; the egg glows and wobbles more. The easy sum comes first and is short.
4. **More reading data.** The two gate reads move their own phrase level (section 18), so a hard start with the new two-word form never makes her word baskets easier. The word level still moves on 5 reads a play.
5. **The colour words are printed in normal ink**, in the wardrobe pill, the phrase bubble and the miss tag. A word printed in its own colour could be matched to the picture without reading it.

## 20. Sound (sound lane)

**The v4 channel (`src/audio/sound.ts`).** It runs on `EmmaAudio.context`, the shared player's context, which `audio.unlock()` unlocks in the Play tap. Its own gain nodes go to the destination, so `shared/audio.js` and v3 do not change.

- **Who speaks.** audio/index.ts counts the pending `say`, `sayAll`, `sound` and `hold` calls per speaker and gives the channel `speaking(): 'elizabeth' | 'story' | null`. A story line is a clip whose id starts with `lumi` or `foal` (`isStoryLine`, VOICES.md: the queen and the foals). Every other clip and every letter sound is Elizabeth. Step 0 counts all speech as Elizabeth's and misses letter sounds; the lane fixes both.
- **Three buses.** Effects: gain 0.8; under Elizabeth 0 (an effect still sounding fades out in 60 ms), under a story line 0.3 (60 ms down, back 400 ms after it ends). Music box: 0.6, and 0.1 under any speech, so the tune stays but never covers a word. Ambience: 0.22 by day, 0.08 under speech, 0 during checked moments (400 ms out, back 1.5 s after the moment).
- `fx(id, { duck, fallback }): Promise<void>` (step 0). Skipped while Elizabeth speaks; skipped under a story line unless `duck`. It resolves at the effect's end, never later than 1 s after it started (`FX_WAIT_MS`), and at once when it was skipped or has nothing to play. A missing file plays the synthesised fallback.
- `audio.sfx()` (tick, pop, chime and the other synthesised sounds) keeps its signature and plays on the channel by the rule of `fx()`: skipped while Elizabeth speaks or a letter sound plays, faded out in 60 ms when she starts, 0.3 under a story line, as loud as before otherwise. A synthesised sound and her line in the same tick give only the first 60 ms of the sound, so a caller that wants the whole sound awaits `audio.sfx()` before her line: naming.ts (the chime before a bell's name, the sparkle before the chosen name) and wardrobe.ts (the chime on Done).
- **Sequencing.** A line by Elizabeth that follows an effect awaits it: heart-glow, then `g4-heart`. The bedtime bell is 2 s and `fx()` resolves after at most 1 s, so `g-dusk` would cut its second ring. level.ts starts the bell 1 s into the 3 s dusk fade, so the bell ends with the fade, and `g-dusk` follows the fade: `await g.run.w(Promise.all([setTime(g, 0.5, 3000), g.run.sleep(1000).then(() => audio.fx('bedtime-bell'))]))`. Her line waits for no effect there. In the wardrobe a pick that says a word plays no effect (section 16). The egg crack plays with `duck` under Lumi's "It's hatching!".
- `ambience('day' | 'night' | null)`: one looping buffer source with loopStart and loopEnd from the manifest (no MP3 gap), crossfaded over 2 s. `audio.stop()` never stops it. Step 0 wires day at Play, night at dusk, and the right one on a jump. Night has no file in 2.3.0 (`later` in sfx.json), so at dusk the day loop fades out and the night is quiet.
- `checked(on)` nests; step 0's `checked()` in game.ts calls it, and the eggs hold it across the three sums.
- `preloadFx()` decodes the 16 effects and the day loop after the speech preloads (step 0 wires it in `begin`). Mono, so all of it is about 6 MB decoded.
- `audio.muted` from the shared player is respected.

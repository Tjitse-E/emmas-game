# Concept: Lumi's Lost Colours

Structure: story chapters and quests. Written 2026-10-12 for the v3 plan of Unicorn Egg Nest.

Sources: `docs/v3/research/` (v2-audit, engagement, curriculum, naming, engine), the six files in `docs/v3/brainstorm/`, `docs/v2/design-unicorn-egg-nest.md`, PERSONA.md, GOAL.md and VOICES.md. Step codes R, S and M refer to the ladders in `research/curriculum.md`.

## Summary

The Grumble Cloud woke up grumpy, sneezed, and blew the colours out of Queen Lumi's Moon Kingdom. Only Emma's herd kept its colours. Book One is seven chapters, one per rainbow colour. Each chapter is one land that starts grey and fills with colour as Emma plays. Each chapter brings one new role-play place, one new egg kind and one new item set, and ends with a festival and a new arch on the Rainbow Bridge. In the last chapter the Grumble Cloud comes to the Royal Ball. Emma gives it a name, it turns into a rainbow cloud, and the whole herd parades over the finished bridge.

The v2 core stays at the centre and is always one tap away: warm an egg with number stones, hatch, name and spell the baby, and add it to a herd that never shrinks. The story wraps around that core. It does not replace it. Every egg, every care task and every quest step paints a little more colour back into the land, so a 5-minute visit and a 45-minute visit both move the story.

The ladders (reading, spelling, maths) are separate from the story. The story moves on play. The ladders move on mastery. A chapter never waits for a skill, and a skill never waits for a chapter. Every activity carries content across a wide range of steps, so whatever step Emma is on, the current chapter has work at her level.

Naming is fixed: every safe name she builds is accepted, and a name without a clip yet is sounded out from her own gems and spoken by Elizabeth from the next visit on.

## 1. The fantasy and Emma's role

**Emma is the Nest Keeper, and becomes the Rainbow Keeper.** Queen Lumi still sends the eggs. After the Big Sneeze, Lumi asks Emma for a second job: bring the colours back. The magic rule is simple and said once in the prologue: "Colour comes back when we look after the kingdom." Warming an egg, helping a baby and finishing a job for the Queen all bring colour back.

**Why her herd kept its colours.** "Your babies kept their colours, because you love them." This makes her v2 herd the heroes of the story from the first minute, and it explains why the babies stay bright in a grey land.

**Her role grows in titles she can see on her crown.** Nest Keeper (start), then one jewel and one ribbon per chapter (ruby, amber, topaz, emerald, sapphire, indigo star, amethyst). At the end of Book One the Queen crowns her Rainbow Princess. Book Two makes her Queen of her own castle.

**The Grumble Cloud** is a big, soft, grey cloud with a sulky face and a red nose. It never talks. It grumbles, yawns and sneezes (sound effects only, so no new voice is needed). It is silly, never scary. In each chapter it drifts in once or twice and sneezes things away, which starts a Sneeze Hunt. Each chapter it gets one small patch of colour and grumbles a little less. Emma can comfort it (tap to give it a blanket, a cup of tea, a lullaby), which is free role play. In the Book One finale she names it. That name is never refused either.

**Cast and voices (per VOICES.md, no new casting).**

| Who | Voice | Speaks |
|---|---|---|
| Narrator | Elizabeth (`eleven_v4`) | Every goal, hint, number, word, name, sound, story page. All learning content. |
| Queen Lumi | Posh | Story lines only: greetings, chapter openings, festivals, praise at ceremonies. Never a learning word. |
| Baby unicorns, visiting cousins, grown herd | Flicker | Giggles and tiny fixed words ("Yay!", "Not me!", "More?"). Never a learning word. |
| The Grumble Cloud | Sound effects | Grumbles, sneezes, yawns, a happy rumble at the end. |

**Unicorns only.** Every character is a unicorn: Lumi, Emma's herd, visiting cousins from other lands, the market customers, the helpers who build stalls. No Fluffles, no Pippa, no magpie, no badgers. The Grumble Cloud is weather, not a creature. If Tjitse reads "unicorns only" as no cloud either, it can become a grey mist with no face and the story still works.

## 2. The core loop and session shapes

### 2.1 Three loops inside each other

**The egg loop (2 to 3 minutes, unlimited).** This is the v2 loop with its known fixes.

1. An egg waits in the nest, always. From the first chapter on, two eggs wait: the chapter's egg kind and a golden egg one maths level higher. She picks one (small choice).
2. She warms it with number stones (one maths item). The egg reacts to every stone: steam and red cheeks for too many, frost and shivers for too few, and Elizabeth names the difference.
3. Hatch: wobble, cracks, burst, fanfare. A first try with no hint gives a shiny baby as a surprise.
4. Name and spell (one spelling item). See 2.2.
5. The baby trots to the Stable of Ten. When the stall number is at her maths level it is a quick place-value moment ("Pip is baby twenty-three. Which stall?"). Otherwise the baby trots in on its own.
6. A colour drop flies from the stall to the land and paints one spot (a rose bush turns red).

No cap. The v2 limit of 12 babies is gone, because babies live in the stable and the meadow only shows "today's friends" (up to 12: the newest babies, babies with a wish, and any baby she calls from the stable).

**The quest loop (one step is 2 to 4 minutes).** Lumi's quest scroll hangs by the nest. It shows the current quest as 3 to 5 picture boxes. One box glows. Tapping the glowing box takes her to the place where that step happens, and one round of that activity finishes the step. The box gets a tick, and drops fly to the land. A finished quest delivers its reward through the Pin Post: envelopes addressed to her babies by name.

**The chapter loop (3 to 5 weeks).** A chapter has 3 or 4 quests and a land to paint. When all quests are done and the land is full of colour, the chapter festival starts on her next visit (or straight away if she wants). The bridge gets its arch, the crown gets its jewel, and the next land appears.

Next to these, **free play** is always open: care stations, the Salon, the Kitchen, the album, the stable. Free play with her own herd is never checked. Checked work arrives with a visitor or a card, so it is always clear when the game wants something (roleplay brainstorm, rule 1).

**Colour drops are not a currency.** Nothing is bought with them, and no line ever says "do this to get drops". They are the world changing because she played. Every completed checked moment gives one drop, whether it was a first try or helped, so a hard day still moves the story. A first try adds a little sparkle to the drop, nothing more.

### 2.2 Naming in v3: never refuse a safe name

From `research/naming.md`, with the name audio decision made here.

- **Three ways to name.** (a) Name bells at her spelling level, fully checked, as in v2. (b) The name garden: picture groups (flowers, gems, sky, sweets) and first-sound groups. She picks a list name like Lily or Luna, Elizabeth says it, and she spells it with gift gems for the parts above her level. Only her slots are checked. (c) The gold bell: free building with 2 to 6 slots and her full gem set. Free building is never a miss.
- **Checks in this order.** No vowel: "Every name needs a pink gem!" (a phonics rule, not a refusal). A strong word: calm "Let's pick another name", nothing repeated, logged for Tjitse. A toilet word: the baby giggles and shakes its head, "That's a silly name!". A list name: Elizabeth says it. A near match: a second bell offers the list name next to hers, and she picks either. Anything else: accepted.
- **A brand new name.** The tag clicks on at once. The slider plays her own letter sounds. Lumi says "A brand new name! I will whisper it to the Moon tonight." On a later visit, once the clip exists, Elizabeth says the name for the first time, the baby jumps, and gets a heart. Until then, lines that need the name skip it, and that baby is left out of roll call.
- **Duplicates are allowed.** "Another Tim! Tim the Second!" A royal number fits the kingdom.
- **Dropped v2 rules:** same first and last letter, wish words as names, the 3-slot limit, the 7-gem tray, and the "I don't know that name" line.
- **Every build is logged**, so the parent corner shows what she tried and the blocklist and name bank learn from real play.
- **Name audio decision.** Pre-generate a name bank (about 600 names in month 1, 1,000 by month 2, 2,000 by month 3). New names travel to odin inside the save sync (no key involved), and the generator makes "Name!" and "Name?" in a batch with the speech-to-text check. Tjitse runs that batch, or switches on a scheduled nightly run once he trusts it. Generation never happens during play, and the key never leaves the generator. This keeps the "generate ahead of time" rule and needs no on-demand server (naming.md D1 stays closed unless the wait bothers Emma).
- **Names get a purpose.** From chapter 3 the Alphabet Arches light up for each first sound she has used, so she names on purpose to fill the grey arches. Roll call, the Pin Post, place cards and plaques make her read her own names back for months.

**How a long visit avoids turning into drill.** The item planner counts checked items per strand in the visit. After about 12 checked items in one strand, new items for that strand stop (review only), and after about 30 checked items in total, visitor cards come less often and free play is offered first. Eggs always stay available, because the egg is what she loves. A long visit gets more play, not more tests. The game never says "one more" and never ends a visit by itself. A parent setting can add a soft goodnight offer after a set time (off by default).

**Balance of strands.** Eggs carry maths and spelling but no reading. If a visit is egg-heavy, roll call gets an extra lantern and the next arrival starts with a baby wish (a reading item). Reading is also carried by quests, care wishes, cards, plaques and story pages.

## 3. What "levels" are, and how the ladders map onto them

### 3.1 Three kinds of level

| Level | What it is | How she sees it | How it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chapter** | A land, a colour, a new place to play, a new egg kind, a new item set | The grey land filling with colour, the bridge arch, the festival, the crown jewel | Play: quests done and the land painted. Never mastery. |
| **Quest** | 3 to 5 steps of one small story ("A cake for Lumi") | The scroll with picture boxes and ticks | Play: each step finishes when the round finishes |
| **Skill step** | One rung on R, S or M | A Moon Egg Night, a Moon Unicorn, a jewel chip on the crown, a letter pin for each secured grapheme | Mastery rules in curriculum.md 5.4 |

Herd milestones (10, 20, 25, 50, 75, 100 babies) are a fourth, smaller kind of level: a parade and a new thing at the stable.

This gives Tjitse's "actual game with levels" twice over: big story levels she can see coming, and frequent skill level-ups that are real learning, celebrated without a test feeling.

### 3.2 Why the story and the ladders are kept apart

A story that waits for a skill stalls: one hard step (for example R11 Dutch pairs) would freeze the whole kingdom for weeks. So chapters move on play, and every activity carries a wide range of steps. The Kitchen works for counting to 10 (M02) and for fractions of amounts (M45). The Salon works for one-word cards (R01) and two-sentence cards (R29). Whatever step she is on, the current chapter has work at that step.

Three things stop this from feeling too easy:

- Every checked moment uses her own current step, review items and mixed old items (curriculum 5.5: 50, 30 and 20 percent).
- The golden egg is always one maths level up, and she chooses it herself.
- Skill steps have their own visible reward (Moon Egg Nights), so progress in learning is felt separately from progress in the story.

### 3.3 Skill homes and the adaptive chapter order

Some skills need a special place: coins need the Market (M32, M33, M44), clock times need the Moon Clock (M34, M46), measuring needs Growing Day (M25, M47), the number line needs Trail Rides and Flying Lessons (M19, M38). Chapters 1 to 3 have a fixed order, because they teach the structure. **Chapters 4, 5 and 6 come in any order.** When chapter 3 ends, Lumi picks the next land whose place serves the strand that is closest to needing it. If Emma reaches money early, the Market comes before the Woods. The bridge arches fill in their rainbow position whatever the order, so the picture still makes sense.

If a strand reaches a step whose home is still locked, the item planner serves extra review and mixed items in that strand until the home opens (at most one chapter). The parent corner shows this, and Tjitse can unlock a home early.

### 3.6 Mastery and review

As in `research/curriculum.md` section 5: a scaffold staircase inside each step (3 first tries up, 2 misses in a row down), step mastery over a rolling window across at least two days, and a Leitner box per item (grapheme, word, tricky word, name, fact). About a third of every visit is review, hidden in the same moments. Old words come back as old babies' wishes, old names come back at roll call and on envelopes, old bonds come back in eggs. A step that slips below 70 percent on review reopens quietly, shown in the world as a returning visitor, never as "you forgot".

## 4. Role play activities

Each activity is a toy first. With her own herd she plays freely and nothing is checked. Checked work arrives as a card, a visitor or a quest step. A mistake is a small funny event in the world, then the v2 hint ladder: idle repeats the goal, one miss gives a focused hint, two misses show the answer with the ghost hand (logged as helped). Nothing a wrong act produces is a reward, so random tapping does not pay.

### 4.1 Care stations and baby wishes (always open, from the prologue)

From reading brainstorm idea 1 (Baby Talk), with memory pearls (wildcard 6) folded in.

- **What she does.** The meadow has care stations: bath tub, hay rack, brush stand, swing, nap cushion, and one more per chapter (the pond at the lake, the berry bush in the Orchard). Free care gives hearts and growth. A baby sometimes hops over with a speech bubble in print: "bath", later "I want my red bow." She takes the baby to the right station or gives the right thing.
- **Learning.** Reading from single words (R01) to two sentences (R29). Old babies bring old words back (spaced review). Some wishes carry numbers ("3 apples", M02 to M14).
- **A mistake.** She puts the baby on the swing when it asked for the bath. It swings once, looks puzzled, hops off and giggles. "bath" glows with its sound dots. After one miss the baby waits until she slides along the word before it moves again.

### 4.2 Sneeze Hunt (every chapter, the running gag)

From reading idea 3 (The Magpie's Treasure Trail), with the magpie replaced by the Grumble Cloud.

- **What she does.** The Grumble Cloud drifts in and sneezes. Pins, bells and bows fly off her babies and land hidden in the scene: in a pot, under a log, behind a hat. The baby that saw it holds up a speech bubble: "It is in the pot." She lifts the right hiding place, the pin flies back onto the baby's sash, and a drop paints the land.
- **Learning.** Object words (R01 to R10), position phrases (R27: "under the big log"), two clues with "not" (R29), later left, right and "the second bush" (M35).
- **A mistake.** She lifts the pan. Inside is an old sock, and the Grumble Cloud giggles for the first time. The deciding word glows. The first hunt shows the pin falling into the pot before the mist, so she learns that the note says where it is.

### 4.3 Sparkle Salon (chapter 1)

From roleplay idea 1, merged with maths idea 6 (Mane Salon).

- **What she does.** A mirror, a stool, bead jars, a ribbon rack and the pin box. Free styling of her own babies, a camera for the album. A visiting cousin rings the counter bell and holds up a style card.
- **Learning.** Cards: one word ("bow"), then colour and size phrases ("a big pink bow", R27), then two short lines with a tick box each (R28, R29). Bead patterns to continue (AB, ABB, ABC, growing patterns, M08). "Make both braids the same" (doubles, M23), "8 beads, the same on each side" (halves, M24). Bead strings of ten for big styles (M27).
- **A mistake.** The cousin looks in the mirror, tilts its head and giggles: "Green? Hee hee!" The bow floats back to the rack. A wrong pattern bead makes the braid twirl and that bead glows until she swaps it.
- **Why it matters.** The pins she loved become real sets with silhouettes, and each pin clips onto a baby she named.

### 4.4 Moonberry Kitchen and Tea Parties (chapter 2)

From roleplay idea 2, merged with maths idea 3 (Moonberry Picnic) and reading idea 4 (Moon Café).

- **What she does.** Two screens, never mixed. Cook: a recipe card with a picture and lines ("6 buns", later "3 figs. 2 nuts."). She fills the bowl, stirs, and the treat comes out with a funny effect (a rainbow cupcake turns a mane rainbow for the visit). Tea party: guests sit by place cards, and she shares food fairly.
- **Learning.** Counting to 10 then 20 (M02, M07), missing part ("7 berries, 3 are in", M09 to M12), sharing and odd and even (M06, M30), cake halves and quarters by swiping a cut line (M24, M31, M45), cupcake trays as arrays (M29). Place cards with her herd's names (name review, R38 later). Recipe steps as sentences (R28).
- **A mistake.** Too many berries roll off with a "bloop" and hang above the bowl, so she sees how many too many. Too few: the cake comes out flat and the baby says "More?". Unequal plates: the baby with more pushes one berry across with its nose, "Tam has two more than Kip." Wrong seat: the baby looks at the card, shakes its mane, "Not me!", and hops down.

### 4.5 Unicorn School (chapter 3)

From roleplay idea 3, wildcard 1, world idea 7 and reading idea 7. Moon School jobs are left for Book Two.

- **What she does.** A schoolroom in a sunflower. Emma is the teacher with a bell, a gold star stamp and a magic eraser. Two or three babies sit at desks with slates. Elizabeth says the target ("Bed."). The baby has built b, e, t. The slate plays back what the baby built. Emma swaps the wrong gem, slides, and the baby gets its chalk star.
- **Learning.** Segmenting and checking (S02 to S30). This is the home of the Dutch contrast steps (R11, S06, R20, M20): the baby makes the slip Emma is likely to make (bet for bed, "moan" for moon, 42 for twenty-four), and she fixes it with no risk to herself. About a third of the slates are already right, so she must really check.
- **A mistake.** If she stamps a wrong slate, the baby proudly carries it to Lumi at the end. "Let's listen together." The slate plays back, the baby says "Oops!" and they fix it together. If she changes a right slate, the slate plays her version and the baby tilts its head.
- **Start.** First lessons use mistakes that are obvious in the picture (a cat picture, a slate that says "dog"), and the ghost hand shows both tools once.

### 4.6 Trail Rides (Green chapter)

From roleplay idea 9, merged with world idea 5 (Egg Trails).

- **What she does.** She picks one to three grown babies and rides out. One fork on screen at a time, the goal picture always visible in a bubble (a wild egg, a lost pin). Numbered stepping stones, word signposts, a bridge with a gap.
- **Learning.** Hop along a number line (dots at first, then numerals, M02, M04, M19, later jumps of 10 and 5, M26, M38). Signposts: "pond", "hill", then "marsh", "cliff" (R21 to R23), then "The bridge is shut. Go by the hill." (R28, R29). Left and right at forks (M35). A bridge gap of 10 planks (bonds).
- **A mistake.** The unicorn hops to the stone she picked and finds something funny there (a puddle splash). Lumi counts back to the start of the jump. A wrong path leads to a short funny dead end, the babies shake off and trot back to the fork.

### 4.7 Growing Day and Flying Lessons (Blue chapter)

From roleplay idea 4 and maths ideas 5 and 9.

- **What she does.** A nurse corner by the lake: a height wall, a balance scale, a tailor's ribbon table. Babies come because they are growing, never because they are ill. When a baby reaches the young unicorn stage it gets little wings at the lake and can take Flying Lessons over a path of numbered clouds.
- **Learning.** Height in heart blocks, then a number line, then cm (M19, M25, M47). "Pip was 12. Now Pip is 15. How much did Pip grow?" (M15, M38). Balance with apples (M25). Flying: "Pip is on cloud seven. The star is on cloud twelve. How many hops?" (M19), later hops of 2, 5 and 10 (M26, M41 to M43). The baby tries first ("Four hops?") and Emma agrees or fixes it.
- **A mistake.** The marker goes to 14 for a baby that is 12: the baby stretches its neck, wobbles and giggles "I'm not that tall!" Too far on the clouds: a dizzy loop-the-loop, "Two clouds too far." Too short: a puff, "Nearly! Three more clouds."

### 4.8 Market Day and the Moon Clock (Indigo chapter)

From roleplay idea 8, maths ideas 7 and 8, reading idea 9, and wildcard 3 (Papa as customer, optional).

- **What she does.** Emma runs a stall in the night village. Customers are visiting unicorns with a shopping note and a purse. Coins only live in this scene: they go into the till and light the village lanterns, and nothing is bought or saved with them. The Moon Clock tower opens the market: "The market opens at six o'clock. Turn the moon to six."
- **Learning.** Shopping notes (R12 plurals, R27 phrases, R28 sentences). Coin values with dots on early coins (M32), paying exact amounts (M33), adding two prices (M13, M38), change in Book Two (M44). Currency is a setting (pence or euro cents). O'clock and half past (M34), always "half past", with Dutch one-hour-off errors logged.
- **A mistake.** Too many coins: "That's too many pennies. Here, two back for you." Wrong item: the customer tries it on ("A bun on my head?"). Wrong time: the babies come to the market in their nightcaps, "Too early for bed!", and Lumi points at the hour hand.
- **Papa mode (optional).** Tjitse can play the customer. A small adult card in his corner suggests an order at her current step.

### 4.9 Royal Wardrobe and the Royal Ball (chapter 7)

From roleplay idea 5, collecting idea 5 and reading idea 5.

- **What she does.** A sealed envelope with the Queen's gold seal. The invitation card stays pinned to the mirror while she dresses the invited babies. Matching babies chime as each item goes on. At the ball they dance and Lumi greets the herd.
- **Learning.** Invitations with names, colours and clothing (R27 to R29, tricky words come, wear, one), negation later ("Do not wear a hat."). Dance partners, odd and even (M06, M41). Dance steps, quarter and half turns (M35). Guests counted in 2s, 5s and 10s (M26).
- **A mistake.** No baby is turned away. A baby in the wrong colour dances anyway but does not shimmer. Lumi says "Pretty! The card said blue. Shall we find something blue?" A wrong dance step makes two babies bump softly and giggle.

### 4.10 Stable of Ten, roll call and the storybook (always open)

- **Stable of Ten** (roleplay 6, collecting 1, maths 4, world 2, reading 6). Rows of ten stalls, a real ten-frame. Name plaques, door decorations, her pins on the doors. Place value from her own herd (M02 to M28), hay bundles of ten, "Six more babies and Row Three is full." A full row gets a flag and a small party.
- **Roll call at goodnight** (collecting 1). A lantern shows a written name, not spoken. She finds that baby's door. Capped at 1 to 3 lanterns. A wrong door: the baby inside pokes its head out, yawns "Not me!" and goes back to sleep.
- **The chapter storybook** (reading idea 2, collecting idea 9). The Book of the Lost Colours gets 2 to 4 new pages per chapter, starring her own babies by name. On each page she reads her part (one word at R01, one sentence at R28, later a whole page) and makes it happen by dragging a baby. Elizabeth reads the rest. A misread is acted out literally ("Pip jumps on the bun!") and the page rewinds. The storybook is also the recap: she can reread any page, with no checks.

## 8. Clarity: how every moment's goal is spoken and shown

Queen Emma's Letters failed on clarity alone, so this has rules, not hopes.

- **Three goals on screen, one active.** Top: the bridge with its next grey arch and the grey land (the chapter goal). Beside the nest: the quest scroll with picture boxes, one glowing (the quest goal). In the scene: Lumi's moonbeam and a gold outline on what to touch now (the moment goal).
- **Every visit starts with a one-line recap and a picture.** "The Orchard is still pale. The scroll needs a cake." Never more than one sentence of story before she can act.
- **Every moment has one spoken goal.** Elizabeth says it, and a tap on Lumi repeats it. Repeats never give the answer.
- **The picture carries the goal, the print decides the detail.** She sees that a baby wants something, that a pin is lost, that a cousin wants a style. The words only decide which or where (reading brainstorm rule).
- **For reading, the deciding word is never spoken before she acts.** Sound dots, pure sounds on tap, hearts on tricky parts. After she acts, Elizabeth reads the whole text fluently.
- **For spelling, the target is always spoken first**, as Emma asked for in the Pippa feedback.
- **Free play is announced.** "Play time! Do what you like." No moonbeam is shown in free play, so she knows nothing is asked.
- **Choices are pictures, at most three.** Two eggs, three bells, three doors after a quest step. Never a menu.
- **First time, the ghost hand.** Each new gesture is shown once, on an object that is not the answer.
- **The v2 hint ladder** in every checked moment (idle 15 s, 10 s on reading).
- **Fixed places.** The moon (goodnight) is always top right, Lumi always bottom left, the scroll always beside the nest.
- **Goal words.** Warmth words for eggs ("warm", "still cold", "too hot"). "Look at the letters", never "read".
- **Watched, not assumed.** If she idles twice at the same step without touching anything, the log flags that step's line or picture for rework.

## 9. Biggest risks and the v3 first playable build

### 9.1 Risks

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| **Too much to build.** Seven places, quests, story pages and art for every growth stage. | Build the prologue and chapter 1 first. Make scenes, quests, cards and story pages data, composed from a scene kit. Build each later chapter only after Emma has played the one before it. |
| **The story thread gets lost** over irregular visits. | A one-line recap with a picture every visit, the bridge always on screen, chapters of 2 to 5 weeks, the storybook to reread. If she ignores the story, the core still works: eggs and care paint the land too. |
| **Quests repeat the Queen Emma's Letters failure.** | One active step, a picture for every step, the word only decides the detail. Test chapter 1 quests with Emma before writing more. |
| **A 5-minute visit feels thin, or a 45-minute visit feels like drill.** | An egg is always ready. The planner caps new items per strand per visit and offers free play first in long visits. |
| **Drops feel like wages** and lower her interest. | Drops are never announced as payment, never spent, and come from completion, not from accuracy. The world changes, not a number. |
| **Too easy** (Moonbeam Stables failure). | Content always at her own step with review and interleaving. The golden egg. Planned distractors from v2. First-try rewards are surprises only. |
| **The Grumble Cloud scares her**, or breaks "unicorns only". | Soft, round, red nose, silly sneezes, she comforts it. Test the sneeze scene with her first. Fallback: a faceless grey mist. |
| **Many unicorns slow the iPad** (engine research, main risk). | The 24-baby stress test in week one, before other scenes. The meadow shows at most 12 friends, the stable shows one row at a time. PixiJS fallback for the meadow only if needed. |
| **Saves clash between the iPad and a second device.** | One JSON save per child, synced to odin. Merge rules: the herd, items and logs are append-only by id, so they merge as a union. Ladder and memory boxes keep the newest change per item. Story progress keeps the furthest point. Export and import stay in the parent corner. |
| **Waiting for a new name's clip** disappoints her. | The tag goes on at once and her own sounds play. The first spoken name is a story moment. If the wait still bothers her, Tjitse can turn on the scheduled batch or decide on on-demand generation (naming.md D1). |
| **Credits run short** (about 40k a month, floor of 8k). | Name bank in tiers, chapter audio generated per chapter, the hash cache reuses v2 clips, 15 percent kept for regeneration. See section 10. |
| **Art quality and consistency.** | The engine research pipeline: one locked style reference, one rigged unicorn, colourable layers, a contact sheet check per batch. |

### 9.2 What the v3 first playable build contains

Goal: Emma plays the prologue and all of chapter 1 for about two weeks, on the iPad from the home screen, with her v2 herd, and the save on odin.

4. **Naming v3** in full: bells, the name garden with gift gems, the free gold bell, never-refuse checks, duplicates, the name queue in the save, a name bank of about 600 names, the blocklist by sound.
5. **Eggs.** Moon egg and snow egg across M02 to M17 with dots, dots plus numerals, and numerals. Golden egg choice. Shiny surprise.
6. **Places.** Meadow with care stations and baby wishes, Stable of Ten with plaques, Sneeze Hunt, Sparkle Salon (free play, visitor cards for words, phrases and patterns), roll call at goodnight.
7. **Story.** The prologue, chapter 1 (three quests), the quest scroll, colour drops painting the Rose Meadow, the bridge, the Red Festival, the first jewel, the first two storybook pages. The Orchard shown as the next land (not playable yet).
8. **Progress.** The three ladders with content for R01 to R20, S01 to S11 and M01 to M17, placement probe, Leitner boxes, Moon Egg Nights, herd parades at 10 and 20, Herd in the Sky.
9. **Collecting.** The album with pages of ten and baby pages, pin sashes, rose pins, Pin Post envelopes, letter pins.
10. **Parent corner.** Levels and accuracy per strand, Dutch pair errors, tried names, the name queue, step overrides, the optional soft goodnight timer, export and import.

**What to learn from Emma in those two weeks:** does she follow the scroll or only hatch eggs; does she understand the grey land filling up; does she like the Sneeze Hunt and the cloud; does she use the free gold bell, and which names does she build; how long are her visits really; does the Red Festival make her proud.

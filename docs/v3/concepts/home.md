# Concept: Moon Meadow Home

A v3 game concept for Unicorn Egg Nest, structure "home meadow that grows". Written 2026-10-12. Sources: the v2 demo and design (`docs/v2/design-unicorn-egg-nest.md`), the v3 research (`docs/v3/research/`: v2-audit, engagement, curriculum, naming, engine) and the 59 brainstorm ideas (`docs/v3/brainstorm/`). Step codes R01 to R45, S01 to S42 and M01 to M48 are the ladders in `research/curriculum.md`.

## Summary

Queen Lumi gives Emma an empty meadow at the foot of a hill and asks her to build a home for the baby unicorns. Over months, Emma's meadow grows a nest, stables, a bath pond, a salon, a kitchen, a school, a market and finally a castle with a throne room. The v2 loop stays the heartbeat: an egg is always waiting in the nest, she warms it with number stones, it hatches, she names it and spells the name, the baby says its first wish, and it moves into its own numbered stall. The herd never shrinks.

Levels are places. A new building or room opens when Emma learns something: every ladder step she passes puts a glowing foundation stone on the next building plot, and when a plot's stones are all lit, the builders come and Emma builds it herself with bricks, beads and letter gems. The herd size opens the other half of the levels: new stable rows, the parade field, titles and stars in the sky.

Role play is free in every building. Her own babies are toys with no wrong answers. The checked reading and maths arrive with a visitor or a wish: a cousin who wants a style, a party guest with a place card, a baby who writes "I want a bath". A mistake is a small funny event in the world, followed by the v2 hint ladder.

Naming is fixed for good: any safe name she builds is accepted at once, Elizabeth says it from a large pre-generated list, and a brand new name is spoken from the next visit on, after Tjitse's generator has made its clip.

## 1. The fantasy and Emma's role

**The story in one breath.** Queen Lumi lives in the moon. Each night she sends unicorn eggs down on moonbeams. The eggs need a warm home on the ground. Lumi gives Emma a meadow at the foot of a hill: "This meadow is yours, Emma. Will you make it a home for my unicorns?" Emma warms the eggs, names the babies, gives them a stall each, and builds everything they need. The hill at the back holds the ruins of an old castle under ivy. In the second half of the year Emma rebuilds it, room by room, and in the end the Queen comes down to crown her in the throne room she built.

**Emma's role.** Nest Keeper first, then everything a home needs: stable keeper, hairdresser, chef, party host, teacher, shopkeeper, nurse, builder. Her title grows with the home and is shown on her crown badge:

| Title | When |
|---|---|
| Nest Keeper | First visit |
| Stable Keeper | 10 babies |
| Meadow Keeper | 5 buildings open |
| Royal Nest Keeper | 50 babies |
| Keeper of the Castle | Castle Gate built |
| Princess of the Moon Meadow | Throne Room built |

**Cast.** Only unicorns are characters.

| Character | Voice | Speaks |
|---|---|---|
| Narrator | Elizabeth | Every instruction, hint, praise line, letter sound, word, name and number. The only voice for learning content. |
| Queen Lumi | Posh | Story lines only ("Hello, Emma!", "The builders are here!"). Her mouth moves on these lines. As in v2. |
| Baby unicorns | Flicker | Giggles and tiny fixed words ("Yay!", "Not me!", "Hello!"). Never a learning word. |
| Visiting cousins, grown unicorns, builders | Flicker giggles or nickers, no words | They hold printed cards. The card is the clue. |

Small meadow animals (a sleepy frog, birds) appear only as silent props in funny mistake reactions. If Tjitse reads "unicorns only" as no other animals at all, they are replaced by unicorn reactions with no loss.

**Why this fits Emma.** It keeps what she loved in v2 (eggs, naming, collecting, a herd that stays), adds the role play Tjitse asked for, and turns her pride in "more than 10" into something she sees every visit: rows of stalls with her names on the doors, a growing count, a sky full of stars. The home is hers. Nothing in it is ever taken away, and nothing in it suffers when she is gone.

## 2. Core loop and session shapes

### 2.1 The loops

**The heartbeat (about 3 minutes, repeatable without limit).** This is the v2 "one more egg" loop, extended so that every pass touches all three ladders.

1. **Egg (maths).** The nest always holds one egg. Its kind depends on her maths step. She warms it with stones, as in v2. About 45 s.
2. **Hatch.** Wobble, cracks, burst, fanfare. A shiny baby after a first try with no hint (never mentioned when it is not shiny). 10 s.
3. **Name (spelling).** Bells, the name garden or the gold bell. Elizabeth says the chosen name, Emma spells it with gems. Any safe name is accepted (section 5.3). 60 to 90 s.
4. **First wish (reading).** The newborn's first speech bubble, one word or phrase at her reading step ("hay", later "a red bow"). She brings the thing. 30 s.
5. **Moving in (number).** "Daisy is baby number nineteen! Which stall is hers?" She taps the door. Number recognition to 20, later tens and ones to 100. Once she is secure on the number range, the baby trots in by itself and only every third move-in is a task. 15 to 30 s.

Then the nest gets a new egg at once, and she chooses: another egg, a who's-waiting bubble, or free play.

**The visit loop.** Arrive, see who is waiting, play as long as she wants, optionally say goodnight. There is no fixed order and no fixed length.

**The long loop (weeks).** Passing a ladder step puts a badge on her Royal Sash and lights a foundation stone on the next plot. A plot with all stones lit gets a Building Day. The new building brings new role play, new visitors, a new item set and new kinds of reading and maths. In parallel the herd grows, and herd milestones open stable rows, the parade field, sky unicorns and titles.

### 2.2 Who's waiting

On arrival, and every time she returns to the meadow, up to three picture bubbles float over places in the home. Each bubble is a picture of who or what is waiting: a cousin's face over the salon, a cake over the tea garden, a bell over the school, a hammer over a plot that is ready to build. The egg in the nest is always there and does not need a bubble.

The planner picks the bubbles so that the strands she has practised least today come first. Bubbles never expire, never multiply and never nag. If she ignores them all and only hatches eggs, that is fine: the heartbeat carries all three ladders on its own.

## 3. Levels and how the ladders map onto them

### 3.1 What a level is

There are two kinds of level. Both are visible places, not numbers.

**Openings.** The home has building plots. Only the next plot is visible, as a soft outline with a picture sign of what it will be. The rest of the valley is under moon mist. Each plot has three to six foundation stones.

- When Emma passes a ladder step, a badge goes onto her **Royal Sash** (a letter pin for a reading or spelling step, a number charm for a maths step). The same moment lights one foundation stone on the current plot. The stone shows the badge's picture (the gem "sh", the stones 7 and 3). Elizabeth: "You know sh now! A new stone for the salon." This is information, not wages. Nothing is spent and nothing can be bought.
- Some plots also have a key step, because the building's activity needs that skill (the market needs coins, M32). A key plot waits for its key step even when its stones are lit. The building after it does not wait: the plot order skips ahead, so progress never stalls on one strand.
- When all stones are lit, a hammer bubble appears. Building Day starts when she taps it (section 4.10).
- Steps she has passed before the game started (her placement) do not light stones. She does not get ten buildings on day one.

**Herd milestones.** Counted babies, not learning steps. They open stable rows, the parade field, titles, sky unicorns and the Hundred Party. They reward the loop she already loves, and every egg in that loop is a maths, spelling and reading item.

| Herd size | What happens |
|---|---|
| 5 | Bath Pond (first Building Day, the tutorial for how building works) |
| Every 10 | A new stable row: she builds it (planks to fill a gap of ten), names the row (any name, never refused), cuts the ribbon. A new sky unicorn. |
| 15 | The camera arrives as a gift |
| 20 | Letter Lane |
| 30 | Parade Field and the first parade |
| 50 | Royal Nest Keeper title, the herd banner (a pictogram by colour) |
| 75 | Show Ring |
| 100 | The Hundred Party: a flag on the castle tower, a parade of the whole herd in tens |
| 150, 200, 300 | A parade medal, a new float, a sky picture |

Growth milestones are a third, smaller source: the first foal, the first young unicorn (opens the Growing Corner), the first grown unicorn (gets a job), the first two grown friends (opens the Friendship Garden).

### 3.4 Mastery under the hood

As in `research/curriculum.md` section 5, without changes: two layers (scaffold moves inside a step on a softened staircase, 3 first tries up and 2 misses down; the ladder moves on the step mastery rule), a Leitner box per item, decision time for fluency items with no visible timer, Dutch pairs held back until their contrast step, a hidden placement probe in the first two sessions, and a reopened step shown as a returning visitor, never as "you forgot".

## 4. Role play activities

Every activity follows four rules.

1. **Her own babies are free play.** Styling, feeding, bathing and dressing her own herd has no checks and no wrong answers. Items say their names when tapped, so English vocabulary grows on the side.
2. **A visitor or a wish brings the challenge.** A cousin with a card, a baby with a printed wish, a guest with a place card, an envelope with a name. Then it is clear when the game wants something.
3. **Only known tools.** Gems and slots for spelling, stones and cups for numbers, print with sound dots for reading (tap a letter for its sound, slide for all sounds, the deciding word is never spoken before she acts). New buildings bring new pictures, not new ways to answer.

## 8. Clarity: every moment's goal is spoken and shown

Queen Emma's Letters failed on clarity alone, so these rules are hard.

1. **One goal per screen, always a picture.** Cold cups that must turn warm. Empty gem slots and the baby. A wish bubble on a baby. An outlined gap in a wall. A tick box per card line.
2. **Lumi's moonbeam shows where to touch.** A gold outline on the target area. On choice steps it covers the whole area, never the answer.
3. **Elizabeth says the goal once, short.** "Make every cup warm, and the egg will hatch." Tapping Lumi or the replay button repeats the goal and never the answer.
4. **Reading steps never say the deciding word.** They say "Look at the letters." Tapping letters gives sounds. Elizabeth reads the text after she acts.
5. **Spelling steps always say the target first** ("Let's write Daisy. Listen: Daisy."), as Emma asked in the Pippa feedback.
6. **The ghost hand shows each new gesture once**, on an object that is not the answer.
7. **The hint ladder** from v2: 15 s idle repeats the goal, one miss or a second idle gives a focused hint, two misses show the answer and count as helped. Idle never goes past the focused hint.
8. **Who's-waiting bubbles show what she can do next** as pictures. At most three. On arrival Lumi names them: "Pip has a wish. And someone is waiting at the salon."
9. **The home has two zoom levels.** The meadow (herd, nest, care stations, the front of the stables) is the hub. A house button zooms out to the valley, which shows four areas (Meadow, Stable Yard, Village Lane, Castle Hill) and the sky. Tapping an area shows its buildings, at most six. Mist hides everything that is not open yet, except the next plot. Never a menu of twenty.
10. **Every visit to a building has a visible end.** The customer leaves happy, the party photo flies to the album, the bell rings for break. Then a big meadow arrow glows and Elizabeth says "Back to the meadow?" She can stay and play freely instead.
11. **A card or invitation stays pinned on screen** while she works on it (on the mirror, on the oven, on the gate).
12. **New buildings bring new pictures, not new controls.** Gems, stones and print with sound dots everywhere.
13. **One new thing per visit is announced.** Never two first-time explanations in a row.
14. **Watch, do not assume.** The game logs idle events per step. Two idles at the same step with no touch in between is a flag in the parent corner: that step's line or picture needs work.

## 9. Biggest risks and the first playable build

### 9.1 Risks

| Risk | What it looks like | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| She only hatches eggs | Buildings stay empty, later maths (coins, time) is never practised | The heartbeat carries all three strands on its own. Coin eggs carry money. Bubbles point to the least practised strand. Watch the logs. If eggs dominate, let a building's visitor bring its card to the meadow. |
| The stone-to-building link is too abstract | She does not feel that learning builds her home, so learning is not "felt" | The stone shows the picture of what she learned, Elizabeth says it, and the stone flies from her sash. Test this first with Emma. Fallback: herd milestones also add a stone. |
| Herd inflation | 300 babies, each one less special, and long naming queues | Favourites, baby pages, memory bubbles, hatch days, jobs and best friends keep old babies alive. Milestones grow (10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150). Duplicate names are allowed. |
| Too many places | Unclear goals, a map she wanders around | Two zoom levels, mist, at most six buildings per area, at most three bubbles, one opening at a time in a fixed order |
| Free play feels too easy | The Moonbeam Stables verdict | Checked moments are frequent: every heartbeat has three, every visitor has one or two. Free play is extra, not instead. |
| Performance with many unicorns | Stutter or reloads on her iPad | The engine research stress test in week one: 24 rigged babies in the meadow. Cap the meadow at 12 to 16 animated babies. Stalls show static heads. The sky is stars. PixiJS fallback for the meadow only. |
| Art volume and drift | Growth stages times outfits times poses | One locked style reference, one rig, colour by mask, accessories on anchor points. Outfits fit all stages by anchor, not by redraw. |
| Audio budget and review time | Too many clips for Tjitse to check | Section 10 budget, the hash cache, batch review on `review.html`, speech-to-text checks, finite sentence lists per step |
| Name queue delay | She wants to hear her new name now | Turn the wait into the "moon told me" moment. If it still bothers her, consider same-night generation on odin (decision D1). |
| Save loss or sync conflicts | Herd gone, or two devices disagree | IndexedDB first, `persist()`, home-screen install, sync to odin after every change. Babies are never deleted, so the herd merges by baby id (union). Item memory merges by the latest result per item. Export from the parent corner. |
| v2 import fails | The Mac's address or port has changed | Back up now. Keep the address and port fixed until Moving Day. Web Inspector fallback. |
| Placement wrong, school mismatch | Too easy or too hard in week one | Hidden probe, a start one step below, the parent corner override, and the questions to the school (phonics programme, phase, Year group, currency) |
| Dutch names sound English | Sanne said wrong | Decision D2 in naming.md, ear check of a Dutch tier |
| Long visits tire her | Mistakes rise late in a 45 minute visit | The hint ladder eases within a visit after two misses on a skill. Soft bedtime in the parent corner. No pressure to stay. |
| New activity types confuse her | Unicorn School "find the mistake" | Each new activity is tested with Emma as a short demo before its content is built. The first school lesson has obvious picture mistakes and a ghost-hand demo of both tools. |

## 10. Content and audio volume

### 10.1 Budget rules

- Starter plan: about 40,000 credits a month. Elizabeth (`eleven_v4`) and Posh cost 1 credit per character. Flicker costs 2.
- The generator refuses to run below 8,000 credits left. So plan **about 30,000 credits of batch content a month** and keep about 2,000 for the name queue (about 120 new names). Tjitse can lower the floor at the end of a month to spend the remainder on the next name tier.
- Every learning word, name, sentence and number is a pre-generated Elizabeth clip. Sentences come from finite lists per step, never assembled at runtime. Clips join only at sentence boundaries ("Tam!" + "What a lovely name!").
- The hash cache reuses every unchanged v2 clip (about 200) for free.
- Sound effects: chimes stay in code. ElevenLabs sound effects and music are a one-off batch per building. Their credit cost per generation must be checked on the plan before they are budgeted. They are not in the tables below.

# Brainstorm: maths hidden in unicorn play

Lens: every maths skill from the curriculum map (M01 to M48 in [../research/curriculum.md](../research/curriculum.md)) must be the natural way to make progress in something Emma does with her unicorns. No sums on cards. A wrong answer is a funny, kind thing that happens in the world, followed by a hint.

All ideas build on what she loved in v2: the egg that reacts to every stone, the hatch, naming, a herd that only grows, and items her babies keep (the pins). Lumi (Elizabeth) says every number, every word Emma learns from, and every hint. Baby unicorns use the Flicker voice for giggles and tiny words. Any new character (market customers, the herald) needs a voice from VOICES.md or a new casting round.

## 1. Eggs of Many Lands

**What she sees and does.** The egg stays the heart of the game. Each new land sends its own kind of egg, and each egg kind is a maths step.

- **Moon Meadow (moon cups, M09 to M12).** As in v2: a frame of 10 cups, some warm, she drags a stone to warm the rest.
- **Crystal Lake (frost egg, M14).** The egg arrives from the sun spring far too warm. All 10 cups glow. "This egg is too warm. Seven cups must stay warm." She picks a snow stone, and its dots put out cups one by one with a soft hiss.
- **Rainbow Forest (golden twenty-frame, M18 and M21).** Two frames side by side. A stone's dots fill the first frame, it clicks shut with a chime at ten, and the rest spill into the second frame. This shows "make ten" without saying it.
- **Star Mountain (hundred egg, M27 and M28).** Ten rows of ten cups. Stones now come as ten-sticks (a bar of ten dots) and single dots. "Twenty-four cups are cold." She drags two ten-sticks and four dots.

**What she hears.** "Every cup is warm! Four and six make ten." On the hundred egg the bond is said tens first: "Two tens and four. Twenty-four."

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Too many: extra dots bounce off red, the egg steams and gets red cheeks. "Too hot! Two too many." Too few: frost creeps over the egg and it shivers. "Brr! Three cups are still cold." On the hundred egg, 42 for 24 makes the egg wobble under a big heap of extra rows, and Lumi says "That is four tens. We need two tens." This is logged as a Dutch reversal.

**Reading.** Unchanged from v2: the baby is named and spelled right after the hatch. Each land's babies have names from that land's grapheme step.

**Collecting.** Each land hatches its own coat set (crystal manes, leaf manes, star manes). The Nest Book has a page per land with silhouettes of coats not yet hatched.

**Why it lasts.** It is the loop she already loves, and each land is a new look and a new challenge. The maths ladder runs from Reception to Year 1 summer inside one familiar action.

**Clarity risk.** The frost egg reverses the goal (put out, not warm). It needs its own first-time ghost hand and a clearly different look (blue, icy). Ten-sticks may look like one big stone at first.

**Cost: M.** The egg logic exists. Each egg kind is a variant with new art and lines.

## 2. Twin Eggs

**What she sees and does.** Now and then a twin egg arrives: one egg with a crack line down the middle and two tiny horns poking out. The cups are split into a left half and a right half. A mirror pond sits next to the nest. Whatever stone she puts on the left half, the pond copies onto the right half.

- **Doubles (M23).** "These twins need twelve warm cups. Pick a stone for one side." The 6-stone fills the left and its mirror copy fills the right.
- **Halves (M24).** Same action, but now the frame shows the total and she must find half. Later the egg shows only a number on a moon ribbon, with no cups visible.
- **Odd and even (M06, M41).** Some nights two eggs arrive with 9 and 10 cups. "Only one of these is a twin egg. Which one?" The odd egg has one lonely cup in the middle that no twin can share. If she picks it, the mirror pond tries to copy and one cup is left over, glowing on its own. "Oh! One cup has no twin."

**What she hears.** "Six and six make twelve! Double six." The twins giggle together in the Flicker voice.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Too small a stone: the left twin pokes its head out of the shell, looks at its cold sister, and taps the shell. "Not yet! Two more cups each side." Too big: both halves steam and both twins fan themselves with their hooves.

**Reading.** She names both twins. Lumi suggests rhyming pairs (Pip and Kip, Tam and Sam). Spelling the second twin is a one-gem change from the first: she pulls out the first gem and drops in a new one. This trains phoneme swapping, which is a core decoding skill. As always, any name she builds herself is accepted.

**Collecting.** A twins page in the Nest Book. Twins get matching ribbons. A full row of twins unlocks twin tricks in play time (they jump in step).

**Why it lasts.** Twins are rare and special, so they feel like a treat. Doubles grow from 1 + 1 to 10 + 10, then into the 2 times table (twin parades of pairs).

**Clarity risk.** The mirror pond copy must be very visible, or she will think one stone fills all the cups. The odd and even choice is abstract and needs the lonely-cup picture every time.

**Cost: S.** It reuses the egg, the stones and naming. New: the mirror animation and twin art.

## 3. Moonberry Picnic

**What she sees and does.** A picnic blanket in the meadow. Two to four of her own babies sit at plates. A basket of moonberries and a moon cake sit in the middle. "Picnic time! Make it fair. Every baby gets the same."

- **Sharing (M06, M30).** She drags berries onto plates. When the basket is empty, the babies look at each other's plates.
- **Grouping (M29, M30).** Later the question flips. "Each baby wants three berries. How many babies can come?" Babies wait at the edge of the blanket and she invites them one by one.
- **Cake (M31, M45).** She cuts the cake by swiping a line across it with her finger. The cake splits along her line. Round cakes, square cakes and star cakes. Halves for two babies, quarters for four.

**What she hears.** "Every plate has four berries. That's fair! Twelve shared by three is four each."

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Unequal plates: the baby with more looks at its plate, then at its friend, and pushes one berry across with its nose. "Tam has two more than Kip. Can you make it fair?" Nobody is sad. A wonky cake cut: the two slices are clearly different, and the baby with the small slice holds it up next to the big one and giggles. "One piece is bigger! Let's try again." The cake joins back together with a sparkle.

**Reading.** Babies are seated by name cards on the plates. She reads the cards ("Pip", "Tam") and drags each baby to its own plate. Later the invitation is a short sentence the Queen sends: "Pip and Tam can come", or "Bring a hat". Every baby in the sentence must come to the picnic, and in a hat if it says so.

**Collecting.** A recipe book of cakes. Each new cake shape or flavour is a sticker with a silhouette for the ones still missing. Picnic blankets and cups as sets. A picnic photo for the Nest Book after each picnic.

**Why it lasts.** The guests are always her own babies, so each picnic is different. Sharing grows from 2 plates and 6 berries to fractions of amounts in Year 2.

**Clarity risk.** "Fair" is an abstract word for a Dutch child learning English. The first picnic must show it with pictures: two plates side by side with equal stacks, then a sparkle. Swiping to cut must not be confused with brushing.

**Cost: M.** New scene, a cut gesture, and a sentence reader for the invitation.

## 4. The Royal Stables

**What she sees and does.** When the herd passes ten, the Queen gives Emma a stable block. Stalls are built in rows of ten. Each row is a street of ten doors on a hillside: Row One at the bottom, then Row Two, and so on. Every baby has a numbered stall with its name plate.

- **A new baby moves in (M27, M28).** "Pip's stall is number twenty-four." She taps the row (two tens up the hill), the row zooms in, and she taps door four. Pip trots in and the name plate clicks on.
- **Visible gaps.** Empty stalls show as open doors with straw. "Six more babies, and Row Two is full!"
- **Hay day (M27, M38).** The hay cart brings bales in bundles of ten tied with a ribbon, plus loose bales. "Every stall gets one bale. How many stalls are full?" She drags bundles and singles onto the cart.
- **Moving house (M28).** "Pip wants to live upstairs, ten more." She finds stall 34, directly above 24.

**What she hears.** Every stall number is said tens first by Lumi. Babies poke their heads out and say "Hello!" when she passes.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Wrong door: a sleepy baby already lives there and opens the door in its nightcap. "That's my stall!" Lumi says what she tapped: "That is forty-two. Four tens and two." Reversals are logged as Dutch slips. An empty wrong stall has a mouse in it that waves.

**Reading.** Bedtime moves into the stables on some nights. Babies run to the wrong stalls, and she reads the name plates to bring each one home ("Where is Pip?"). Name plates also feed the spaced review of names she spelled weeks ago.

**Collecting.** Each stall can be decorated: door colour, window box flowers, a hanging lantern. The stable is the most visible proof of her herd size. A full row of ten gets a flag on its roof.

**Why it lasts.** It grows with her herd up to 100, which is exactly the Year 1 summer and Year 2 autumn place value range. Every new baby is a new place value task that she actually wants to do.

**Clarity risk.** The zoom from hill to row to door must be smooth, or she loses track of where she is. Numbers above 50 need a herd that big, so the game may need visiting cousins to fill stalls for practice.

**Cost: L.** A persistent scrolling scene that grows, new art for many stalls, and stall decoration.

## 5. Flying Lessons

**What she sees and does.** When a baby reaches its "little wings" growth stage, it can learn to fly. Emma is the flying teacher. The sky shows a path of numbered clouds (0 to 20, later 0 to 100 in tens). The baby stands on one cloud. A star treat floats over another cloud.

- **Counting on and back (M19).** "Pip is on cloud seven. The star is on cloud twelve. How many hops?" She drags a wing card with a number of feathers onto Pip, and Pip hops cloud by cloud with a chime per hop.
- **The baby tries first (protege effect).** On later nights Pip says "Four hops?" in its tiny voice before Emma answers. She agrees with a tap, or gives a different card. If Pip was wrong, Emma fixed it, and Pip thanks her.
- **Big wings (M26, M41 to M43).** Older babies hop in 2s, 5s and then 10s. On a 0 to 100 sky, "Tam hops ten clouds at a time. Get Tam to sixty."

**What she hears.** "Five and seven. Twelve! You got Pip to the star." Pip catches the star and does a loop.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Too far: Pip zooms past, does a loop-the-loop and lands on cloud 14, dizzy and giggling. "Whee! Two clouds too far." Pip flies back if she picks the take-away card. Too short: Pip flaps hard and lands just short with a puff. "Nearly! Three more clouds."

**Reading.** Clouds can carry words instead of numbers on reading nights. "Fly to the cloud that says sun." The two strands can mix: "Fly to the red cloud" in Year 1, which is a short phrase to read.

**Collecting.** Wing badges per baby, one per flying level. A sky stamp book with a stamp for each land's sky (crystal sky, rainbow sky).

**Why it lasts.** Flying is a growth milestone for each baby, so every new baby brings it back. Teaching her own baby is a strong pull, and the number line grows to 100.

**Clarity risk.** Hops versus landing number: she may answer "twelve" (the target) instead of "five" (the hops). The first lesson should only have the target cloud, then move to "how many hops".

**Cost: M.** One new scene with a number line, a hop animation, and baby voice lines.

## 6. The Mane Salon

**What she sees and does.** A grooming salon with a big mirror, a bead tray and a shelf of hair pins, bows and flowers. One of her babies (or a visiting unicorn) sits in the chair.

- **Free styling.** She brushes and braids beads into the mane any way she likes. No right answer. This is the toy.
- **Style cards (M08).** A visitor brings a card that starts a pattern on a picture braid: pink bead, star, pink bead, star. "Can you finish my braid?" She fills the rest of the braid.
- **Harder patterns.** AB, then ABB and ABC, then growing patterns (one star, two stars, three stars).
- **Mirror styles (M47).** The left side of the mane is braided. She makes the right side match, as in a mirror.
- **Tiaras (M17, M47).** "A tiara with three triangles and one circle." Later: "A shape with four corners."

**What she hears.** "Pink, star, pink, star. Beautiful!" The visitor turns its head side to side in the mirror.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** The visitor looks in the mirror and frowns kindly at the odd bead. That bead glows and spins out of the braid. "Hmm. Pink, star, pink, pink? Let's look again." A lopsided mirror style makes the unicorn tilt its head to one side.

**Reading.** From the second reading stage the style card has words instead of pictures: "red bow", "pink pin", "big star". She reads the card to pick the right pin from the shelf. The pin then stays in that baby's mane.

**Collecting.** This is where the pins live. Hair pins, bows, beads and tiaras come in sets with silhouettes for missing ones. Each finished style is a photo in that baby's Nest Book page.

**Why it lasts.** Free styling is open play with no end, and she can restyle every baby in her herd. New sets arrive with each land and season (a snowflake set in winter).

**Clarity risk.** Low for free play. The pattern card must make clear that she continues it and does not copy it. The first card is just two beads long with the ghost hand showing one bead.

**Cost: S.** Mostly drag and drop on a mane, plus art for sets.

## 7. The Moon Market

**What she sees and does.** On market nights the unicorn village opens. Emma runs a stall with ribbons, treats and toys. Customers come one by one: visiting unicorns, a Fluffle, sometimes the Queen.

- **The shopping note.** Each customer holds a note: "a red bow", "a bun", later "two buns". She reads it and finds the item on her shelves.
- **Paying (M32, M33).** The item has a price tag. Early tags show the price as dots inside a coin. The customer opens a purse. "Seven pence, please." She takes coins from the purse into her till.
- **Change (M44).** Later the customer gives a 10p coin for a 7p bun. "Here you are!" She gives change from the till.
- **Currency setting.** Pence by default, euros and cents as a setting (Cyprus).

**What she hears.** "Five and two make seven. Seven pence! Thank you." The customer leaves with a happy hop.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Too many coins: the customer peers into the till. "Oh! That's too many pennies. Here, two back for you." The extra coins hop back into the purse. Too few: the price tag stays half coloured and the customer rummages for more. Wrong item: the customer tries on the wrong item and giggles ("A bun on my head?").

**Reading.** The shopping note is the reading. Words grow from single CVC words to phrases with colours and numbers to short sentences ("Can I have a cup, please?"), which bring in tricky words.

**Collecting.** No money is kept and there is nothing to buy, so there is no currency to inflate. The coins from each night light lanterns in the village, and the village gets brighter. Each customer leaves a postcard from their land, collected on a postcard page.

**Why it lasts.** Shopkeeper is a classic role play for her age. Customers change, and new lands bring new customers and stall goods.

**Clarity risk.** Coin value is not coin size, which confuses young children. The first coins show their value in dots. Paying versus giving change are two different roles and must not be mixed in one night at first.

**Cost: M.** New scene, customers and their voices, and a coin system.

## 8. The Moon Clock

**What she sees and does.** The castle courtyard has a big clock. The hour hand is a little moon and the minute hand is a sunbeam. The night's plan hangs on the wall as a picture strip: bath, supper, the ball, bed. Each picture has a clock face under it.

- **O'clock (M34).** To start the next activity she turns the clock. "Bath time is at six o'clock. Turn the moon to six." When the hands are right, the bell chimes and the babies run to the bath.
- **Half past (M34, M46).** Later the plan says half past. The moon hand sits between the numbers. Quarter past and to come in Year 2.
- **Days of the week (M34).** A week strip on the castle wall. "Today is Saturday. Saturday is market day!" New egg kinds or visitors can arrive on a set day, without anything being lost if she misses it.

**What she hears.** "Six o'clock! Bath time, babies!"

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** The babies go to the wrong thing. At seven o'clock instead of six they come to the bath with supper bowls, or in ball gowns. "Oops! It's seven o'clock. That's supper time." A time one hour off on half past is logged as a Dutch slip (Dutch "half drie" is 2:30).

**Reading.** The picture strip slowly turns into words: "bath", "bed", "ball", "tea". She reads the strip to know which time to set. Later the strip is a short timetable: "At six, bath."

**Collecting.** Every event at its time leaves a photo in a night diary. Clock faces for the castle (a star clock, a flower clock) come as rewards for lands.

**Why it lasts.** It gives every night a clear order and a clean end at bedtime, which the engagement research asks for. Time runs from o'clock to five minutes across Year 1 and Year 2.

**Clarity risk.** If the clock gates every activity, it becomes a chore. Use it for one or two moments per night, not all of them. Turning a hand with a finger must work well on the iPad.

**Cost: M.** A rotating hand control, a schedule strip, and many short scenes it can point to.

## 9. Growing Day

**What she sees and does.** Once a week is growing day in the nursery corner. Nobody is ill. The babies come for a check-up because they are growing. Emma wears a nurse's cap.

- **Height (M25).** She stacks heart blocks next to the baby until they reach the tip of its ear. The growth chart on the wall has last week's mark. "Pip grew two hearts!"
- **Weight (M25, M47).** A balance scale. The baby sits on one side, and she adds moon pebbles to the other side until it balances. Later the scale has a dial with numbers.
- **Capacity (M25).** Water bottles of different sizes for the drinking trough. "Which bottle holds more?"
- **The tailor.** After measuring, she picks a blanket or saddle that fits. Ribbons of different lengths lie on the table, and she chooses the one as long as the baby.
- **Centimetres (M47).** Later the heart blocks become a ruler.

**What she hears.** "Pip is six hearts tall. Last week Pip was four hearts. Pip grew two hearts!"

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** Too short a blanket: the baby's tail sticks out and it wiggles. "A bit short! Try a longer one." Too long: the baby trips on it and rolls over giggling. Too few pebbles: the baby's side of the scale bumps on the floor and it laughs.

**Reading.** The nurse's bag has labelled items ("comb", "brush", "cup", "bib"). The baby holds up a note with what it needs. Each baby's health card shows words like "tall" and "long" next to the numbers, and she fills it by dragging word stickers that she reads.

**Collecting.** Each baby's growth chart in the Nest Book, a blanket and saddle set with silhouettes, and a "grew this week" sticker. Growth stages tie into flying lessons (idea 5).

**Why it lasts.** It uses her own babies, so she sees them grow over weeks. This is the nursing role she loved in v1, with no illness.

**Clarity risk.** Weekly timing is hard for a 6-year-old to understand. The game should just invite her on growing day without explaining the week. Balance scales are hard to read for small differences, so early steps use big differences.

**Cost: M.** New scene, scale physics (simple), and per-baby growth data.

## 10. The Grand Parade

**What she sees and does.** When the herd reaches 10, 20, 30 and so on, the Queen holds a parade on the castle field. The field has hoof marks in a formation.

- **Rows (M29, M41 to M43).** "Rows of two!" She drags babies onto the hoof marks in pairs. Then the drum beats once per row as they march past the Queen, and Lumi counts with the drum: "Two, four, six, eight..." Later rows of 5 and 10.
- **Leftovers.** With 23 babies in rows of 5, three babies are left. They ride on the parade float with the band.
- **Teams (M03, M15).** The parade has colour teams. "The pink team has seven and the blue team has four. Make the teams the same." She moves babies between teams.
- **The herald's banner (M48).** After the parade, the herald hangs a banner that is a pictogram of her herd by coat colour. She sticks one small unicorn picture per baby in the right column. The tallest column wins the Parade Ribbon for that colour.

**What she hears.** "Twenty unicorns marched for the Queen!" The Queen (Posh voice) says thank you to the Nest Keeper.

**Wrong answers in the fiction.** An uneven row makes the march wobble. One row has three babies who bump into each other and giggle. "This row has three. Rows of two!" Unequal teams: the band plays out of step until the teams match.

**Reading.** Each baby holds up its name sign as it marches. In "show my herd" mode Emma leads Papa through the parade and taps each sign. Lumi says the name only after a short pause, so she can read it aloud first.

**Collecting.** Parade medals for herd sizes (10, 20, 50, 100), float decorations, and a banner for each parade in the Nest Book. This uses her pride in "more than 10 eggs" directly.

**Why it lasts.** It is triggered by herd milestones, which keep coming as long as she plays. It is also the "something to show Papa" moment from the engagement research.

**Clarity risk.** Dragging 20 or more babies is slow. Later formations need a "march in" button that fills rows automatically, so she only fixes the wrong rows. The pictogram may feel like a worksheet unless it is a real banner in the scene.

**Cost: L.** Many babies on screen at once, which is the performance risk named in the engine research.

## Notes across ideas

- **What carries the most maths for the least build:** ideas 1, 2 and 6 reuse the v2 egg and drag systems. Ideas 4 and 10 are the most work but turn her herd size into maths she cares about.
- **Dutch risks** are handled where they occur: tens first in ideas 1 and 4, half past in idea 8, 13 and 30 kept apart until step M20.
- **No pressure:** nothing expires, no baby is ever sad or ill, no coins are kept, and no activity is timed.
- **Placement:** each idea maps to M steps, so the maths ladder decides which activity and which difficulty appears on a given night.

# Brainstorm: reading hidden in play

Lens for v3 of Unicorn Egg Nest: where does Emma read and spell inside the role play with her herd? Written 2026-10-12. Sources: [v2-audit](../research/v2-audit.md), [engagement](../research/engagement.md), [curriculum](../research/curriculum.md), [naming](../research/naming.md).

## Recommendation

Build four of these into the v3 core: **Baby Talk** (1), **The Magpie's Treasure Trail** (3), **Stable Village** (6) and **The Bedtime Storybook** (2). Together they cover the reading ladder from R01 (single CVC words) to R45 (a full page), they sit on what Emma already loved (caring for babies, hide and seek, collecting pins, a herd that keeps growing), and Stable Village removes the cap of 12 babies. The other five are good later activities: one per new land, released in small doses.

All nine keep the core loop (warm an egg, hatch, name, spell, add to the herd). None replaces it.

## Rules that apply to every idea

- **Read to act.** Reading is checked by what she does in the world: give an item, put a baby somewhere, open a door. Never by tapping an answer button.
- **The goal is visible before the reading.** She sees that a baby wants something, that a pin is missing or that an invitation arrived. The written words only decide *which* or *where*. This is the fix for Queen Emma's Letters, where the reading had to happen before she knew what the game was about.
- **For reading, the deciding word is never spoken before she acts.** Decodable words have sound dots. A tap or a slide plays the pure letter sounds from the phonics library. Tricky words have a heart over the tricky part. A tricky word that does not decide the action is spoken whole on tap.
- **For spelling, Elizabeth says the target first** (names, plaques, labels), as Emma asked for in the Pippa feedback.
- **After the act, Elizabeth reads the whole text fluently** while the words light up. This models the sentence she just read.
- **Elizabeth is the only voice for learning content.** Babies squeak and giggle (Flicker). A baby's speech bubble is shown as print, and Elizabeth reads it aloud after the act ("Pip says: I want a bath!"). A baby never says a learning word, wrong or right.
- **A wrong act is a funny event in the world.** The baby plays along with the wrong thing, shakes its head and giggles. The deciding word glows and its sound dots appear. Then the v2 hint ladder runs: focused hint after one miss, the answer shown after two misses (logged as helped).
- **Nothing a wrong act produces is a reward.** Otherwise random tapping pays, as it did in Moonbeam Stables.
- **Names are never refused.** Every naming moment follows [naming.md](../research/naming.md).
- **Distractors follow the planners** from v2: one sound apart, no Dutch pairs before the contrast steps R11, R20 and S06.

## 1. Baby Talk

**What she sees and does.** Any baby in the meadow can hop over with a speech bubble. The bubble holds print, from one word ("bath") up to two sentences ("I am hot. Can I go in the pond?"). The meadow has care stations: a bath tub, a pond, a swing, a hay rack, a brush stand, a hat box, a nap cushion. She reads the bubble and takes the baby to the right station. The care animation plays (bubbles in the bath, the swing going high), the baby gets a heart, and Elizabeth reads the bubble aloud.

**Wrong act.** She puts the baby on the swing when it asked for the bath. The baby swings once, looks puzzled, shakes its head, hops off and giggles. "bath" glows with its sound dots.

- **Role play:** the daily care of her babies. This gives the v2 brush, treats and cuddle tools a reason to differ.
- **Reading:** R01 to R12 single words, then R27 phrases ("a red bow"), R28 one sentence with one action, R29 two sentences with tricky words (I, want, my, me, the, is, was). Later wishes refer to the baby's own history ("I want my red bow."), which is early comprehension.
- **Maths:** some wishes carry a number ("I want 3 apples.", "Give me 2 more."). She counts into the bowl. Later wishes do take away ("I had 6. I ate 2.").
- **Collecting:** every granted wish leaves a sticker on that baby's Nest Book page, as the pins did. Each baby gets a "favourite things" list that grows.
- **Longevity:** wishes come from the whole herd, so older babies bring older words back (spaced review). New stations arrive with each land.
- **Clarity risk:** with seven stations she may drag the baby around at random. After one miss the baby waits for her to slide along the deciding word before it moves again.
- **Build cost:** M.

## 2. The Bedtime Storybook

**What she sees and does.** At bedtime the herd snuggles onto the moss bed and Emma sits in the big storybook chair. A picture book opens. Its stories star her own babies by name. Each page has a picture that is not finished and a sentence that says what happens: "Pip gets in the boat." She makes it happen by dragging Pip into the boat. The page comes alive, the babies on the bed react (ears up, giggles, a big yawn), and Elizabeth reads the page. When the book ends, the babies fall asleep one by one. The night is over.

**Wrong act.** Pip lands in the bin instead of the boat. The picture plays it out, the babies on the bed roll with laughter, and the page rewinds with "boat" glowing.

- **Role play:** a mother reading her children to sleep. It is also the clean end of a night (engagement principle 15).
- **Reading:** first books have two or three words per page (R06 "the cat"). Pages grow to one sentence (R28), two sentences (R29), 3 to 5 sentences (R41) and a full page of 40 to 80 words (R45). After some books the babies muddle the pictures and Emma puts 3 or 4 panels back in order (R42 sequencing). Later pages ask for inference acted out: "Pip is sad. She lost her bell." She gives Pip the bell (R43).
- **Maths:** story maths on the page ("Five ducks swim. Two fly off."). She makes it happen and the ducks that are left wait to be counted into the nest. Ordinal words (first, next, last) in the sequencing step.
- **Collecting:** each finished book goes on the nursery bookshelf, with empty spaces for books still to come. She can reread any book. A rereading has no checks and is fluency practice.
- **Longevity:** a new book every one or two weeks. Her own babies star in them, so a new baby makes the stories change.
- **Clarity risk:** long text can repeat the Queen Emma's Letters failure. Keep one sentence and one act per page until R41. The moonbeam always shows where the act happens.
- **Build cost:** L. Pages need art. Compose them from the scene kit (meadow props, babies, items) so a new book is mostly data.

## 3. The Magpie's Treasure Trail

**What she sees and does.** Mopsy, a cheeky magpie (our own character), flies through the meadow and grabs shiny things off the babies: a pin, a bell, a bow. A mist rolls in while Mopsy hides them. Mopsy leaves a clue note on the fence: "It is in the pot." The scene has six hiding places that look alike in kind: a pot, a pan, a pit, a log, a hat, a tub. Emma taps the place from the note. The pin flies back to its baby, who wears it, and it presses onto the baby's Nest Book page.

**Wrong act.** She lifts the pan. A sleepy frog blinks at her, says "ribbit" and goes back to sleep. Mopsy cackles on a branch. The note's deciding word glows.

- **Role play:** detective and finder. She gets her babies' things back for them.
- **Reading:** object words first (R01 to R10). Then position words and phrases (R27: "under the big log", "on top of the hat"). Then two-clue notes with negation (R29: "It is not in the pot. It is in the pan."). Later the Dutch twin "in" and "on" gets its own contrast.
- **Maths:** position and direction (M35: left, right, above, below), steps along a path ("Go 3 steps to the tree."), and ordinal clues ("the second bush").
- **Collecting:** pin, bell and bow sets per land, each with silhouettes for the missing ones. A full set means the babies can hold a parade.
- **Longevity:** every land has new hiding places and new sets. Notes grow with the ladder. Mopsy can return as a running character the babies love to tease.
- **Clarity risk:** she must understand that the note says where the pin is. The first trail shows Mopsy dropping a pin into the pot, then the mist, then the note "pot". The ghost hand shows the slide along the note once.
- **Build cost:** M.

## 4. The Moon Café: recipe cards and orders

**What she sees and does.** A kitchen in the meadow with labelled jars, a mixing bowl and a moon oven. A recipe card hangs on the wall: "3 figs. 2 nuts. 1 egg." She reads each line, takes the right jar and drops the right number into the bowl. The line gets a tick. Stir, oven, a puff of glitter, and out comes a treat with a magic effect: a rainbow cupcake turns a baby's mane rainbow for the night, a fizzy bun makes a baby float. Later babies come to the counter with order tickets: "Pip wants a bun and jam."

**Wrong act.** The wrong ingredient or the wrong number turns the treat into a green fizzing pie. The baby takes a bite, hiccups bubbles and giggles. The line on the card wiggles.

- **Role play:** baker and café owner, feeding the herd.
- **Reading:** ingredient words, jar labels that differ by one sound (jam, ham; bun, bin), number words, then instruction verbs and sequence words ("First mix it. Then put it in the pot."), which is R28 to R42.
- **Maths:** counting to 10 and then to 20, adding on ("2 more nuts"), doubling a recipe for two babies (M23), halving a cake (M24, M31), capacity with cups and spoons (M25).
- **Collecting:** a recipe book with a page per recipe, and a list of treat effects she has seen. Each baby gets a favourite treat.
- **Longevity:** seasonal recipes (a winter pudding, a Cyprus summer ice lolly). The maths grows with the same cards.
- **Clarity risk:** a recipe has several steps. Show one highlighted line at a time and tick each line when done.
- **Build cost:** M.

## 5. The Royal Ball: invitations and dress-up

**What she sees and does.** A sealed envelope arrives with the Queen's gold seal, showing a picture of a ballroom. Emma breaks the seal. The card says: "Pip and Tam, come to the ball. Wear a red bow." She chooses the invited babies at the gate, takes them to the wardrobe and dresses them. At the ball the babies dance, and the Queen (Posh voice) admires them.

**Wrong act.** A baby that was not invited skips to the gate, sees its name is not on the card held up by the doorman, and sits down to wait for next time with a happy wave. A wrong outfit: the baby twirls in front of the mirror, the card in the mirror frame glows at "red bow", and the baby giggles.

- **Role play:** princess, dressing up, getting ready for a party, the Queen's court. This is Tjitse's "more things to do with the unicorns".
- **Reading:** reading names from her herd and cousin names (R38 pseudo-words, the same skill as the phonics screening check), colour and clothing words, adjectives (big, small), tricky words (come, to, the, said, they), later negation ("Do not wear a hat.").
- **Maths:** chairs at the royal table ("6 guests, 4 chairs"), dance pairs (odd and even, groups of 2), ribbon patterns on gowns (M08).
- **Collecting:** a wardrobe with silhouettes (bows, crowns, capes, saddles, slippers). Each baby keeps its outfit. A ball photo goes into the Nest Book.
- **Longevity:** themed balls through the year (Snow Ball, Summer Beach party). Bigger guest lists as the herd grows.
- **Clarity risk:** free dress-up can distract from the card. Pin the card to the mirror. With a herd of 30 or more, show only 4 to 6 babies at the gate.
- **Build cost:** L. Dress-up needs layered art for every baby shape and growth stage.

## 6. Stable Village: plaques, labels and the feeding board

**What she sees and does.** When the meadow fence is full, babies move into the Stable Village, a row of stables with numbered doors. For each new stable, Elizabeth says the baby's name and Emma spells it onto the name plaque with gems. She also labels the feed bins (hay, oats, nuts). Each morning a feeding board says "Pip: 3 oats. Tam: 2 nuts." She fills the buckets. At bedtime the babies trot home and she reads which door is whose.

**Wrong act.** A baby goes into the wrong stable, sees a strange teddy in the bed, peeks out with a confused look and trots back. The plaque of the right door shimmers only after she slides along it.

- **Role play:** stable owner. Feeding, mucking out, painting doors, planting flower boxes.
- **Reading:** she reads what she wrote herself, which makes her own spelling meaningful. Herd names at scale (dozens of pseudo-words), labels, the feeding board (names, nouns, numerals), later yard signs ("Shut the gate.").
- **Spelling:** plaques and labels, S02 to S14, with gift gems for names she cannot spell yet.
- **Maths:** stable numbers up to 100. Stable 24 sits between 23 and 25, which trains the Dutch "four and twenty" reversal (M20, M27). Rows of 5 and 10 stables for counting in 5s and 10s and arrays (M26, M29). Feeding board totals.
- **Collecting:** the village grows with every baby. A big herd count at the gate ("You have 34 unicorns!"). Stable decorations.
- **Longevity:** it is the answer to the 12-baby cap and grows for as long as she hatches eggs.
- **Clarity risk:** many doors. Show only the row of 4 to 6 doors around the baby, with the moonbeam on the baby that needs a home.
- **Build cost:** L. A scrolling village scene and a save format with stable positions.

## 7. Unicorn School: Emma is the teacher

**What she sees and does.** Older babies sit at little desks and Emma is the teacher. On the board is a picture of a cat and the baby's attempt in gems: c, o, t. Emma swaps the wrong gem and slides to hear it. Or the board says "pen" and the baby holds up a picture of a pin; she gives the baby the right picture. The baby beams and gets a gold star. A full star chart means the baby graduates with a tiny cap, a new growth stage.

**Wrong act.** If Emma swaps the wrong gem, the baby tilts its head, the picture on the board does not match the slide (the slider plays the sounds she built), and the class giggles kindly.

- **Role play:** playing school, which she knows from her own day. The babies raise hooves, the bell rings, there is a playground break.
- **Reading and spelling:** spotting and fixing errors. This fits the Dutch contrast steps well once they are passed (cat, cut; bed, bet; ship, sheep) and R36 "which sound?" (cow, snow). Tricky words with the heart part on the board.
- **Maths:** a baby puts 6 stars on the 7 card and Emma adds one. A baby's bead frame shows a wrong bond to 10 and she fixes it.
- **Collecting:** a report card and graduation photo per baby. The Nest Book shows which letters each baby "knows", which mirrors what Emma knows.
- **Longevity:** the babies' mistakes are her own review items at growing gaps (spaced review), so the content is endless and personal.
- **Clarity risk:** fixing a mistake is a harder idea than giving an item. Start with the picture swap. Gem fixing comes later. It must feel like a classroom, not a quiz screen.
- **Build cost:** M.

## 8. Snapshot Album: captions for Papa

**What she sees and does.** In play time she has a camera. She snaps a baby doing something (in the bath, on the swing, wearing a crown). The photo drops into her album with an empty caption strip and a few word tiles. She builds the caption: "Pip is in the tub." The slider reads her caption back with Elizabeth's word clips. In "show Papa" mode she turns the pages and Elizabeth reads each caption, or Emma reads them to Papa herself.

**Wrong act.** She picks "tab" for "tub". A doodle appears on the photo to match: a little tag drawn on Pip, who giggles. She swaps the tile.

- **Role play:** family photographer and scrapbook maker. It is the "something to show Papa" principle.
- **Reading and writing:** choosing between tiles one sound apart, word order, capital letter and full stop (S11, S18), tricky words. Captions start with one blank and grow to a whole sentence.
- **Maths:** album pages of 10 photos, so "2 full pages and 4 more is 24 photos" (tens and ones, M27). Counting babies in a group photo.
- **Collecting:** photos, frames and album pages without limit.
- **Longevity:** every new baby, outfit and land gives new photos. The album becomes a record of her months with the herd.
- **Clarity risk:** tile choice must be obvious. First captions have one empty tile and two choices.
- **Build cost:** M. Needs a word clip for every caption word.

## 9. Market Day: shopping list and price tags

**What she sees and does.** Once a week there is a market in the village. The babies want to build something: a swing, a slide, a flower bed. A plan picture shows the finished thing. Lumi gives a purse with coins and a shopping list in words: "rope, 2 logs, a bell". Stalls have word signs and price tags, kept by our own characters (a hedgehog grocer, a badger carpenter). Emma reads the list, finds the stall by its sign and pays. Back home the babies build it, and it stays in the meadow.

**Wrong act.** The hedgehog wraps the wrong item. At home the list line stays empty and the babies hold the item up, puzzled. She can return it and gets the coins back. Nothing is lost.

- **Role play:** going shopping, choosing, paying.
- **Reading:** list nouns, stall signs, adjectives, later short notes ("We need 2 big logs.").
- **Maths:** recognising coins, paying with 1, 2, 5 and 10 (M32, M33), comparing prices, change later (M44). Currency is a setting (euros or pounds).
- **Collecting:** what she builds stays in the meadow or the Stable Village, so the world fills with her own things.
- **Longevity:** a project per land and per season.
- **No currency economy:** the purse is filled for each trip and empty after it. She never earns, saves or loses money. This keeps engagement principle 7 (no currency to spend).
- **Clarity risk:** several steps in a row. The plan picture must not show the parts as silhouettes, or she matches pictures instead of reading the list.
- **Build cost:** L.

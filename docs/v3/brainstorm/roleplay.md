# Brainstorm: role play and care

Lens: everything Emma can do with her unicorns beyond hatch, name, wish, brush and bedtime. Written 2026-10-12 for the v3 plan. Sources: `docs/v3/research/` (v2-audit, engagement, curriculum, naming, engine) and `docs/v2/design-unicorn-egg-nest.md`. Step codes (R, S, M) refer to the ladders in `docs/v3/research/curriculum.md`.

## Recommendation

Demo three activities first: **Sparkle Salon**, **Moonberry Kitchen and Tea Parties** and **Unicorn School**. Build **Stable Homes** as part of the core game in any case, because the herd needs somewhere to live past 12 babies.

The Salon carries the pins and bows she loved and adds patterns and phrase reading. The Kitchen is the richest maths room (sharing, halves, arrays, money later). The School is the only place where Emma's own Dutch slips can be practised without her ever being wrong, because the baby makes them and she fixes them.

## Three rules that hold for every activity

1. **Her own herd is free play. A visitor brings the challenge.** When Emma styles, feeds or dresses her own babies, nothing is checked and nothing is wrong. Checked work arrives with a visitor or a card: a cousin who wants a style, a guest with a recipe, an invitation from the Queen. This keeps the toy a toy, and it makes clear when the game wants something. Her own babies still bring wishes, as in v2.
2. **Three tools only.** Every activity uses the gadgets she already knows from v2: the gem tray and slots for spelling, the stone basket for numbers, and the word cloud with sound dots for reading. New rooms bring new pictures, never a new way to answer.
3. **A miss is a funny moment in the story.** A wrong answer makes the world react (a giggle, a wobble, a bead that rolls away), then the item goes back and Lumi gives the v2 hint ladder. No buzzer, no lost item, no sad baby.

Each night offers two or three activity doors after the egg (principle 9 in engagement.md: small choices). Anything she finishes stays: photos, stickers, rosettes and items go to the baby's page in the Nest Book.

## 1. Sparkle Salon

**What she sees and does.** A salon corner in the meadow: a big round mirror, a little stool, a shelf of bead jars, a ribbon rack and a pin box. A baby sits on the stool facing the mirror. A visiting cousin trots in holding a style card, rings the counter bell, and waits. Emma reads the card, threads the beads into the braid, ties the bow and clips the pin. She taps the camera and the photo flies into the Nest Book.

**Role play.** Hairdresser. She rings the "next please" bell, spins the stool, sprays sparkle mist, holds up the hand mirror for the customer.

**Reading.**
- Early steps (R01 to R05): the card shows one word, like "bow", "pin" or "bell". She finds the item on the shelf.
- R27: colour and size phrases ("a red bow", "a big pink bow"). Colour words are learned here, because every item exists in several colours.
- R28 and R29: two short sentences ("Put three stars in my mane. Make my tail blue."). Each line gets a tick when done.
- Lumi only says "Look at the card." Tapping a word plays its sounds, never the whole word.

**Maths.**
- M08 patterns: the card shows a bead pattern with a gap (red, blue, red, blue, ?). She continues the braid. Later ABB, ABC and growing patterns.
- M03 the same, then M23 doubles: "Make both braids the same." The left braid has 4 beads, she adds 4 to the right. Then "8 beads, the same on each side" (M24 halves).
- M27 and M28: big styles use bead strings of ten ("26 sparkles": two strings and six loose).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** The customer looks in the mirror, sees a green bow, tilts its head and giggles: "Green? Hee hee!" The bow floats back to the rack. A wrong bead in a pattern clicks in, then the braid gives a little twirl and that bead glows until she swaps it.

**Collecting.** A Salon Album with a photo of every style. Bow, ribbon and pin sets on a page with grey silhouettes of the ones still missing. A pin box with 12 pin shapes per land (star, moon, heart, shell in the beach land).

**Why it lasts.** Each land sends new customers with new card types. Seasonal styles (snowflake beads in winter, shells in summer). The pattern ladder grows from AB to growing patterns. The album fills with her own work.

**Clarity risk.** A two-line card at R28 can be half done. She may finish the bow and wait. Each line on the card gets its own tick box that fills when done, and the customer points at the unticked line.

**Build cost.** M. Bead, bow and pin art in colours, a braid that takes beads, one customer per land.

**Fit with feedback.** She loved collecting the pins. Here pins become a real item set she can fill, and every pin clips onto a baby she named.

## 2. Moonberry Kitchen and Tea Parties

**What she sees and does.** A small kitchen hut: a stove, a big mixing bowl, a pantry with moonberries, carrots, apples and buns, a cake stand. Outside, a long table with chairs and plates. Two parts:
- **Cooking.** Lumi's cookbook opens on a recipe card: a picture of the dish, numbers and words. She drops the right things in the bowl, stirs with a big spoon and the dish pops out of the oven.
- **Tea party.** Guests arrive. She seats them by their place cards, shares out the food and pours the tea.

**Role play.** Chef and tea party host. Apron, oven glove, a teapot she tilts, cups that clink.

**Reading.**
- Recipe words at her level (jam, bun, egg, nut, then cheese, cake, sweets).
- Place cards with the names of her own babies and visiting cousins. She reads each card and drags the right baby to its seat. This is review of the names she gave (spaced review, principle 12) and pseudo-word reading (R38).
- Later, recipe steps as sentences: "Mix the jam and the buns." (R28)

**Maths.**
- Count out to 10, then 20 (M02, M07).
- Missing part: "7 berries. 3 are in the bowl already." (M09, M12)
- Sharing: "12 berries. Every friend gets the same." She deals onto plates (M06, M30).
- Halves and quarters: cut the cake with the knife line (M24, M31, M45).
- Arrays: the cupcake tray of 2 rows of 5 (M29, M41).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.**
- Too many berries: the extra ones roll off the table with a "bloop" and hang above the bowl, so she can see how many too many.
- Too few: the cake comes out flat and tiny. The baby nibbles it and says "More?" (baby voice).
- Unequal plates: the table tips like a see-saw toward the fuller plate, and the cups slide.
- Wrong seat: the baby hops onto the chair, looks at the card, shakes its mane, "Not me!", and hops down.

**Collecting.** A recipe book with a sticker for each dish she has cooked. Tea set pieces (teapot, cups, cake stand) in sets per land. A party photo for each party.

**Why it lasts.** The recipe book has 40 or more dishes spread over the lands. Every season has a party (winter cocoa, summer ice cream, birthday cakes from idea 10). The maths in food climbs naturally from counting to fractions and times tables.

**Clarity risk.** Cooking and serving are two goals. Keep them on two screens: cook first, then the party. Sharing needs a ghost-hand demo the first time.

**Build cost.** M. Food art is small and reusable. The tipping table and the cake cut need care.

**Fit with feedback.** Fluffle Nursery feeding and the "exact plates" were liked in v2. This brings that nursing and feeding to her own herd, and the place cards use the names she chose.

## 3. Unicorn School

**What she sees and does.** A schoolroom in a big tree. Emma is the teacher: a bell, a long star pointer and a gold star stamp. One to three babies sit at little desks with slates. On each slate a baby has tried a task: a word built from gems under a picture, or stars counted with a number. Emma checks each slate. If it is right she stamps the gold star. If it is wrong she uses the magic eraser on the wrong gem or stone and puts the right one in.

**Role play.** Teacher. She rings the bell, calls the register (taps each name card, the baby says "Here!"), hands out gold stars, sends them out to play.

**Reading and spelling.**
- The baby's slate plays back what the baby built, in Elizabeth's letter sounds, like the v2 slider. Emma hears "b, e, t" under a picture of a bed and fixes the t.
- This is the home of the Dutch steps: R11 and S06 (a/e/u, final d/t, g/k, b/p), R20 (ee, oo, ie, ui read the Dutch way) and R36 (which sound fits, cow or snow). The baby makes the mistake that Emma is likely to make, so she practises spotting it with no risk to herself.
- The register names are her own babies' names.

**Maths.**
- Counting: the baby touches the stars and skips one. Emma taps the skipped star.
- M20 Dutch number order: Elizabeth says "twenty-four" and the baby has written 42. Emma swaps the two stones.
- Bonds and facts: the baby's slate says 8 and 5 make 12. Emma fixes it with the ten-frame.

**Wrong answer in the fiction.**
- If she stamps a wrong slate, the baby proudly carries it to Lumi at the end of the lesson. Lumi says "Let's listen together", the slate plays back, the baby says "Oops!" and they fix it together.
- If she changes a slate that was right, the slate plays her version and the baby tilts its head: "Hmm?" Lumi then says the target once.
- About a third of the slates are right, so she must really check and cannot just erase everything.

**Collecting.** Each baby earns a skill badge for each letter group or number step on its Nest Book page. A graduation cap when a baby finishes a land's school. A class photo per term.

**Why it lasts.** Every new grapheme or maths step enters the classroom. New babies arrive needing lessons, so a bigger herd means more school. The babies' badges mirror her own progress, so principle 18 ("she sees she is getting better") is shown through her babies.

**Clarity risk.** "Find the mistake" is abstract for a 6-year-old. Start with mistakes that are obvious in the picture (a cat picture, a slate that says "dog") and a ghost-hand demo of both tools. Two big tools only: the stamp and the eraser.

**Build cost.** M. Reuses the gem tray, stones and slider. The planner must create believable mistakes per step.

**Fit with feedback.** Nursing and caring were her v1 favourite. Teaching her babies is caring with a purpose, and it is role play that only she controls.

## 4. Growing Day at the Nurse's Corner

**What she sees and does.** A bright little corner with a height wall (painted stripes with numbers and hoof marks), a balance scale, a basket of plasters with pictures and a shelf of "moon drop" bottles. She picks a baby. It stands at the wall, she slides the marker to the top of its horn and reads the number. She weighs it on the balance with apples. When a baby has had enough hearts and check-ups, the check-up ends with a growth moment like a hatch: the baby glows and grows to the next stage.

This is not a sick bay. No baby is ever ill or hurt because of her. Plasters are for a happy scrape from play ("I did a big jump!").

**Role play.** Nurse and doctor. Stethoscope that plays a heartbeat, a tiny torch for the ears, a lollipop at the end.

**Reading.**
- Plaster choice: "the plaster with a star", "a red plaster with a moon" (R27).
- Moon drop labels: "Give Tam two drops." Names, number words and later sentences (R28).

**Maths.**
- Read a height on a number line to 20, then 100, then in cm (M19, M28, M47).
- "Pip was 12. Now Pip is 15. How much did Pip grow?" (difference, M15, M38).
- Balance: how many apples weigh the same as Pip (M25), later reading a scale dial (M47).
- A growth chart per baby as bars, and a herd chart of who is tallest (M48).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** The marker goes to 14 for a baby that is 12. The baby stretches its neck to reach the marker, wobbles on its hooves and giggles "I'm not that tall!" The marker slides back.

**Collecting.** Growth stage badges, the growth chart, plaster designs, the record board for the tallest and smallest unicorn.

**Why it lasts.** Babies grow over weeks, so measuring keeps giving new numbers. Units move from hooves to cm. Growing up is the event she waits for, and it needs no deadline.

**Clarity risk.** Reading a value off a scale is hard. Start with the marker snapping to whole numbers and only number-line reading. It must never look like illness, so no thermometers or medicine for being unwell.

**Build cost.** M. Each growth stage needs art for every coat.

**Fit with feedback.** Nursing (v1) and pride in her herd (v2). Babies that grow up are the long arc behind "I hatched more than 10".

## 5. Wardrobe and the Royal Ball

**What she sees and does.** A big wardrobe in the castle with drawers of capes, crowns, saddles, flower garlands, horseshoes and pins. A bird drops a scroll from the Queen: the ball invitation. She dresses the babies and walks them up the steps into the ballroom. The ball has a theme. Babies that match the invitation glow and do the theme dance. Then she leads a dance.

**Role play.** Princess, dresser, and guest at the Queen's ball. The Queen (Posh voice) greets the herd by name, music plays, the babies bow.

**Reading.**
- Invitations at her level: "Wear a hat." "Come in blue. Wear a crown." Later "Bring four unicorns. Each one needs a cape." (R27 to R29, tricky words "come", "wear", "one").
- Dance cards with arrows and words: "left", "right", "turn" (R reading with M35).
- The invitation stays pinned on screen while she dresses.

**Maths.**
- Partners: "7 unicorns. Can everyone have a partner?" She pairs them and sees the one left over (M06, M41 odd and even).
- Dance steps: left, right, quarter and half turns (M35).
- Line dances as patterns (M08).
- Count the guests in 2s, 5s and 10s as they come in (M26).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** No baby is ever turned away. A baby in the wrong colour goes in and dances anyway, but it does not glow. Lumi says "Pretty! The card said blue. Shall we find something blue?" and the wardrobe opens again. A wrong dance step makes the baby spin the wrong way and bump softly into its partner, both giggle.

**Collecting.** Outfit sets by colour and land, with silhouettes of the missing ones. A ball pin for each ball she attends. A ball photo of the whole herd.

**Why it lasts.** A ball every season with a new theme (Snow Ball, Flower Ball, Star Ball). The wardrobe keeps growing. Babies grow into bigger outfits.

**Clarity risk.** The link between the invitation and the wardrobe. The scroll stays visible and each matching item gives a small chime when it goes on.

**Build cost.** L. Many outfit layers for every growth stage and pose.

**Fit with feedback.** Princesses and queens (PERSONA), dressing up as role play, and a pin set again.

## 6. Stable Homes

**What she sees and does.** A stable yard with rows of stalls. Each stall door has a name plaque. Empty stall frames wait at the end of the row. When the herd grows, she builds a new stall with planks and blocks, spells the new baby's name on its plaque, and decorates the inside.

This solves the v2 cap of 12 babies. The herd can grow without limit because the stable grows with it.

**Role play.** Builder and home maker. Hammer, paint roller, wallpaper, a little bed and a toy box in every stall.

**Reading and spelling.**
- Plaques: she spells each name with gems, crowned capital first (S11). Any name she gave is accepted (naming.md).
- Bedtime extension: she leads each baby to its own stall by reading the plaques.
- Later, alphabet order: "Put the stalls in ABC order" (S19).
- A furniture catalogue with labels: "a pink bed", "a big rug" (R27).

**Maths.**
- Planks of length 1 to 10 fill a gap of 10 (bonds, M09 to M12).
- Window and door shapes (M17).
- The herd count: "You have 14 unicorns. A row has 5 stalls. Build enough rows." Counting in 5s and 10s, arrays (M26, M29, M43).
- Wallpaper and floor tiles as patterns (M08).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** A plank too short leaves a gap, and a leaf blows in through it. A plank too long sticks out and a bird lands on the end and sings. She can see how much too long or short.

**Collecting.** Furniture and wallpaper sets, a stall for every baby, each new row a visible landmark. A yard sign that shows the herd count.

**Why it lasts.** The herd keeps growing, so the stable keeps growing. Each land has its own house style (crystal stalls by the lake, treehouse stalls in the forest).

**Clarity risk.** Building with lengths needs a ghost-hand demo and a gap that is clearly outlined.

**Build cost.** M. The stable view must handle a large herd on iPad (engine.md main risk).

**Fit with feedback.** She was proud of more than 10 babies. A stable with her names on the doors shows that number every time she visits.

## 7. Rosette Show Ring

**What she sees and does.** Grown babies (from idea 4) can enter shows. A show ring with numbered jumps, a judges' tent with the Queen, and a rosette board on the stable wall. She leads a unicorn around a course in number order, then rides a short test from a test card. At the end the herd parades past the judges.

Everyone who finishes a course gets a clear-round rosette. There is no ranking against other unicorns, so there is nothing to lose.

**Role play.** Rider and show girl. Plaited mane, numbered saddle cloth, a lap of honour.

**Reading.**
- Test cards with action words: "walk", "trot", "jump", "stop", then sentences "Trot to the tree. Jump the log." (R28, R29).
- Ordinal words when the herd lines up for the parade ("first", "second").

**Maths.**
- Jump in number order 1 to 20, then counting in 2s, 5s and 10s (M07, M26).
- Bar heights in cm (M47).
- Three judges hold up score cards and she adds them (M39), then compares totals between rounds (M36).
- Direction on the test (M35).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** The unicorn trots to the wrong jump, sniffs the flowers on it, sneezes, and trots back to the start of the line. The right jump does not flash. She finds it herself.

**Collecting.** Rosettes per baby on its stall door, ribbon colours per show type, a small trophy for each land's show.

**Show my herd for Papa.** The parade doubles as the "show my herd" mode (engagement principle 19). Emma leads the babies past one by one while Lumi says each name and its rosettes.

**Why it lasts.** A show season per land, longer courses with bigger numbers, every baby with its own board to fill.

**Clarity risk.** A riding course needs one obvious next target. Only the next number range is lit, and the path is drawn.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** Horses (PERSONA), pride in her herd, and something to show her father.

## 8. Market Day

**What she sees and does.** A village square with a little market stall. Price tags hang on the goods. A till with coin trays sits on the counter. Customers queue: Fluffles, an owl, a hedgehog, sometimes the Queen in disguise. Each customer holds up a shopping note and babbles. Emma reads the note, puts the things in a basket, and takes the coins. Later she gives change.

Money lives only in this scene. Coins stay in the till. There is no wallet that carries over and nothing to buy with it elsewhere (engagement principle 7: no currency).

**Role play.** Shopkeeper. Paper bags, a till that rings, a bell on the door.

**Reading.**
- Shopping notes: single words, then plurals "2 buns" (R12), then "3 red apples" (R27).
- Customers never say the item (Fluffle babble must never sound like a word, VOICES.md). The note is the only clue, as in Fluffle Nursery.

**Maths.**
- Coins and their value (M32), with euro or pound as a setting (curriculum.md).
- Pay an amount with 1, 2, 5 and 10 coins (M33).
- Add two prices (M13, M38), then give change (M44).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** The customer looks at the note, looks at the wrong item, sneezes a giggle and hands it back. Wrong change: the customer counts the coins in its paw (ghost counting) and pushes one back across the counter.

**Collecting.** A visitor book where each new kind of customer leaves a paw print. Stall decorations. New goods for the stall per land.

**Why it lasts.** Money covers Year 1 and Year 2, prices grow, new customers come with each land.

**Clarity risk.** The idea of paying is new for her. Start with exact amounts and coins that match one price. The value of a coin is not its size, so first steps show dots on the coins.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** Fluffles return as customers, and v2 liked the Fluffle wishes. It is role play that also carries the money maths no other activity covers.

## 9. Trail Rides

**What she sees and does.** She takes one to three grown babies on an outing. The trail scrolls sideways through a land: forks with signposts, stepping stones, a bridge. At the end there is something to find: a wild egg of a kind that only trails give, or a missing item for a set. The goal is shown as a picture in a bubble above Lumi, the signposts are words. She reads the signs to choose the way.

**Role play.** Explorer with a little backpack, a picnic stop, the babies splashing in a stream.

**Reading.**
- Signposts: "lake", "hill", "cave" (R levels), later "The bridge is shut. Go by the hill." (R28, R29) and short Queen's letters with who and where (R41).
- Tapping a sign plays its sounds, never the word.

**Maths.**
- Numbered stepping stones: "Jump 3 on" (M19), later on a number line to 100 (M38).
- Left, right and turns at forks (M35).
- A bridge with a gap of 10 planks (bonds).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** The wrong path leads to a short, funny dead end: a sleepy badger who snores, a puddle that splashes the babies. They giggle, shake off and trot back to the fork. Nothing is lost.

**Collecting.** Wild egg kinds (straight into the hatching loop she loves), stamps on the land map, pressed flowers.

**Why it lasts.** Trails are where the lands live (principle 13). A new land opens new trails and new wild eggs. The map shows where she has been.

**Clarity risk.** A scrolling scene with a goal picture and word signs. Keep one fork on screen at a time and the goal picture always visible.

**Build cost.** L. Scrolling scenes, many backgrounds.

**Fit with feedback.** More eggs to hatch, and a new reason to go out with the babies she named.

## 10. Hatch Days and Best Friends

**What she sees and does.** Every baby has a hatch day on the calendar on the stable wall. When she opens the game on or after that day, the baby's party is waiting: she bakes the cake in the Kitchen, puts the candles on, writes a card, and the herd sings. Babies also make best friends: two babies walk together, and she makes them a matching friendship bracelet. Little notes from the babies appear in a mailbox ("I love my red bow!", "Can we go to the lake?"). Each note maps to one thing she can do.

A party never expires. If she misses the day, it waits for her.

**Role play.** Party planner, big sister to a family of unicorns. Grown babies help warm the next egg, so the herd becomes a family.

**Reading and spelling.**
- Mailbox notes: picture plus one word at first, then short sentences with tricky words (R28, R29).
- The card: she builds "Happy hatch day, Pip!" from a word bank (S18), with a capital for the name (S11), later "Pip's party" (S41).
- Days of the week on the calendar (S20).

**Maths.**
- Candles: the age in weeks, counting and one more (M04).
- Days on the calendar, before and after (M34).
- Bracelet bead patterns (M08).
- A herd chart by colour or by friend group (M48).

**Wrong answer in the fiction.** A wrong candle count: the cake gives a little puff of smoke and one candle blows out by itself, or a candle is missing and the baby counts its own age on its hooves. A note done wrong: the baby looks puzzled, holds the note up again, and the note's picture wiggles.

**Collecting.** Party photos, cards she wrote, friendship bracelets, a family page that shows who is friends with whom.

**Why it lasts.** With a big herd there is always a hatch day coming. Friendships and families give each baby a story that grows.

**Clarity risk.** Notes that ask for action are where Queen Emma's Letters failed. Every note must have a picture target and lead to exactly one place. The calendar is abstract, so show it as a strip of days with the baby's face on its day.

**Build cost.** M. Depends on the Kitchen being built.

**Fit with feedback.** The babies she named become individuals with friends and a past. It uses her love of naming and role play at the level of the whole herd.

## Audio note

All learning words, phrases and sentences in these activities are pre-generated Elizabeth clips. Card and invitation content must come from finite lists per step, built by the generator, not assembled at runtime. Customers, babies and Fluffles only giggle, babble or say tiny fixed words. Names follow the naming.md flow: any name is accepted, and the clip for a new name is made in the next batch.

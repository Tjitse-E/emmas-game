# Brainstorm: world, levels and long-term structure

Lens: how Unicorn Egg Nest becomes "an actual game with levels" that Emma still wants to play after months. Written 2026-10-12, based on the v2 verdict and the research in `docs/v3/research/` (v2-audit, engagement, curriculum, naming, engine).

## What every idea keeps

These are fixed, whatever structure we pick:

- **The core loop stays.** Warm an egg with number stones, hatch, name, spell the name, add the baby to the herd. "One more egg" stays easy to reach. This is what she played more than 10 times in a row.
- **Names are never refused.** Any safe name she builds is accepted. The game sounds it out from her own gems, and the generator makes Elizabeth's clip for the next night (see `research/naming.md`).
- **The ladders drive the learning, the world is the skin.** Reading R01 to R45, spelling S01 to S42 and maths M01 to M48 move on the mastery rules in `research/curriculum.md`. A step takes one to two weeks. The world shows that progress, so progress in the world equals progress in learning.
- **Mistakes are funny events in the world.** Frosty cups, steam, a baby with a pan on its nose. Never a buzzer, a red cross or a lost item.
- **No pressure.** No streaks, timers, sick babies, locked paid items or "come back tomorrow". Absence costs nothing.
- **Everything spoken.** Elizabeth for all learning content, characters per VOICES.md. A new character needs a voice from Tjitse and Emma before it gets lines.

## 1. Lands of the Moon Kingdom

**Pitch.** A painted pop-up map of the Moon Kingdom. Moon Meadow is home. Around it lie Crystal Lake, Rainbow Woods, Cloud Castle and Starfall Beach, still under soft fog with a baby peeking out. Each land has its own egg kind, item set and role play, and the fog lifts piece by piece as she learns.

**On screen and touch.** The map is the hub. She taps a land, the camera flies there, and the night happens in that land (the egg loop plus that land's activity). Each land has 6 to 10 small spots (the willow, the berry bushes, the bridge, the pond). Each spot opens when one ladder step is passed, so something new opens about every week. A new spot gives a new egg kind and a new item set.

**Lands and the ladders.**

| Land | Reading | Spelling | Maths | Egg kind |
|------|---------|----------|-------|----------|
| Moon Meadow | R01 to R12 (Phase 2) | S01 to S08 | M01 to M08 (to 10) | Moon egg |
| Crystal Lake | R13 to R20 (Phase 3 vowels) | S09 to S11 | M09 to M17 (bonds, add and take within 10) | Water egg |
| Rainbow Woods | R21 to R29 (blends, phrases, sentences) | S12 to S18 | M18 to M25 (teens, to 20) | Moss egg |
| Cloud Castle | R30 to R39 (Phase 5) | S19 to S30 | M26 to M35 (tens, money, time) | Cloud egg |
| Starfall Beach | R40 to R45 (texts) | S31 to S42 | M36 to M48 (to 100, tables, fractions) | Shell egg |

The strands move at different speeds, so a land opens when any one strand reaches it. Inside a land, every task still uses her own current step per strand.

**Wrong answer.** As in v2 inside the egg and naming. On the map there is no "locked". Fogged places show a baby waving from behind the fog, and Lumi says "The fog is getting thinner!"

**Role play, reading, maths, collecting.** Each land adds one role play (lake: bathing and swimming, woods: picnics and hide and seek, castle: the royal ball). Reading: signposts and wish words use the land's sounds (Crystal Lake is full of ee and oo words: reeds, moon, boat). Maths: each land's egg uses its maths stage. Collecting: an item set and an egg kind per spot, shown with silhouettes in the Nest Book.

**Longevity.** About 40 spots over 9 to 12 months. A new spot about every week, a new land every 2 to 3 months.

**Clarity risk.** A map with many places can turn into a menu of twenty. Show only the lands she has opened plus one fogged neighbour. Lumi always says where tonight's egg is.

## 2. Stables of Ten

**Pitch.** Every baby gets its own stall in a stable block of 10 stalls: two rows of five, a real ten-frame. When a block is full, the herd holds a Stable Raising party and a new block appears. At 20, 30, 50 and 100 babies the nest grows into a village with a bakery, a school, a ballroom and a castle wing.

**On screen and touch.** After naming, the new baby trots to the stables. Emma drags it to the next empty stall and the stall door gets its name plate. The big herd counter above the stables reads "3 stables and 4. 34 babies!" as tens and ones. She names each stable block herself (Rose Row, Pip Lane), and that name is never refused either.

**Role play.** Mucking out, filling hay nets, painting stall doors, hanging her pins and ribbons on the doors. Each village building is a new place to play.

**Reading.** At bedtime she finds babies by their name plates (R names at her level, later alphabet order, S19: babies move into Letter Lane by first letter). Stall door signs ("Tam is in the bath") become R28 sentences later.

**Maths.** This is where place value is real. Counting on to the next free stall (M02, M04). Hay in bundles of 10 (M27, M28). Rows of five and arrays (M26, M29). "How many more babies until the bakery?" (M15, M36). Comparing two stable blocks (M03).

**Wrong answer.** If she drags a baby to a taken stall, the two babies bump noses and giggle, and the newcomer hops to the right stall with Lumi counting the gap.

**Collecting.** The herd itself. Door decorations make each stall show her items.

**Longevity.** The herd grows for months. Every tenth baby is a party she can see coming. It replaces the hidden v2 cap of 12 with a reason to keep going.

**Clarity risk.** Low. The risk is busywork: placing the 40th baby must not be tedious. After the first blocks, the baby trots in by itself unless the stall is part of tonight's maths.

## 3. Queen Lumi's Quest Board

**Pitch.** At the castle gate hangs a board with three picture scrolls. Each scroll is a small quest of 3 to 5 nights: "The baker lost her pins", "Pip's birthday picnic", "The owl needs a lantern". She picks one, and the quest's picture checklist fills with sparkles as she plays.

**On screen and touch.** Each scroll is mainly a picture. Lumi (or the Queen, voiced per VOICES.md) acts the quest out. One key word on the scroll is decodable at her level, and Elizabeth reads the rest. Quest steps happen inside the normal night: an egg, a find, a delivery. The quest ends with a party in that place and a surprise item set or egg.

**Role play.** She is the Queen's helper: deliver, bake, decorate, search. Her own babies come along and show up in the quest story.

**Reading.** Picture quests at first, one key word that changes the job ("red" or "big" pin). Then phrases (R27), one-sentence instructions (R28, R29) and later short letters from the Queen (R41 to R43).

**Maths.** Quantities inside the quest: 7 guests and 4 cakes on the table (M13 to M15), share 12 berries between 2 babies (M06, M24), pay the baker (M32, M33).

**Wrong answer.** The character reacts in a kind, funny way: "A pan? I asked for a pin!" and balances the pan on her head. The picture on the scroll glows on the part to look at again.

**Collecting.** Quest rewards are surprise item sets. A quest page in the Nest Book keeps the finished scrolls with a sticker of the party.

**Longevity.** A library of hand-written quests plus quests made from her own herd (a birthday quest for a baby she named weeks ago). New quests come in small batches every week or two.

**Clarity risk.** High. Queen Emma's Letters failed on exactly this: reading to know what to do. The picture must carry the whole goal, reading only changes a detail, and the spotlight and ghost hand work on every quest step.

## 4. The Grumble Cloud and the Lost Colours

**Pitch.** A grey, grumpy cloud sneezed the colours out of the kingdom. Seven chapters, one per rainbow colour, each 2 to 4 weeks. Every night she plays, part of the scene is painted back in colour, and at the end of the last chapter she gives the Grumble Cloud a name, and it turns into a rainbow cloud that carries her babies.

**On screen and touch.** The current land starts grey. Each right first try sends a paint drop to the scene: a tree goes green, a door goes red. The chapter opens with a short picture-book page that Lumi reads. The chapter ends with a colour festival where all babies wear the colour.

**Role play.** Emma is the rescuer and painter. She paints babies' manes, decorates in the chapter colour and comforts the Grumble Cloud. The cloud only grumbles and sneezes (sound effects, no words), so it needs no new voice.

**Reading.** Colour words are early spelling targets. The story pages grow with her: first Elizabeth reads it all and Emma reads one highlighted word, later whole sentences (R28, R29), sequencing story panels (R42) and a page she reads to the herd (R45).

**Maths.** Paint pots: "The rainbow bridge needs 10 drops, 6 are in" (M09 to M11). Mixing paint in equal parts (M06, M31). Counting painted items in a scene (M02, M07).

**Wrong answer.** A drop lands in a silly spot (a green nose on Lumi), the cloud giggles for the first time, and the drop slides off for another try.

**Collecting.** One paint colour and one item set per chapter. A colour wheel in the Nest Book fills up.

**Longevity.** A clear story arc of about 6 months, with a finale she can look forward to. After the story, a second story can follow on the same spine.

**Clarity risk.** A 6-year-old may lose the thread over weeks. Every night starts with one picture recap. The cloud must look silly and sad, never scary. Stories over months risk feeling slow if a chapter waits on a hard ladder step, so a chapter ends on a night count as well as on learning.

## 5. Egg Trails

**Pitch.** Wild eggs hide around the kingdom. She picks a grown unicorn, rides out along a trail of numbered stepping stones, reads signposts at the forks and finds a wild egg to carry home and warm. Every biome has its own egg kinds, and the Egg Kinds page shows silhouettes of the ones still missing.

**On screen and touch.** A side-scrolling path of stones. A hoof stone shows how far to go ("Hop 6!"). She taps the stone she thinks the unicorn lands on, and the unicorn hops there counting. At a fork, two signposts with pictures and words (pond, hill). A clue from the baby or the Queen points to the egg ("It is by the pond") and she reads which way to go. At the end, the egg sits in a nest of leaves.

**Role play.** Riding her own unicorns, which Emma loves (horses), and picking which one comes along.

**Reading.** Signposts at the forks: CVC words first (pond, hill, den), then blends and digraphs (marsh, cliff, stream), then short clues (R27, R28).

**Maths.** The trail is a number line. Dots on stones at first (M02), numerals (M04), counting on and back within 20 (M19), jumps of 10 and 5 on longer trails (M26, M28, M38).

**Wrong answer.** The unicorn hops to the stone she picked and finds something funny there (a sleeping frog, a puddle splash), then Lumi counts back to the start of the jump and she tries again.

**Collecting.** 40 or more egg kinds, each hatching babies with a biome look (moss manes, shell horns). Rare eggs only on longer trails. Items found along the way go in the Nest Book.

**Longevity.** Trails get longer as her maths grows (to 10, to 20, to 100), so the world literally gets bigger with her number sense.

**Clarity risk.** "Which stone does it land on?" is abstract at first. Start with the ghost hand and dots, and let the unicorn count out loud while hopping.

## 6. Moon Egg Nights

**Pitch.** When she passes a ladder step, a giant Moon Egg appears on the hill under a full moon. On Moon Egg Night the whole herd stands in a circle around it, and the egg has five glowing rings that she fills with tasks from what she just mastered. When it hatches, she gets a Moon Unicorn, a jewel in her crown and a new royal title from the Queen.

**On screen and touch.** A big night scene, every baby she ever hatched in the circle, the herd count shown big ("29 babies came to watch!"). Each ring is one short task from the step she passed or a recent one: warm a ring with stones, spell a name on the ring, read a sign to find the ring's key. The hatch is bigger than a normal hatch: fireworks, music, the Queen speaks.

**Role play.** A ceremony with a title: Egg Warmer, Nest Keeper, Royal Nest Keeper, Moon Princess, Queen of the Moon Meadow. Babies wear party hats. The Moon Unicorn becomes her riding unicorn or herd leader.

**Reading, spelling, maths.** The rings are a mastery check in disguise. They use the mastery rule (right first time in two different sessions), so the boss moment confirms what she learned. One ring is always a review of something older (retrieval practice).

**Wrong answer.** The ring flickers and a baby from her herd trots up to help. The hint ladder becomes the herd helping her. The rings she has filled stay filled, and the Moon Egg waits for her if the night runs out.

**Collecting.** Moon Unicorns (one per passed step, about 130 over the full map), crown jewels, title ribbons.

**Longevity.** A ceremony every one to two weeks for about a year. It gives the "level up" moment Tjitse asked for, without a fail state.

**Clarity risk.** Low, because each ring reuses a task she knows. A night with five rings could get too long: keep each ring under a minute and allow the egg to finish the next night.

## 7. Moon School

**Pitch.** Babies grow up by going to Moon School in the castle, and Emma is the teacher. She shows a baby a sound or a number, the baby tries, and sometimes the baby gets it wrong and Emma fixes it. A grown unicorn picks a job from two or three pictures and opens a place in the village where Emma plays with it.

**On screen and touch.** A small classroom with a slate. A baby sits at a desk. Emma drags a letter gem onto the slate, Elizabeth says the sound, the baby repeats it with a giggle. The baby "reads" a word, and the picture of what it read pops up. If it read "cup" when the slate says "cap", a cup lands on its head. Emma taps the wrong gem and swaps it, and a cap appears instead.

**Jobs.**

| Job | Place | Learning inside |
|-----|-------|-----------------|
| Baker | Bakery | Count buns into boxes, bonds and making 10 (M09 to M13, M21) |
| Postie | Post office | Read names and addresses on letters and deliver them to the herd (R names, R28, S41 "Pip's") |
| Gardener | Garden | Plant rows and arrays, count in 2s, 5s, 10s (M26, M29) |
| Tailor | Ribbon shop | Measure ribbons, longer and shorter (M25, M47) |
| Lamplighter | Tower | Light lamps at the right o'clock (M34, M46) |
| Shopkeeper | Stall | Prices and change (M32, M33, M44) |

**Role play.** Teacher, then village life with the grown unicorns in their jobs. This gives the many "things to do with the unicorns" Tjitse asked for.

**Wrong answer.** If the baby errs, Emma is the one who fixes it, so a mistake never feels like hers. If Emma picks the wrong gem, the baby copies it, the picture that pops up is silly, and Lumi gives the focused hint.

**Collecting.** Growth stages per baby (baby, foal, young unicorn, grown), school badges on each baby's page, jobs and the places they open.

**Longevity.** Every baby has its own small journey, so 50 babies are 50 stories. The village grows with every job.

**Clarity risk.** Spotting a baby's mistake is harder than making her own answer. The picture that pops up must show the mismatch clearly, and early lessons let the baby get it right so she learns the routine first. Also the most art: growth stages, classroom, six job places.

## 8. Seasons and Festivals

**Pitch.** The meadow follows the real calendar in Cyprus: autumn leaves now, a snowy meadow and the Snow Ball in winter, the Blossom Fair in spring, a summer beach with shell eggs. Each season brings a new egg kind, an item set (snowflake pins, shell pins) and one festival night.

**On screen and touch.** The meadow art changes with the season. A wall calendar by the nest shows the week. Each night she places the day sticker, and Elizabeth reads it. Festival nights are big role play events: at the Snow Ball she dresses her babies and pairs them for a dance, at the Blossom Fair she runs a stall. Emma's and Papa's birthdays are set in the parent corner and get their own party.

**Role play.** Dressing up for festivals, decorating, dancing, running stalls, building a snow unicorn.

**Reading.** Festival invitations and menus (R27, R28), day names (S20), seasonal words on wishes (snow, leaf, shell, sun).

**Maths.** Counting sleeps on the calendar (M02, M07), days of the week and o'clock for the festival (M34), stall prices in the right currency (M32, M33, M44), seasonal measures (snow height, M25).

**Wrong answer.** As in the core loop. At the festival stall a wrong amount makes the customer giggle and hand a coin back.

**Collecting.** Seasonal egg kinds and item sets. Nothing expires: what she missed waits in a "memory trunk" and comes back the next year.

**Longevity.** A year-long cycle with a new thing every few weeks. This is the content calendar that keeps novelty in small doses.

**Clarity risk.** Low. The risk is the calendar becoming a "come back tomorrow" pressure. The calendar only counts, it never warns, and a missed festival has no cost.

## 9. Sun-to-Moon Day

**Pitch.** Every session is one day in the kingdom, and a tower clock shows where the day is. Morning is breakfast for the babies, daytime is her choice of two or three places, evening is egg time, and bedtime ends the session with the herd tucked in. She moves the day on by turning the clock hands herself.

**On screen and touch.** The sun crosses the sky above the castle tower clock. Morning: babies line up with breakfast wishes she reads and counts out. Daytime: two or three picture doors (a land, a quest, the school). Evening: eggs, with "one more egg" offered once. Bedtime: name signs, a story, goodnight. Days collect on a week chart, and the seventh day is Ball Day.

**Role play.** A full day of caring: feeding, playing, nursing, putting to bed.

**Reading.** Breakfast orders (wish words), bedtime name signs, the bedtime story she gradually reads more of (R41, R45).

**Maths.** The clock is the button to move on. Before M34 she just taps it. From M34 she turns the hand to 6 o'clock for egg time, later half past and quarter past (M46). Breakfast portions (M13, M14, M24).

**Wrong answer.** If she sets the clock to the wrong time, the day jumps there for a moment: at 3 o'clock all the babies yawn and fall asleep on the grass, she laughs, and Lumi says "Too early for bed!" and points at the hour hand.

**Collecting.** A week chart with a sticker per day she played, a scrapbook photo of one moment per day.

**Longevity.** A ritual she can count on, with a clean end. It is the session frame that other structures (lands, quests, school) plug into.

**Clarity risk.** Low for the rhythm. The clock must never block her: until the clock skill is in play, one tap moves the day, and a misset clock never stops the session.

## 10. The Star Sky

**Pitch.** Above the meadow at night the sky slowly fills with constellations shaped like unicorns, crowns and eggs. Every star is one thing she has learned: a sound, a tricky word, a number fact. When a constellation is complete, its unicorn comes alive, gallops down the sky and leaves a star egg.

**On screen and touch.** At bedtime Lumi points up: "You learned sh! A new star." The star shows its gem. She can tap any star to hear what it is (Elizabeth says the sound, word or fact). A telescope zooms in on her constellations. Stars never go out. Items due for review twinkle, and the next night's wishes and eggs quietly use them.

**Role play.** Stargazing with the herd at bedtime, naming constellations (never refused), riding the constellation unicorns.

**Reading, spelling, maths.** The sky is the map of the three ladders: one constellation per step (R, S and M steps in different parts of the sky). It gives Emma a picture of her own progress (principle 18) and Tjitse a parent view in the same picture.

**Wrong answer.** Nothing in the sky reacts to a miss. Misses only show in the normal loop, as in v2.

**Collecting.** Stars, constellations and star eggs. About 135 constellations over the full map.

**Longevity.** The sky fills over a year, and she can see the total grow, which matches her pride in quantity.

**Clarity risk.** The link between stars and learning is abstract for a 6-year-old. It works as a backdrop and a moment at bedtime, not as the main way to play. Tapping a star to hear it must not turn into a flash card drill.

## How they combine

These are separate structures, but several fit together. One possible spine:

- **Sun-to-Moon Day (9)** as the frame for every session.
- **Lands of the Moon Kingdom (1)** as the levels, with a new spot about every week.
- **Stables of Ten (2)** as the home for the growing herd, which removes the cap of 12.
- **Moon Egg Nights (6)** as the level-up moment when a step is passed.
- **Seasons and Festivals (8)** for small, regular novelty.

Moon School (7), Quests (3) and Egg Trails (5) are the role play and activity layers inside the lands. The Grumble Cloud (4) and the Star Sky (10) are alternative ways to show long-term progress and can replace the map or sit beside it.

## Questions to test with Emma

- Does she want to go places (map, trails) or have places come to her (village, seasons)?
- Does a ceremony with a title (Moon Egg Nights) make her proud, or does she skip it to get back to eggs?
- Does she enjoy correcting a baby's mistake (Moon School), or does it confuse her?
- Does she follow a story over weeks (Grumble Cloud), or does she only care about tonight?

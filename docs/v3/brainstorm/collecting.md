# Brainstorm: collecting and pride

Lens for v3 of Unicorn Egg Nest, 2026-10-12. Sources: the v2 verdict, `docs/v3/research/` (v2-audit, engagement, curriculum, naming).

Emma was proud of hatching more than 10 eggs. She loved naming the babies and collecting the pins. The ideas below make what she owns the place where the maths and reading happen. The herd count, the item sets and the album are the things she counts, sorts, reads and spells.

Rules for every idea:

- The egg, hatch, name and spell loop stays the core. These ideas give the babies somewhere to go and something to collect.
- No chance boxes, no currency to spend, no streaks, no timers, no sad or sick babies. A rare thing comes from a choice she made or a first-try success, never from a random pull.
- A wrong answer is a small funny event in the world, followed by the v2 hint ladder. No buzzer, no red cross.
- Every name, word and number Emma learns from is spoken by Elizabeth from a pre-generated clip. A name without a clip yet is shown and sounded out, and is spoken once the next clip batch runs (see `research/naming.md`).
- Ladder codes (R, S, M) refer to `research/curriculum.md`.

## 1. Ten-Stall Stable

**What she sees and does.** The fence with 12 spots becomes a stable with rows of ten numbered stall doors. After naming, the new baby stands in the yard and Lumi says: "Pip is baby number twenty-three! Which stall is hers?" Emma taps a door, the baby trots in, and Emma hangs the name plaque. When a row of ten is full, two builder beavers arrive, and Emma loads their cart with exactly the planks they still need. A new row goes up with a ribbon to cut.

**Role play.** Stable keeper. She opens doors, hangs plaques, cuts ribbons and closes the doors at bedtime.

**Reading.** Bedtime roll call. Lumi shows a lantern with a name written on it, without saying it. Emma reads the name and closes that baby's door. The names are her own herd's names, so she reads made-up words she cares about (R38, the phonics check skill). Early on the lantern is spoken and only the plaque must be matched. Later the lantern is the only clue. Wrong door: the baby inside pokes its head out, yawns "Not me!" and goes back to sleep.

**Maths.** Number recognition to 100 (M02, M07, M28). Finding stall 23 means counting on from the last full door or reading the numeral. Tens and ones (M18, M27): the roof sign shows the herd as hay bundles of ten plus loose stars. The builders' cart is a bond to 10 ("This row has 7 babies. How many more planks?"). A tap on stall 25 when 24 is empty: the baby peeks in, looks back and trots to the empty stall before it. A tap on a taken stall: "That's Tam's stall! Tam says hello."

**Collecting.** The herd count, rows, plaques and milestone ribbons. At 10, 25, 50 and 100 babies the stable gets something new (a weather vane, a tower, a flag) and the Queen's bells ring.

**Why she keeps playing.** The count keeps growing past 100. Roll call cycles through the whole herd, so old names come back for review.

**Where she might get lost.** Numbers above 20 before she knows them. At low maths levels the next free door glows. Roll call is capped at 3 to 5 babies a night.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** It replaces the hidden cap of 12 that probably stopped her hatching. Her pride in "more than 10" becomes the biggest number on the screen.

## 2. The Pin Post

**What she sees and does.** Each night the moonbeam post drops two or three small envelopes. Each is addressed to one baby by name. Emma drags the envelope to the right baby, who opens it with its nose and pins the new pin on its sash. The sash holds ten pins in two rows of five. The pin board in the Nest Book shows every pin set with grey silhouettes of the pins still missing.

**Role play.** Post keeper and dresser. She delivers, then decides where each pin goes on the sash.

**Reading.** The envelope shows only the written name. Tapping it gives the first sound, then the sounds one by one (hint ladder). Later envelopes carry two names ("Pip and Tam", R27) or a short note ("Pip, this is for your bath.", R28). Wrong baby: it sniffs the envelope, shakes its head with a giggle and pushes it back. On a second miss the right baby hops and whinnies.

**Maths.** The sash is a five-frame pair. "Pip has 7 pins. How many more to fill the sash?" (bonds to 10). A full sash turns gold as a surprise. Envelopes with "3 pins for Pip and Tam" ask her to share between two babies (M06). At the parade (idea 6) she compares sashes: who has more, who has fewer (M03).

**Collecting.** Pin sets: flower pins, star pins, land pins (one per land), moment pins (first bath, tenth baby) and letter pins. A letter pin arrives when she has secured a grapheme on the ladder. Tapping it plays Elizabeth's pure sound. The letter pin board is her phonics progress as a picture she understands.

**Why she keeps playing.** Letter pins follow the reading ladder for more than a year. New pin sets come with each land and season.

**Where she might get lost.** Finding the addressee in a big herd. Only three babies stand in the meadow at delivery time, one of them the addressee.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** The pins were her favourite collectable. Reading the names she gave herself makes the reading personal.

## 3. The Photo Album

**What she sees and does.** The Nest Book becomes a photo album. Emma poses a baby (sit, rear, wink, rainbow sneeze), picks a frame and taps the camera. The photo flies into the album. Each page holds ten photos in a ten-frame. There is a colour page and an index with letter tabs.

**Role play.** Photographer. She chooses the pose and the frame.

**Reading.** To file a photo she taps the tab for the first sound of the baby's name (S01, later letter names and alphabet order, S19). Wrong tab: the babies already on that page lean in, look at the photo and shake their heads, and the photo floats back. Each photo gets a caption she builds from word tiles: "Pip is pink." (S18, R28). Elizabeth reads the caption back.

**Maths.** Pages of ten show the herd as full pages plus loose photos (place value). On the colour page she drags each new photo onto its colour row, which makes a pictogram (M48). Lumi asks: "Which colour do you have most of?" or "How many more pink babies than blue?" (M03, M15).

**Collecting.** The album itself, frames, poses and a page per land.

**Why she keeps playing.** The album grows with the herd. Captions grow from two words to short sentences as her reading grows. A "Show Papa" slideshow plays every photo while Lumi says each name and the count.

**Where she might get lost.** Filing by letter needs a ghost-hand demo. Every tab plays its sound on touch.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** The v1 sticker book and the v2 Nest Book were loved, but paging one baby at a time gets slow with 30 babies.

## 4. Egg Kinds and Breeds

**What she sees and does.** The nest holds two or three eggs of different kinds, and Emma picks one. Each kind is warmed with a different maths action and hatches its own breed. A moon egg fills its cups to ten. A twin egg needs a double and hatches two babies. A snow egg is too warm and needs stones taken away. A star egg is warmed in steps of two or five. A teen egg has a twenty-frame. The Egg Book shows a silhouette for each kind.

**Role play.** Nest keeper, as in v2, with more variety.

**Reading.** The Queen's delivery note names tonight's eggs: "a snow egg and a big red egg" (R27). Emma reads it to choose which to warm first. Pictures carry the meaning at first, and the words take over later.

**Maths.** Each kind is a ladder step: moon egg M09 to M12, berry egg counting on M13, snow egg take away M14, twin egg doubles M23, teen egg M18 and M21, star egg counting in 2s and 5s M26, bundle egg tens and ones M27. Errors keep the v2 hot and cold feedback. A twin egg that is one off hatches one twin while the other shell stays closed and shivers: "One twin is still cold!"

**Collecting.** Breeds (moon foal, snow foal, sea foal, flower foal, cloud foal, star foal), egg kinds and twins. The golden version of each egg is a harder egg she chooses herself. Shiny stays a surprise for a first try with no hint.

**Why she keeps playing.** A new egg kind every two weeks, matched to her maths ladder. Old kinds stay in the nest for review.

**Where she might get lost.** Too many mechanics at once. Each new kind arrives alone, with a ghost-hand demo and a picture on the shell.

**Build cost.** L.

**Fit with feedback.** "One more egg" was the engine of her 10+ hatches. This varies it without replacing it, and twins push her count up faster.

## 5. The Royal Wardrobe

**What she sees and does.** A wardrobe room with drawers for bows, crowns, saddles, hoof boots, mane beads and blankets. Dressing up is free play with no wrong answer. Sometimes a ball invitation arrives: "Pip wants a red bow and a gold crown." Emma dresses the baby to match and it dances at the Royal Ball.

**Role play.** Royal dresser and ball host.

**Reading.** Drawer labels (bow, hat, cap, sock: CVC words) and the invitations (R27 colour and size phrases, later R28 and R29 sentences). The invitation shows grey item outlines with the colour words written, so the colour can only come from reading. Wrong look: at the ball door the herald reads the card again, the baby looks down at its bow and blushes, and Emma can swap.

**Maths.** Mane braids take beads in a pattern (AB, ABB, ABC, M08). A bead that breaks the pattern makes the braid spring loose with a "boing". Hoof boots come in pairs (M29). The ball hall has ten seats: "Six babies are here. How many more can come?"

**Collecting.** Every item, in sets with silhouettes. Each baby keeps its saved outfit, which shows in the album and the parade.

**Why she keeps playing.** A new set every two weeks from a tailor visitor or a Queen's gift. Seasonal sets (snow scarves, beach hats for a Cyprus summer).

**Where she might get lost.** Free play needs no explanation. The invitation is the only goal, so it must be clear that it is a request. Lumi says "Pip has an invitation! Let's dress her for the ball."

**Build cost.** L. Items must layer on the unicorn, which needs a new art rig.

**Fit with feedback.** More role play, which Tjitse asked for, and more items to collect, like the pins.

## 6. The Grand Parade

**What she sees and does.** When she wants, Emma holds a parade for the Queen. She picks the babies, sets the order and sends them over the bridge past the royal box. A big counter climbs as they pass. Every baby gets a rosette for something it did ("best hop", "shiniest mane"). Nobody loses.

**Role play.** Parade master.

**Reading.** The herald's scroll lists the order in writing. Emma reads the next name and sends that baby. Wrong baby: the herald peers at the scroll, the baby makes a funny bow and steps back into line.

**Maths.** Order and ordinals (first, second, third). Babies march in twos or fives and the counter goes 2, 4, 6 (M26, M29). The bridge holds ten: "Seven are on. How many more?" Line up by height, tallest first (M25).

**Collecting.** A rosette wall for each baby, parade pennants, a new route for each land.

**Why she keeps playing.** The parade grows with the herd. A "Show Papa" mode skips the tasks and parades the whole herd while Lumi says every name and the count ends with "Thirty-one unicorns!".

**Where she might get lost.** Managing a big line-up. A parade group is capped at ten.

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** It turns her pride in the herd size into an event she can show her father.

## 7. Colour Families

**What she sees and does.** Two grown unicorns who are best friends can look after one egg together. The egg shows a stripe of each friend's colour. The baby gets the body colour of one and the mane colour of the other, so Emma can plan the baby she wants. A colour chart has a square for every body and mane mix, with empty squares still to fill.

**Role play.** Matchmaker for friendships and planner of the family.

**Reading and spelling.** A family baby's name bells offer names one sound away from a parent's name. Pip's baby could be Pop, Pim or Tip. Spelling it means swapping one gem in the parent's name (phoneme substitution, S02 to S05, then longer names). The family tree shows all the names, and Emma reads them to find a baby.

**Maths.** The chart is a grid, rows by columns (arrays, M29). "Half your babies have pink manes" (halves, M24). Family totals: "Pip has 3 babies and 2 grandbabies. How big is Pip's family?" (adding, then three numbers, M39).

**Collecting.** Chart squares (16 at first, 64 later), family trees and generations.

**Why she keeps playing.** Filling the chart is a long goal she controls. Nothing is random, so planning pays off.

**Where she might get lost.** Colour mixing can feel abstract. The egg stripe shows the result before hatching, and Lumi says it: "Pink body, blue mane!"

**Build cost.** M.

**Fit with feedback.** Naming and nursing were her favourites. Families make every baby more personal, and the name ladder trains spelling.

## 8. The Little Museum

**What she sees and does.** Each land hides treasures: shells at the Crystal Lake, feathers in the Rainbow Forest, crystals in the caves. A baby leads Emma on a short treasure walk. A clue note or a path of numbered stepping stones points to the dig spot. Finds go into glass cases of ten in Lumi's museum.

**Role play.** Explorer and museum keeper.

**Reading.** Clue notes, with pictures first, then "Dig by the big red rock." (R27 to R29) and position words (under, behind, next to). Each find needs a label. Emma spells it with gems (shell, S07). Wrong spot: the baby digs up something silly (an old boot, a grumpy frog), which goes on the Silly Shelf, and the clue glows again.

**Maths.** "Hop seven stones" along a number path (counting on and back, M19). Cases of ten: "How many more shells to fill the case?" Comparing cases: which land has more finds.

**Collecting.** Museum cases with silhouettes and the Silly Shelf. A full case brings a visitor from that land.

**Why she keeps playing.** Every new land adds a case. Animal Crossing's museum held children for years.

**Where she might get lost.** Clue notes may be too hard to read at first. The first clues are pictures only, and words replace them one at a time.

**Build cost.** L.

**Fit with feedback.** Collecting found items was a clear favourite. The museum gives the babies a reason to explore the levels.

## 9. The Bedtime Story Shelf

**What she sees and does.** Each land and each herd milestone brings a small picture book about her own babies, with their names written in. At bedtime Emma reads a page to the herd. Each sentence is read, then acted: "Pip jumps on the bed." She drags Pip onto the bed and the page turns. Books stand on a shelf with gaps for the books still to come.

**Role play.** She reads bedtime stories to her babies, like a parent.

**Reading.** Decodable sentences at her level, with her herd's names and her tricky words (R28, R29, then R41 to R45). A tap on a word shows its sound buttons first, and the whole word only after she tries. The game has no microphone, so the check is the action. A misread is acted out literally, and the baby does the wrong thing in a funny way ("Pip jumps on the bun!"). She reads again and fixes it.

**Maths.** Story sums the pictures act out: "Three babies jump in the pond. Two hop out." She makes the scene match.

**Collecting.** Books, each with a cover showing her babies. A moon on the spine fills when every baby in the herd has heard it, which rewards re-reading without a reward for each read.

**Why she keeps playing.** New books arrive as her reading grows. Re-reading old favourites at bedtime builds fluency.

**Where she might get lost.** Sentences that are too long. One sentence per page at first, and a picture always shows the action.

**Build cost.** L. Content-heavy, and every word needs an Elizabeth clip.

**Fit with feedback.** A role-play moment with her babies that carries real sentence reading, which v2 had none of.

## 10. The Alphabet Arches

**What she sees and does.** A row of arches stands at the meadow edge, one for each letter sound. Later arches come for sh, ch, th and the vowel teams. A baby's name lights the arch of its first sound, and its little statue stands under it. Empty arches are grey: "Nobody starts with z-z-z yet!" Emma names new babies to fill them.

**Role play.** Royal namer. She chooses names on purpose to complete the arches.

**Reading and spelling.** First sounds (S01), letter names and alphabet order (S19), and digraph names when the sh, ch and th arches appear (S07). Free naming with the gold bell fills arches, so this supports "never refuse a name". There is no wrong answer. The arch that lights is the honest result of her first gem.

**Maths.** Count lit arches ("18 arches. How many are still grey?"). Babies under each arch make a tally (M48).

**Collecting.** Arches and statues. The second baby under an arch gets a ribbon, the fifth a flag.

**Why she keeps playing.** 26 letters, then digraph arches, then "names with ee inside". The goal moves with the phonics ladder.

**Where she might get lost.** An arch is abstract. Each arch shows its letter and a picture that starts with that sound, and plays the pure sound when touched.

**Build cost.** S.

**Fit with feedback.** Naming is her favourite act. This gives each new name a purpose and pushes her to use new letters.

## How the ideas fit together

Ideas 1, 2, 3 and 10 are cheap and fix the v2 limits directly: no cap of 12, pins as a real collection, an album that scales, and a purpose for every name. They would make a strong first v3 build around the existing egg loop. Ideas 4 and 5 bring the most variety but need new maths models and new art. Ideas 6, 8 and 9 fit best once lands exist. Idea 7 suits the time when the first babies are fully grown.

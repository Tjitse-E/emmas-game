# Learning map: Reception to Year 2

Research for v3 of Unicorn Egg Nest. This is the full list of what the game must eventually teach, in the order British schools teach it, cut into small steps a game can walk through. Written 2026-10-12. Re-based the same day on Emma's Year 1 homework workbook from NOVA (section 1.1).

## Recommendation

- **Three ladders: Reading (54 steps), Spelling (45 steps) and Maths (48 steps).** Each step is small enough to pass in 3 to 8 short sessions. At 10 to 15 minutes a day, 5 or 6 days a week, the full map takes 9 to 12 months from a Phase 2 start, or about 6 months from a Phase 4 start.
- **Year 1 follows Emma's NOVA workbook.** She is in Year 1 at NOVA, a private British-system English school, and her homework for the rest of the year is a Phase 5 workbook (90 pages). The Year 1 reading and spelling steps use its grapheme groups, its tricky words and its sentence work, in page order. Phase 2 to 4 below it, and the Phase 5 graphemes it does not cover, keep Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised order. Maths is not in the workbook and follows White Rose Maths.
- **The app is fully British English.** No Dutch word, text, sound or name pronunciation reaches the screen or the speaker. Her Dutch background only decides which English contrasts get extra practice (section 3).
- **Two layers of progress.** The ladder step says *what* she learns. The scaffold level inside a step says *how much help* she gets (gaps in the slider, dots before numerals, a visible frame). The v2 staircase (3 first tries up, 1 miss down) moves the scaffold only. A rolling window and per-item memory move the ladder. This removes the harsh v2 drop where one miss sent her back a level.
- **Every item has a memory box.** Graphemes, tricky words, names and number facts each sit in a Leitner box with growing review gaps (next session, 2, 4, 8 and 16 days). About a third of every session is review, hidden in the same game moments.
- **Plurals are a live school topic.** Emma is learning them at school now, so R19 (reading) and S10 (spelling) are school steps: never placed, and served from the end of placement in the first playable (section 5.6, spec section 3.10).
- **Place her with an in-game probe that starts at Phase 4.** She is in Year 1 and her homework is Phase 5. As an EAL reader she may not be secure on all of Phase 3 and 4, so the reading and spelling probes search from Phase 3 upward and only go lower if she misses at Phase 3 (section 5.6).
- **Her Dutch background matters in four places:** the vowels in cat, bed and cup; th, j, g, ch, v and w; Dutch letter values for ee, oo, ie, ai, oe, ui and au; and in maths the Dutch order of number words and the Dutch way of saying half past. Each has its own contrast step with English words only, and is never used as a trap before that step.

## 1. Which phonics order to follow

England requires a systematic synthetic phonics (SSP) programme in Reception and Year 1. The Department for Education keeps a list of validated programmes. Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised replaced the old 2007 Letters and Sounds for most schools that used it. Other common ones are Read Write Inc. Phonics, Jolly Phonics and Twinkl Phonics.

The order of the first graphemes differs per programme:

| Programme | First graphemes |
|---|---|
| Little Wandle (Reception Autumn 1) | s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f l |
| Read Write Inc. (Set 1) | m a s d t i n p g o c k u b f e l h sh r j v y w th z ch qu x ng nk |
| Jolly Phonics (7 groups) | s a t i p n, c k e h r m d, g o u l f b, ai j oa ie ee or, z w ng v oo oo, y x ch sh th th, qu ou oi ue er ar |

The differences matter in the first weeks only. By the end of Reception all three cover the same Phase 2 and 3 graphemes, and by the end of Year 1 the same Phase 5 alternatives. Emma's school programme is not known and is not needed: Phase 2 to 4 keep Little Wandle order, and Year 1 follows her workbook (section 1.1). The order is data in the ladder files.

**Caveats from the extraction, kept in the game.**

- The video does not show due dates, lesson order or which pages are set for which week. Page order is the default ladder order, not a known school order. Tjitse can reorder the Year 1 steps in the ladder file if homework comes in another order.
- Completion marks in the video are not proof of mastery. The workbook does not place her. The probe does (section 5.6).
- Page 21 claims that every sentence must have a noun. The game does not teach that rule.
- Page 73 matches singing with "sung". The game uses "sang" for the simple past.
- Page 80 says -er is added to an adjective. The listed words are verbs that become nouns, and the game uses the verb-to-noun rule.
- Page 72 uses "geckoes". The game leaves gecko out.
- Pages 43 and 59 print wrong plurals to reject (foxs, keyes, dresss). The game never prints a misspelling for her to choose. A wrong plural appears only when she builds one, and the slider reads it back.
- Pages 11, 44, 69 item 2 and 77 item 5 have uncertain readings in the extraction. The game does not copy those items.
- Open answers (drawings, About Me, picture sentences, independent writing) have no single answer and are not used as checked items.

**How the game uses it.** The game takes the workbook's targets, never its tasks. No worksheet layout, no tick boxes, no sorting tables, no "circle the right answer". Each target becomes an act in the unicorn world (section 4.4). Handwriting, letter formation, drawing and open writing stay out of scope. UK spellings stay (colour, favourite, mum, grey) and workbook words are used where they fit the world.

**Money in Cyprus.** Cyprus uses the euro. British schools in Cyprus teach the English money steps, but some swap pounds and pence for euros and cents. The coin values are the same set (1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200), so the game can draw either. Ask the school and make the currency a setting.

## 3. Sounds and words that are hard for a Dutch speaker

Emma is Dutch, learns English at school, and may see Dutch print at home. This section explains the risks for the design team. None of the Dutch in it goes into the app: the app is fully British English. The contrast steps that answer these risks use English words, English pictures and Elizabeth's British English clips only.

Two kinds of interference: she may **hear and say** an English sound as a Dutch one (this hurts spelling), and she may **read** a letter group with its Dutch value (this hurts reading). Main source: Collins and Mees, *The Phonetics of English and Dutch*, and the Groningen open textbook on English phonetics for Dutch learners.

### 3.1 Hearing and saying

| Area | What goes wrong | Examples | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short vowels a, e, u | Dutch has no /æ/ and no /ʌ/. cat, bet and cut can sound alike to her. Dutch short a is a back /ɑ/. | cat/bet/cut, hat/hut, pan/pen, cap/cup, bag/beg/bug | High. Hits CVC spelling from the first week. |
| Final b, d, g, v, z | Dutch makes them voiceless at the end of a word. She hears and says bed as "bet" and spells it so. | bed/bet, dog/dock, bag/back, his, buzz | High for spelling |
| th | Not in Dutch. Becomes t, d, s, z or f. | the, this, that, thin, three, moth, bath | High |
| v and w | Dutch w is between English v and w. Dutch v is often said as f. | van/wan, vet/wet, very, web | Medium |
| g | Dutch g is a throat sound. Hard /g/ only exists in loan words. | gap, dog, go, big | Medium. Watch her own speech. |
| j | Dutch j is the English y sound. | jam read and said as "yam", jug, jet | High when reading |
| r after a vowel | British English drops it (car, fork). Dutch says it. | car, star, fork, bird, her | Low for reading, medium for spelling (she may add r where there is none, or miss ar/or/er) |
| ee / i, oo (foot) / oo (moon) | Length and quality differ from Dutch. | ship/sheep, bit/beat, full/fool | Medium |
| Weak forms | to, of, a, the, was are said with a schwa in speech. | "a cup of tea" | Low |

### 3.3 Words with a Dutch twin

Some early words exist in Dutch with the same spelling and another sound or meaning. They are useful (she knows the meaning) and risky (she knows the wrong sound).

- Same spelling, different sound: is, in, bus, put, hand, arm, bed, pen, pan, lamp, tent, web, ring, kat/cat, water, school, half.
- False friends: **of** (Dutch "or", said "off"), **was** (Dutch "laundry"), **put** (Dutch "a well"), **have** (Dutch "belongings"), **door** (Dutch "through").
- Sound-alikes with other spelling: my/mij, by/bij, you/jou, do/doe, to/toe, here/hier, house/huis, mouse/muis, friend/vriend. These help reading for meaning once the English spelling is known.

### 3.4 Maths

| Area | What goes wrong | Game rule |
|---|---|---|
| Number word order | Dutch says 24 as "vier-en-twintig" (four and twenty). Dutch children write 42 for 24 more often. English teens have the same problem: four-teen says the ones first. | Teen and two-digit steps always show tens and ones as separate objects (a ten-stick and loose ones). Feedback names the tens first. A reversal (42 for 24) is a tracked error type. |
| thirteen / thirty | th plus a stress difference that is hard to hear | Never offer 13 and 30 as distractors before step M20. Then give them their own contrast moment. |
| Half past | Dutch "half drie" is 2:30, "half to three". English "half past two" is 2:30. A Dutch child will read "half three" style times one hour off. | Time steps say "half past" only, always show the hour hand between the two numbers, and track one-hour-off errors. |
| Money | Cyprus uses euros. School may use pounds. | Currency is a setting. |

### 3.5 Rules for the game

1. Before a contrast step is passed, interference pairs never appear as distractors (as in v2).
2. Each pair then gets an explicit contrast step (R11, R20, S06, M20 below). The spec moves the a, e, u pairs forward to two extra steps, R04D and S03D, right after R04 and S03 (spec section 3.8). From then on the pairs are normal distractors.
3. Interference errors are logged with their own tag. They show on the parent page as "Dutch pairs", so Tjitse can tell a Dutch-sound slip from a gap in phonics.
4. Elizabeth always gives the British model after the act ("Bed. Bed has a d at the end."). Emma never hears an accented model for a learning sound (v2 rule).
5. Pair words are English words with English pictures ("pan" and "pen", "boat" and "boot"). The game never shows or says a Dutch word, never explains a mistake by naming Dutch, and never gives help in Dutch. The tag `dutch` and the "Dutch pairs" label exist only in the event log and the parent corner.

## 4. The ladders

Codes: R = reading, S = spelling, M = maths. **School** gives the Little Wandle term (Rec, Y1, Y2 with Au1, Au2, Sp1, Sp2, Su1, Su2) or the White Rose block. **WB** in the same column gives the pages of Emma's workbook that the step covers. **DU** marks a step with a contrast that is hard for a Dutch speaker. **Hook** is one example of how the step can live in the unicorn world. The hooks are suggestions for the design work, not decisions. The Year 1 workbook hooks (section 4.4) are the ones the spec builds.

Reading leads spelling by one to three steps. Decoding a grapheme comes before choosing it from a tray.

## 5. Mastery and review

### 5.1 Two layers

| Layer | Moves what | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Scaffold | The support inside a step (slider gap 500, 300, 0 ms; dots, dots and numeral, numeral only; frame visible, hidden) | v2 staircase, softened: 3 first tries in a row up, **2 misses in a row** down. One miss is too harsh (v2 audit). |
| Ladder | The content (R12 to R13) | Step mastery rule below |

### 5.2 What the game records per checked moment

- Item id (grapheme, word, tricky word, name, fact such as "7+3", skill such as "half past").
- Result: first try, miss, or helped. Replay of the goal does not count against her (v2 rule).
- Decision time: from the moment the answer is possible to her first touch of the chosen object. Not total time, because dragging and animations differ per moment.
- Error type: wrong vowel, Dutch pair, reversal (42 for 24), one-hour-off, off by one, and so on.

### 5.3 Item memory (spaced review)

Every item has a Leitner box from 0 to 5.

| Box | Next review | Promote when | On a miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 New | same session, after 2 to 4 other items | first try | stays 0 |
| 1 | next session | first try | stays 1 |
| 2 | 2 days | first try | to box 1 |
| 3 | 4 days | first try | to box 1 |
| 4 | 8 days | first try, and fast for fluency items | to box 2 |
| 5 | 16 days | first try and fast | to box 3 |
| Retired | spot check every 6 weeks | | to box 3 |

- **Helped counts as a miss** for the box, but the step still finishes, so she is never stuck.
- **Fluency items** are tricky words, Phase 2 and 3 graphemes, bonds within 10, doubles, facts to 20 and the 2, 5, 10 tables. These need speed as well as accuracy before box 5: a decision time below her own median for that item type, with a fixed ceiling of about 3 seconds. No timer is shown, and speed never affects rewards.
- A Dutch-pair miss moves the item down only when the matching contrast step (R11, R20, S06, M20) is passed. Before that, pair items never appear.
- Only two or three new items per strand enter in one session. New items always come with full scaffold.

### 5.4 Step mastery (moving up the ladder)

A step is **passed** when all three hold:

1. The scaffold is at its top level for that step.
2. At least 12 of the last 15 checked items of this step (80 percent) were first tries, spread over at least two sessions on different days.
3. Every item of the step has been a first try in at least two different sessions (box 2 or higher).

A passed step is **secure** when all its items reach box 4. The parent page shows passed and secure per step.

If review accuracy on a passed step falls below 70 percent over 10 items, the step is reopened for one or two sessions. In the world this is a returning visitor or a lost item, never a "you forgot" message.

### 5.5 What a session looks like

A night of 10 to 15 minutes, as in v2: egg, hatch, name, wish, play, bedtime. Every checked moment draws its item from a queue:

- About 50 percent from the current step of that strand.
- About 30 percent due reviews, oldest due first.
- About 20 percent mixed items from passed steps, so old skills meet each other (a wish word from R13 next to one from R30). This interleaving is what stops "too easy" without making it harder.

Per night roughly 8 to 12 reading items, 4 to 6 spelling items (names count) and 8 to 12 maths items. At 5 or 6 nights a week that is about 50 to 70 checked items per strand per week, enough to pass one step in one to two weeks on average.

### 5.6 Placement

In the first visits each strand runs a hidden probe. It gives two items from a step and moves by halving: both first tries means jump up half the remaining distance, any miss means jump down. After 8 to 10 items per strand it settles. She starts one step below the probe result, with full scaffold, so the first nights feel easy and successful. Tjitse can override any strand in the parent corner.

Where the probe starts for Emma. She is in Year 1 and her homework is Phase 5, so reading and spelling search from Phase 3 upward:

| Strand | Lowest step searched | First probe | Highest step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | R13 (Phase 3) | R21 (Phase 4) | The top loaded step |
| Spelling | S09 (Phase 3) | S12 (Phase 4) | The top loaded step |
| Maths | M01 | M10 (her v2 level, and Year 1 autumn in White Rose) | The top loaded step |

If she misses both items at the lowest step, the search opens down to R01 or S01 and goes on. Steps below her result are marked as placed. Contrast steps below her result are not placed: they run beside her current step until each passes (the spec, section 3.5).

**School steps.** A step she is learning at school right now is flagged `school` in the ladder file. Today that is R19 and S10, the plurals. The probe leaves school steps out of its search and never places them, because a pass in the probe is not a pass at school pace. From the end of placement, usually visit 1 or 2, the school step takes the current-step share in every place that can serve it, starting at full scaffold, until it passes by the rule in section 5.4. In reading her current step waits about three play weeks while plurals are open. Tjitse clears the flag, or marks the step placed, when school moves on.

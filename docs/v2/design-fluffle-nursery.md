# Fluffle Nursery v2: design

## Core fantasy and role

Emma is Nurse Emma, who runs the Fluffle Nursery in the treehouse. A cloud brings a basket to the window. Inside is a baby Fluffle. Emma wakes it, builds its name from sounds, and looks after it for three play days: granting its wishes, feeding it, bathing it and putting it to bed. Each night the baby grows one stage (baby, little, big, grown), and Emma sees the change the next morning. On the fourth morning it is grown, its own fur pattern appears and it moves into the Fluffle Tree, her growing family. That same morning a new basket arrives. Pom, the v1 baby, already lives in the Tree as a grown Fluffle, so Emma meets an old friend.

The fiction makes the learning necessary:

- **Reading.** Fluffles cannot talk. Their wishes appear as written words in a thought bubble. Only the Nurse can read Fluffle bubbles. Elizabeth never says a wish word before Emma has tried to read it, and the slider never runs the sounds together into the word.
- **Maths.** Feeding and bathing need exact amounts, and the result shows on the baby's body. A wrong plate shows ghost berries against the tummy, with a gap or a spill, and the baby will not eat it. A rinse jug that is not filled exactly to the star line will not wash the mud off.
- **What Emma loves:** role play (the nurse with an apron and a crown badge), fantasy (cloud baskets, the Tree), naming (every baby), nursing (food, bath, bed) and collecting (Fluffles, stickers, room gifts).
- **What makes it different from Unicorn Egg Nest.** Its identity is the challenges (the wish cupboard, the plate, the rinse jug) and the Tree family. Arrival and naming are kept short. Naming uses one component shared with Unicorn Egg Nest.

One rule applies to every moment, so the goal is always obvious:

1. A body cue shows the need. A wish is a sparkle with the baby pointing at its bubble. Hungry is a rumbling tummy and a tummy rub (food needs only, which fixes the v1 bug). Muddy is mud splats. Sleepy is a yawn.
2. Elizabeth says one goal sentence, then one how sentence. Spoken lines are 8 words or fewer where possible.
3. Exactly one thing glows. Everything else is dimmed and cannot be used until that step is done. The only exception is tickling the baby, which is allowed at any time and never moves it.
4. The first time a mechanic appears, the hint hand shows it once.

There is no decay and no guilt. A Fluffle never gets sad because Emma stayed away.

## The round (one nursery day, about 4 to 5 minutes)

Below is day 1 of a new baby, which is the demo session. The example baby is called Mip. Only 5 spoken lines contain the baby's name (see Audio needs). Every other line says "your Fluffle", so names are never spliced in and no name ever sounds like a wish word.

**0. Play (5 s).** A big Play button unlocks audio on iOS. The round's clips are decoded into WebAudio buffers before anything plays.
> "Hello, Nurse Emma! Welcome to the Fluffle Nursery."

**1. Arrival (15 s).** A cloud drifts to the round window and leaves the v1 wicker basket on the sill. There is a knock and the basket glows.
> "Look! A cloud brought a basket. Tap the basket."

Each tap makes the basket wobble. On the third tap a curled baby pops out, squeaks and hops to the rug.
> "A baby Fluffle!"
> "Fluffles can't talk. Read their bubbles."

**2. Naming (50 s).** Pom swings down from the Tree first and shows how a name is made.
> "This is Pom. Watch!"

- The hand drops three gems into the name board: p, o, m. Each one plays its sound.
- The hand slides the star. The sounds play one by one, then Pom squeaks and Elizabeth says "Pom!". Pom waves and swings back up. The gems pop back into their trays.

Now Emma's turn. The board has three boxes and the first box glows. Under it are three gem trays: blue start sounds, pink middle sounds and blue end sounds. Only the tray for the glowing box can be used.
> "Your baby needs a name. You choose!"
> "Tap a gem to hear it. Put one in the box."

- Each gem plays its pure sound when tapped. Emma drags a gem into the glowing box and the next box glows.
- When all three boxes are full, the star under them glows.
> "Slide the star, and listen."
- The sounds play as the star passes each gem. Then Elizabeth says the name ("Mip!").
> "Do you like it? Tap the heart."
- Tapping a gem in a box pops it out so she can change it.
- On the heart:
> "Hello, Mip!"
- The baby spins and giggles. A name sign drops onto its cot.

For every later baby the boxes come pre-filled with a free name. She can change any gem or just slide and tap the heart. Pom's demonstration is shown only for the first baby.

**3. Wish 1, reading (40 s).** The baby sparkles, points at its bubble and bounces. The bubble shows a word in lowercase with a sound dot under each letter and the star slider under the word. The Wish Cupboard on the wall is shut with a small lock.
> "Your Fluffle wants something. Read the bubble."
> (first time only) "Tap the dots. Then slide the star."

- The hand demonstrates once.
- When the slide is finished, the lock pops and the cupboard opens onto a nursery shelf with 6 items.
> "Find the thing you read." (the hand points from the bubble to the shelf)
- Items on the shelf are silent. Tapping one only makes it wiggle.
- Emma picks an item off the shelf and carries it across the room to the baby. Nothing happens until she drops it on the baby.
> (first carry only) "Give it to your Fluffle."
- **Right item:** the baby uses it (wears the cap, eats the bun, hugs the dog) and squeaks the word in a high Fluffle echo.
> "Yes! A cap. Your Fluffle loves it."

  A sticker flies to the book icon. Which sticker she gets depends on the help used (see Collection).
- **Wrong item:** the baby sniffs and shakes its head with a soft "hmm". The item flies back, the cupboard shuts and the shelf shuffles.
> "Not that one. Read the bubble again."

  The help ladder is in Reading.

**4. Feeding, maths (45 s).** The tummy rumbles and the baby rubs it. A round tummy window opens on the baby and shows a ten-frame with some spots already full (breakfast). A jar holds about 15 berries in a messy heap. An empty plate glows.
> "Your Fluffle is hungry! Fill up the tummy."
> "Put berries on the plate. Give it to your Fluffle."

- Each berry makes a soft plop on the plate. **Nobody counts while Emma places berries.**
- She drags the plate to the baby. The baby only eats an exact plate. It never eats part of a plate.
- **Just right:** the baby eats one berry at a time into the empty spots while Elizabeth counts on from the full spots: "Seven, eight, nine, ten!"
> "Just right! The tummy is full."

  Purr, heart.
- **Too few:** ghost berries from the plate float into the tummy spots. The spots that stay empty blink. The baby rubs its tummy and the whole plate comes back.
> "Not enough. Look at the empty spots."
- **Too many:** ghost berries fill the spots and the extras spill over the edge of the tummy window. The baby puffs its cheeks and the whole plate comes back.
> "Too many! They won't fit."
- In both wrong cases she adds or removes berries and hands the plate over again. The voice stays silent during the ghost display.

**5. Wish 2 (35 s).** If wish 1 was right first time, wish 2 is a golden wish: the bubble shimmers gold and the word is one level above hers.
> "Ooh, a golden wish! Read the bubble."

- Success gives a golden sticker and a bigger celebration.
- Failure turns the bubble back to normal without comment. After that it runs the normal help ladder. It never costs anything visible.
- If wish 1 was not right first time, wish 2 is a normal wish at her level.

**6. Bath, maths (45 s).** After eating, the baby rolls in the garden doorway and comes back with three mud splats.
> "Oh dear, muddy! Take your Fluffle to the bath."

- The tub glows. Emma drags the baby in (the v1 move).
- A rinse jug stands on the edge of the tub. It has 10 lines and a star at the top line. Eight soap bubbles bob in place in the tub, each with a number.
> "Fill the jug to the star. Use two bubbles."
- Emma drags a bubble to the jug. It pours in that many lines of water with a soft glug, without a voice count. Then she drags a second bubble.
- **Exactly to the star:** the jug glows.
> "Seven and three make ten!"
> "Pour it on the mud!"

  She drags the jug onto the baby and one splat washes off.
- **Below the star:** the empty lines blink.
> "Four and three make seven. We need ten."

  The water drains back and both bubbles float back to their places.
- **Over the star:** the water spills over the top with a splash.
> "Six and seven make thirteen. We need ten."

  Both bubbles float back to their places.
- When all three splats are gone, the leftover bubbles are free to pop for a few seconds with giggles. This is play with no maths, and Emma liked it in v1. The baby shakes and puffs up to twice its size.
> "Clean and fluffy!"

**7. Bedtime, reading at review level (35 s).** The window turns to dusk. The baby yawns. A toy basket with a small lock sits on the rug, and the bubble shows a toy word.
> "Sleepy time. Your Fluffle wants a cuddly toy."
> "Read the bubble."

- The basket opens only after the slide (the same gate as the wishes). It holds three toys that share sounds, for example dog, doll and duck.
- Right toy: the baby hugs it.
> "Take your Fluffle to bed."

  The cot with the name sign glows. Emma drags the baby in. The room goes dark, stars come out, z's float up and the baby snores.
> "Night night, Mip. Sleep tight."
- Wrong toy:
> "Not that one. Read the bubble again."

**8. Night and nursery book (25 s).** The fifth heart fills.
> "Your Fluffle will grow in the night."

- The book flies open. Today's stickers land with a swoosh.
> "New stickers for your book!"
- It turns to the Fluffle Tree page: Pom (grown, peach), Mip (baby, mint) and six silhouettes.
- The nursery stays dark with the baby asleep.
> "Come back tomorrow!"
- The next day unlocks on the next calendar day. If a parent has raised the session limit, a moon button appears instead.
> "Tap the moon for tomorrow."

**Days 2 to 4 (same engine).**

- Morning: the baby sleeps in its cot.
> "Good morning, Nurse Emma! Tap the cot."
> "Good morning, Mip!"

  The baby wakes up visibly bigger than yesterday, with new ears or a fluffier tail.
> "Look! Your Fluffle is bigger!"
- Days 2 and 3 run the needs as above, with new words and numbers drawn from her levels.
- Day 4 morning: the baby wakes up grown.
> "Mip is all grown up!"
> "Look at the new fur!"

  The pattern and a tiny crown appear, with one sparkle gem on the crown for each golden heart she earned with this baby.
> "Off to the Fluffle Tree!"

  Mip leaves a room gift. Emma picks one of three pictured gifts and it appears in the room. Then a cloud brings the next basket, and day 1 of the new baby starts.
- **Session limit:** the default is 1 day per sitting. A parent can raise it. When the limit is reached:
> "The Fluffles are sleepy now. See you tomorrow!"

**Play between steps.** After each need, the baby has a short happy moment. During that moment Emma can lift and bounce it. Tickling works at any time. Lifting does not work during a need, so the drop target never moves.

**Hints.** The speaker button (top left) repeats the current goal line and does not count as a miss. The idle hint repeats the how line and the hand shows how to do it. It waits 8 seconds in reading steps and 15 seconds in maths steps. It stops once Emma has acted in the step, and it is never spoken while the plate has berries on it or the jug has water in it. Hints never say what the answer is and never name a place or a category.

## Reading

### Skills

| Skill | Where | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| Letter sounds (grapheme to phoneme) | Sound dots, naming gems | Not checked. It is a tool. Each tap is logged. |
| Blending CVC words, then digraphs and long vowels | Wish 1, wish 2, golden wish | The item dropped on the baby must match the word: `item.word === wish.word`. Only first-try results count for progress. |
| Blending at review level | Bedtime toy | Same check. Not logged as a Blend trial, because it is always review level. |
| Blending made-up words | Naming (the "alien words" of the Year 1 phonics check) | Not checked. She hears her own blend. |
| Reading for meaning (full game) | Phrases ("red cap" against "red cup" and "pink cap"), sentences with tricky words ("I want the big bun") | Same item check. The shelf holds items that differ in one attribute. |

Fluffle Nursery stays decoding-first. Naming is its only encoding moment. Spelling a spoken word belongs to Pippa's Word Wand, so the Wish Hatch idea is not used here.

### Bubble display

- Lowercase letters in Andika (SIL Open Font License), self-hosted. It has a single-storey a and g like school handwriting.
- One dot under each single letter. A short bar under a digraph (UK convention).
- Tapping a letter plays its pure sound. The bubble never plays the whole word on tap.
- Tricky words (full game) are drawn in a different colour with a heart over the tricky part. For their first 3 appearances, tapping one says the whole word.

### Slider

- The star follows her finger, but each sound starts only when the previous one has ended plus a fixed gap: 500 ms at sub-step (a), 300 ms from sub-step (b). If she slides faster, the star waits at the next letter until its sound may play.
- Sounds never overlap and are never crossfaded. Separate phonemes do not sound like a word, and the game does not pretend they do. Emma does the blending in her head.
- The slide never says the whole word.

### R1 word sets

These are Phase 2 graphemes only: s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f l ff ll ss.

Every tray has one item that differs from the target only in the first sound and one that differs only in the final sound. Where a picturable word exists, a third item differs only in the vowel. So the first sound plus the vowel is never enough, and in most trays every sound is needed.

| Target | Only first sound differs | Only vowel differs | Only final sound differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| cat | rat | | cap |
| hat | cat | hut | ham |
| cap | map | cup | cat |
| map | tap | mop | man |
| man | fan | | map |
| bun | sun | bin | bus |
| bug | rug | bag | bun |
| bin | fin | bun | bib |
| fin | bin | fan | fig |
| fig | pig | | fin |
| log | dog | leg | lock |
| lock | sock | | log |
| dog | log | | doll |
| mug | rug | | mud |
| lip | hip | | lid |

**Shelf.** The shelf holds the target, its one-difference items and fillers up to 6 items (8 at sub-step c). Fillers are other words at the same level that share at most one sound with the target.

**Pairs that never appear together:** cap and hat, cup and mug, dog and pup, rug and mat, pot and pan, rock and stone, bed and cot. These pairs would make the picture ambiguous. This is the lesson from Word Wand's "unclear which word" problem.

**Words that are not used:** tub (it is the bath in the room), can (a "tin" in the UK), bed (the room has a cot), pin (a drawing pin or a sewing pin), rock (a child says "stone"), peg and pup (weak vocabulary for a Dutch speaker).

**Bedtime cuddly toys** (review level, 3 toys in a locked basket): dog, doll, duck, cat, rat, bat. Sets share sounds: dog with doll and duck, cat with rat and bat.

### Later levels (R2 to R4 are in the demo; R5 to R7 are full game)

Example sets list the target and its one-difference items. The grapheme order follows Emma's school scheme once Tjitse has confirmed it.

| Level | Graphemes | Example sets |
|---|---|---|
| R2 | + j v w x y z zz qu | van (fan, man), fox (box, fog), jet (net, vet), zip (lip, hip), wig (pig, fig), jam (ham) |
| R3 | + ch sh th ng nk | chip (ship, chop, chin), shop (chop, ship), king (ring, wing), ring (king, wing), moth (mop), fish (dish) |
| R4 | ai ee igh oa oo ar or | sheep (ship, sheet), goat (boat, coat, goal), boat (coat, goat, boot), tail (nail, sail), car (jar), light (night), corn (horn) |
| R5 | clusters | frog/crab/drum/plum, nest/tent/belt/lamp, tree/three |
| R6 | two-word phrases | red cap/red cup/pink cap/big cap |
| R7 | sentences with tricky words I, the, to, go, no, want | "I want the red cap." |

Few R2 words have a picturable final-sound partner. R2 trays test the new first sound, and the final-sound check comes from R1 trays in the same round.

Plural s (cups, pigs) joins feeding from R2 for combined needs ("5 cups") in the full game.

### Help ladder for each wish (one step per wrong pick)

1. The baby sniffs and shakes its head with "Not that one. Read the bubble again." The cupboard shuts and the shelf shuffles. The letters pulse one by one with their sounds. She must slide again (at sub-step d: tap every dot) before the cupboard reopens.
2. Elizabeth plays the approved sound clips with 500 ms gaps, then again with 150 ms gaps. She never says the word. The fillers fade out. The one-difference items stay, so Emma still has to blend.
3. "It says cap." The right item glows and the hand points at it. She gets no sticker (the silhouette stays in the book), and the wish counts as a miss.

### Why it cannot be guessed

- **The cupboard stays locked until the slide is done.** This fixes v1, where `G.slid` was never checked.
- **No two sounds decide.** Every tray has a first-sound and a final-sound item, and most have a vowel item too. She has to blend the whole word.
- **It is not a picture quiz.** Six items sit on a shelf among the nursery things, and she carries her choice across the room. A random pick works about 1 time in 6.
- **There is no category cue.** One cupboard holds food, toys and clothes. The body cue for a wish is a generic sparkle. No hint names a shelf or a box.
- **Items are silent during a wish.** If they said their names, reading would become listening.
- **Every round is different.** Words, sets and shelf positions are drawn per round. A word does not come back within 2 rounds, and positions reshuffle after every wrong pick.
- **Guessing costs what she cares about.** Only first-try reading gives a shiny sticker or a golden heart. Help step 3 gives no sticker at all. Golden hearts show on the grown Fluffle's crown in the Tree.

### Vocabulary (Emma's first language is Dutch)

- A word is only used if Emma knows it in English.
- **Picture check.** `picture-check.html` shows a grid of every item picture. Tjitse shows each one to Emma, taps it to hear the word, and marks it known or unknown. It is stored in the same save as the game. A word is only used in the demo after it has passed the picture check. This is a precondition, not an option.
- Words also come back through the sticker book (tapping a sticker says the word) and through the right answer, which Elizabeth always names.
- The full game adds a morning parcel that introduces new items by voice one session before they can be wished for.

## Maths

### Skills

- Numeral to quantity up to 10, then up to 20.
- Counting out an exact amount.
- Counting on.
- Number bonds to 5, 10 and 20.
- Adding within 10, then 20, and taking away (full game).

### Feeding ladder (Count strand)

| Level | What the baby shows | The maths |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Numeral bubble with fading dots ("4"). The tummy is closed. | Count out 3 to 6 |
| F2 | Numeral only, 5 to 10, messy heap | Count out a set above what she can see at a glance |
| F3 | Ten-frame tummy, 3, 4 or 6 spots full (demo start) | Count the 4 to 7 empty spots, then count out that many |
| F4 | Tummy covered by fur. A sign shows the numeral "7" next to one berry icon. The tummy outline is marked "10". | A bond to 10 from the number alone |
| F5 (full game) | Pom visits: "Pom wants 3. Your Fluffle wants 4." One plate for both. | Adding within 10. Short: the last one stays hungry. Too many: berries left over. |
| F6 (full game) | "There are 9 berries. Your Fluffle wants 6." | Taking away |
| F7 (full game) | Big Fluffle with two ten-frames | Bonds and adding within 20 |

At F3 and F4 the number of full spots is never 5 or 10, so the gap is never on a five-boundary that she can see at a glance.

At F4 Elizabeth says: "Your Fluffle ate seven. The tummy holds ten."

**Check:** `plate.length === needed`, checked when the plate is handed over. The baby only eats an exact plate. The first hand-over is the trial. A later exact plate still feeds the baby, but the need counts as a miss.

**Help ladder:**

1. After 1 wrong plate: "Look at the empty spots."
2. After 2 wrong plates: the plate shows ten-frame slots.
3. After 3 wrong plates: the empty tummy spots pulse one at a time, and she still counts them herself.

The spots are drawn from the number, not hard-coded.

### Bath ladder (Bonds strand)

| Level | Rinse jug | Bubbles |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | 5 lines | 6 bubbles, numerals with dot patterns |
| B2 | 10 lines | 8 bubbles, numerals with ten-frame dots (demo start) |
| B3 | 10 lines | 8 bubbles, numerals only |
| B4 (full game) | 11 to 19 lines | Pairs such as 10+4 and 9+5 |
| B5 (full game) | 20 lines | Pairs such as 13+7 |

**Check:** `a + b === target` after the second bubble is poured. Each pour of two bubbles is one Bonds trial.

**Bubble generation.** Each bath has 3 correct pairs that share no bubble, so a correct pair is always left until all splats are gone. At B2 and B3 there are also 2 lonely bubbles. A lonely bubble's partner is not on the board, and the two lonely bubbles do not make the target together (for target 10: one or two of 1, 5 and 9, never 1 and 9 together, never two 5s). At B1 the 6 bubbles are the 3 pairs only. Near misses (4+5 against 10) come from the mix. With 8 bubbles there are 28 possible pairs, so a random pair works about 1 time in 9.

**Help ladder:**

1. After 2 wrong pours, ten-frame dots appear next to the jug's lines.
2. After 3 wrong pours, one bubble of a correct pair glows. Emma still finds its partner.

### Why it cannot be guessed or handed over

- **The voice is silent while she places berries or pours.** This fixes v1, where she could stop at "three". Counting only happens afterwards, as feedback, when the baby eats an exact plate.
- **Dumping everything does not work.** The baby never eats part of a plate, so a full plate or one berry at a time fails just like a wrong guess.
- **She has to count.** Berries lie in a heap, not in rows. From F2 the targets are 5 or more. At F3 the gap is 4 to 7.
- **The levels move away from counting.** From F4 the tummy is covered, so counting spots no longer works. From B3 the bubbles have no dots.
- **The numbers change.** Targets and bond pairs come from a pool weighted towards the ones she misses.
- **The result shows on the body.** Ghost berries show the gap or the spill. The jug shows the gap or overflows. Mud stays until the jug is exactly full.

## Difficulty and adaptation

**Strands.** Blend, Count and Bonds. The full game adds Phrase, Tricky and AddTake. Each strand has its own level, made of small sub-steps.

**Blend sub-steps inside one grapheme group:**

- (a) The slide plays sounds with 500 ms gaps.
- (b) The slide plays sounds with 300 ms gaps.
- (c) Eight items on the shelf instead of six.
- (d) No slider. Only the single sound dots, and she must tap every dot before the cupboard opens.
- (e) No sound dots at all (full game).
- After the last sub-step, the next grapheme group starts at (a).

**Start level.** Tjitse confirms the school's phonics scheme and phase before the build. The start level follows that phase. If it is not known yet, Blend starts at R3 (a), Count at F3 and Bonds at B2. The level override in the parent panel can change it at any time.

**Staircase.** This sets the challenge, so the reading and maths are felt.

- **Rule.** 3 first-try successes in a row move one sub-step up. One miss moves one sub-step down. This settles at about 80% first-try success: challenges are real, and she keeps winning. The staircase carries over between days.
- **Trials per day.** Blend: wish 1, and wish 2 when it is not golden. Count: the first plate of the feeding. Bonds: every pour in the bath.
- **What counts as a miss.** Anything other than a first-try success. Using the speaker button is not a miss. A help step that narrows the choice is.
- **No warm start.** Count and Blend have fewer than 3 trials per day, so every trial counts.
- **Not in the staircase.** The golden wish and bedtime.
- **Placement (full game).** The first 10 trials of each strand use 1-up/1-down, so she reaches her level fast.

**Guards inside a round:**

- After two misses in a row, the next need of any strand is given one sub-step below her level. This does not move the staircase.
- Wish 2 is only golden when wish 1 was right first time.
- Bedtime is always at review level, so the day ends on a success.
- The golden wish is the only need above her level.
- At most one new mechanic per session:
  - day 1: naming, the slider and cupboard, the plate, the rinse jug
  - full game: sharing with Pom from the second Fluffle, tricks on the grown day

**Word mix per round (full game).** About 70% current level, 20% review (Leitner boxes: a miss sends a word back to box 1, so it returns next session) and 10% the golden wish.

**Confusion tracking (full game).** Each miss logs the item she picked against the target. Pairs typical for a Dutch speaker get extra sets, never two in a row. Only pairs where both words can be pictured are kept, because a silent picture choice cannot detect a spoken error.

| Pair | Typical error |
|---|---|
| van / fan | v read as f |
| log / lock | Final g and k not told apart |
| seed / seat, card / cart | Final d and t not told apart |
| pan / pen, man / men | a and e not told apart |
| boot / boat | Dutch oo read as English oa |
| sheep / ship | ee and i not told apart |
| tree / three | th said as t |

**Parent panel.** Hold two fingers on the top-right corner for 3 seconds, then answer a sum aimed at an adult (for example 14 + 9) on a number pad. In the demo it shows a level override per strand and a reset. The full game adds:

- each strand's level and the first-try rate per session
- the confusion list
- the session limit
- a link to the picture check

## Collection and rewards

- **Hearts.** Five per day, one per need. Bedtime fills the fifth. A first-try success makes the heart golden. Hearts are never taken away. A full row means the baby grows in the night.
- **Growth.** Baby (curled ears), little (ears up), big (fluffy tail) and grown (fur pattern and a tiny crown, for Emma's love of royalty). The baby grows overnight and Emma sees it in the morning, so "come back and see it grow" is true.
- **Golden hearts last.** Each golden heart with a baby becomes a sparkle gem on its crown when it is grown. In the Tree, the Fluffles she read well for sparkle the most.
- **Fluffle Tree.** Pom is in it from the start.
  - There are 8 kinds, each with its own colour, ear shape, pattern and squeak pitch: peach, lilac, mint, sky, lemon, rose, cloud and moon.
  - Each new basket brings a kind she does not have yet. Missing kinds show as silhouettes.
  - Tapping a Fluffle in the Tree plays "Hello, {name}!" and its happy animation.
  - Names never repeat across Fluffles.
- **Sticker book ("Mip's Things").**
  - A wish read right first time gives a shiny sticker. A wish read right after one or two wrong picks gives a plain sticker. A wish that needed help step 3 gives no sticker, and the silhouette stays.
  - Tapping a sticker says the word, so the book is also her picture dictionary.
  - Golden wishes give a golden sticker.
  - There is one page per sound group, with silhouettes for words she has not met yet.
  - Full game: each sticker has two star slots. A star is earned when the word is read right first time on a different day. Two stars make the sticker gold.
- **Room gifts.** Each grown Fluffle leaves a gift. Emma picks one of three: a swing, a star lamp, a rainbow rug, a music box or a mobile. All are drawn in SVG with no emoji. A full sticker page unlocks an extra gift.
- **Next-thing teaser.** The book's last page shows one silhouette of what is next: the next growth stage, the next basket or the next gift.
- **Saving.**
  - `localStorage` under a versioned key, with every read and write wrapped in try/catch.
  - It holds: Fluffles (name, kind, stage, day, golden hearts), stickers, levels, known words and the date of the last played day. The full game adds Leitner boxes, confusions, sticker stars and gifts.
  - The game is installed as a Home Screen web app so Safari does not clear the data after 7 days. Home Screen storage is separate from Safari's, so it must be installed before the first real session, and the picture check is done inside the installed app. The README says so.
  - A small JSON save on odin comes later.
- **Full game only:**
  - a trick book of action words (hop, hug, nap, dig, sit, run) read on the grown day
  - a crown choice at the grown stage
  - a weekly Fluffle party with sharing maths

## Audio needs

### Pipeline

- A script in `scripts/` reads `ELEVENLABS_API` from `.env` itself and writes to the v2 idea folder's `audio/`. The key never reaches client code or git.
- Every clip uses voice Elizabeth with one fixed model and fixed voice settings.
- ffmpeg trims silence, normalises loudness to about -16 LUFS and saves mono files.
- `audio-check.html` lists every clip with "ok" and "redo" buttons. Letter sounds play next to their carrier word and next to the school scheme's reference recording (Little Wandle or Jolly Phonics, whichever the school uses). The reference recordings stay local and are never shipped or committed.
- Every clip passes Tjitse's ear check before it ships. There are no spot checks.
- The game preloads each round's clips into AudioBuffers before the round starts. There is no fallback voice.

### Letter sounds (the main complaint, redone)

**Demo set (about 39 sounds):** s a t p i n m d g o c/k e u r h b f l, the naming gem z, then j v w x y qu, ch sh th (voiced and unvoiced) ng nk, and ai ee igh oa oo (long and short) ar or.

**Full game:** about 45 sounds, adding ur ow oi ear air er.

**Rules:**

- Pure sounds only. "b", never "buh". "a" is /æ/ as in "ant", never "ah".
- Never a letter name.
- Plain text-to-speech of a single letter is ruled out.

**Method per sound class (default):**

- **Continuants** (s m n f l r v z sh th): about 500 ms, cut from the held part of stretched carriers ("sssun", "mmmop").
- **Short vowels:** about 300 ms, cut from vowel-first words (ant, egg, in, on, up).
- **Long vowels and vowel digraphs** (ai ee igh oa oo ar or): about 400 ms, cut from carrier words.
- **Stops** (p t k b d g): cut at the burst, with no voiced vowel after it. Voiced stops keep the short voicing before the burst. Under 120 ms.
- **h:** a short breath of about 150 ms, cut from a held "hhhat" before the vowel starts.
- **j and ch:** affricates, cut at the end of the friction, before the vowel starts.
- **w and y:** short glides of about 150 ms, cut before the vowel quality appears.
- **x, qu and nk:** built from approved parts (k+s, k+w, ng+k).

**Fallback order for a sound that fails the ear check:**

1. Re-cut from a different carrier word.
2. Retry with phoneme tags on a model that supports them (check ElevenLabs' current docs).
3. A speaker with British phonics pronunciation (for example from Emma's school) records the sound, and the ElevenLabs Voice Changer converts it to Elizabeth.

**Ear check.** A sound that has not passed it blocks any game code that uses it.

## Risks

- **Phoneme quality is still poor.** It is the top complaint. The ear check against the school's reference recordings is a hard gate, with the per-class method and the fallback order behind it.
- **Mismatch with school.** A different grapheme order or pronunciation would confuse her. Tjitse confirms the school's phonics scheme and phase before the build. The start level and the R2 to R5 order follow it.
- **Reading level too low or too high.** The demo covers R1 to R4 and starts at the confirmed phase (R3 if unknown). Watch whether wish 1 feels like a real challenge, and use the level override if not.
- **Vocabulary gap.** Emma can blend "log" perfectly and still not know what a log is. The picture check is a precondition for every word. Ambiguous pictures are dropped, and confusable pairs never share a shelf.
- **Guessing by elimination.** The locked cupboard, one-difference items, a 6-item shelf, silent items and stickers that depend on reading all work against it. Watch Emma play. If she drags without sliding, add a 2-second wait before the cupboard reopens.
- **Few trials per strand.** Blend and Count get 1 or 2 trials a day, so the staircase moves over days, not within a day. The level override stays in the parent panel. If it is too slow in the build, add a third wish on days 2 and 3.
- **The round is too long.** Day 1 is the longest at about 4.5 minutes. Time it with playwright-cli and with Emma. If it is too long, skip Pom's naming demonstration after the first play.
- **Art volume.** R1 to R4 needs about 90 item pictures in SVG. Draw R1 first and add R2 to R4 only after the picture check, so no art is wasted on unknown words.
- **Ear-check volume.** About 600 clips in the demo. All are checked, in batches per category. If name quality varies, cut the name set to 20.
- **Funny wrong reactions can invite wrong picks.** Wrong reactions stay short and mild. Right reactions are the bigger show.
- **Lost progress.** Safari can clear storage, and Home Screen storage is separate from Safari's. Losing her named Fluffles would hurt more than any mechanic. Install the game on the Home Screen before the first real session, and add a server save on odin for the built version.
- **Overlap with Unicorn Egg Nest and Pippa's Word Wand.** Arrival, naming and nursing overlap with the Egg Nest, and the sticker book with Word Wand. Naming is one shared component and is short after the first baby. Fluffle Nursery's identity is the wish, feeding and bath challenges and the Tree family. Whether the three games share one sticker book is decided when the games are built.

## Rejected critique

All 25 points are applied. Four are applied in a different form than the critique proposed.

- **11, blend made from segmented text ("mmm-aaa-p").** Not used. Text-to-speech of segmented text is unpredictable and would bring back the "buh" sounds. Help step 2 plays the approved letter-sound clips with shrinking gaps instead, so every sound in it has passed the ear check.
- **14, F4 sign with berry icons.** The sign shows the numeral with one berry icon, not seven icons. Seven icons can be counted, and F4 exists to make her use the bond to 10 instead of counting.
- **9, example name "Zib".** "Zib" cannot be built, because b is not an end gem. The example is "Mip". The end tray gains m so that Pom can show how naming works. After blocking, 28 names remain instead of about 25, and Tjitse may cut more after his review.
- **6, sticker after help step 2.** The critique says both "help step 2 or later gives a plain sticker" and "no sticker after help step 3". This design gives a plain sticker after one or two wrong picks and no sticker after help step 3.
